# Project Memory: IPO Tank + Base Breakout Research System

## Project Goal

Build a historical swing-trading research system to study recent IPOs that:

1. IPO/listed relatively recently.
2. Dropped heavily from their post-IPO highs.
3. Stopped free-falling.
4. Formed a base/consolidation.
5. Broke out or failed.
6. Can be analyzed to determine which traits produced better breakout expectancy.

The core research question is:

> Among recent IPOs that tanked, based, and broke out, do the good breakouts have CAN SLIM-like traits? If not, what traits do they actually share?

This project is not day trading. It is daily/weekly swing research.

---

## Current Data Plan

Use two main data sources:

### Portfolio123

Portfolio123 is used mainly to generate the historical recent-IPO ticker universe.

Do not rely on a single “current” export because that creates survivorship bias. Instead, use Portfolio123 historical `As Of` dates to capture which stocks qualified as recent IPOs at each point in time.

The Portfolio123 API appears to have the needed endpoints:

- `POST /screen/run`
- `POST /data`

Portfolio123 Recent IPO Screen Logic

Current recent-IPO proxy rules:

Close(1260) = NA
Close(120) != NA

Meaning:

Close(1260) = NA

The stock did not have price data roughly 5 trading years ago, so it is probably a newer listing.

Close(120) != NA

The stock did have price data roughly 120 trading days ago, so it has at least around 6 months of trading history.

Together this means:

Find stocks that existed at least 120 trading days ago but did not exist 1260 trading days ago.

This is a decent IPO/listing-age proxy.

Other initial Portfolio123 screen filters can include:

Close(0) > 3 or Close(0) > 5
MktCap > 1000
AvgDailyTot(30) > 5000000
Common stocks only
U.S. exchanges only
Exclude ETFs, warrants, units, preferreds, rights, funds

However, for broader research, it may be better to export a wider universe and apply tighter filters later in Python.

Historical Snapshot Frequency

Do not export only one current list.

To capture historical IPO universe membership, run Portfolio123 screen snapshots over time.

MVP

Quarterly snapshots are acceptable for a quick test.

Example:

2023-01-01
2023-04-01
2023-07-01
2023-10-01
...
Better

Monthly snapshots are preferred.

Example:

2023-01-31
2023-02-28
2023-03-31
...

Monthly is better because an IPO can appear, break out, fail, delist, or change ticker within one year. Yearly snapshots are too coarse for swing-trading research.

The goal of Portfolio123 monthly snapshots is not to find final candidates. It is only to build the historical eligible ticker universe.

Important As-Of Date Behavior

Portfolio123 As Of works like a time machine.

Example:

As of 2026-07-01, ARM may appear because it existed and had enough trading history by then.
As of 2023-01-01, ARM would not appear because it had not IPO’d/listed yet.

This is correct behavior.

The IPO universe must change over time.

Pipeline Overview

Preferred MVP pipeline:

1. Use Portfolio123 API to run recent-IPO screen on monthly historical dates.
2. Save rows as as_of_date + ticker.
3. Combine all monthly exports.
4. Deduplicate tickers into a master ticker list.
5. Use Polygon/Massive to pull daily OHLCV for each ticker.
6. Use Polygon/Massive to pull company reference data, ticker details, and corporate actions.
7. Pull financial statement data with filing dates if available.
8. Build point-in-time features in Python.
9. Detect tank/base setups.
10. Detect breakouts.
11. Label forward outcomes.
12. Analyze which traits predict better breakout expectancy.

Main Setup: IPO Tank + Base

The base universe is recent IPOs/listings that got crushed and then consolidated.

Initial filters:

IPO/list age >= 60 trading days
IPO/list age <= 5 years
Close >= 3 or 5
Average dollar volume 30D >= 2M or 5M

Tank/drawdown filters:

Drawdown from 1Y high <= -50%
OR
Drawdown from since-IPO high <= -50%

Definitions:

drawdown_from_high_pct = (close - rolling_high) / rolling_high * 100

Consolidation/base filters:

30D change between -15% and +15%
60D change between -30% and +30%
30D range <= 25% to 35%
60D range <= 40% to 60%

Definitions:

30D range % = (30D high - 30D low) / close * 100
60D range % = (60D high - 60D low) / close * 100

Optional stabilization filters:

ADTV 7 / ADTV 30 <= 0.80
Close within +/- 10% of 21 EMA
Close within +/- 15% of 50 SMA

Do not make the base too tight initially. IPOs can form valid bases with wider ranges.

Breakout Logic

Breakout rules should come after the base detection.

Example breakout:

Close > prior 30D high
OR
Close > prior 60D high

Volume >= 1.5x ADTV 30
Close > 21 EMA
Close > 50 SMA
Position in 60D range >= 80%

Critical rule:

When checking breakout above range high, use the prior range high, excluding today’s candle.

Correct:

breakout = today_close > max(high over prior 60 trading days excluding today)

Wrong:

breakout = today_close > max(high over current 60 days including today)
Research Question

Main question:

Among IPOs that declined 50%+ and formed bases, do breakouts with stronger CAN SLIM-like traits show better 20D/60D/120D forward expectancy and lower failure rates?

Do not assume CAN SLIM is automatically correct. The goal is to test whether winners actually have CAN SLIM traits or whether other traits matter more.

CAN SLIM Trait Mapping

O’Neil’s CAN SLIM traits:

C = Current quarterly earnings/sales growth
A = Annual earnings growth and ROE
N = New products, new management, new highs
S = Supply and demand, shares outstanding, volume demand
L = Leader or laggard
I = Institutional sponsorship
M = Market direction

For this project, create testable proxies.

C: Current Quarterly Growth

Possible proxies:

latest quarter revenue growth YoY
latest quarter EPS growth YoY
sales acceleration
EPS acceleration
gross margin improvement
operating loss narrowing

For IPOs, EPS can be noisy or negative, so revenue growth and margin improvement may be more reliable than EPS.

A: Annual Growth / ROE

Possible proxies:

3Y sales growth if available
3Y EPS growth if available
ROE
operating margin
gross margin
positive or improving free cash flow

For recent IPOs, do not overweight this too much because many do not have three public years of annual earnings.

N: New / New Highs

Possible proxies:

IPO age
breakout to new 30D high
breakout to new 60D high
breakout to new 52W high
reclaiming key moving averages
new product/news not in MVP unless manually added

For tanked IPOs, “new highs” should often mean new base highs or 30D/60D highs, not necessarily 52-week highs.

S: Supply and Demand

Possible proxies:

volume dry-up during base
breakout volume expansion
relative volume on breakout
dollar volume
shares outstanding
float if available

Example:

volume_dryup_ratio = ADTV_7 / ADTV_30
breakout_volume_ratio = breakout_day_volume / ADTV_30
L: Leader or Laggard

Possible proxies:

relative strength 1M vs SPY
relative strength 3M vs SPY
relative strength 6M vs SPY
relative strength vs QQQ
relative strength vs sector ETF
relative strength improvement before breakout
price above 50 SMA
price above 200 SMA

Likely one of the most important groups.

I: Institutional Sponsorship

Possible proxies if available:

number of institutions
institutional ownership %
institutional net purchases
change in institutional ownership

This may be useful but noisy for IPOs.

M: Market Direction

Possible proxies:

SPY above 50 SMA
SPY above 200 SMA
QQQ above 50 SMA
QQQ above 200 SMA
SPY/QQQ 20D and 60D returns
market breadth if available
sector ETF trend

This is probably very important. Good breakouts can fail in hostile markets.

Expected High-Signal Traits

Before testing, the likely strongest predictors of better breakout expectancy are:

relative strength improvement before breakout
base tightness / volatility contraction
volume dry-up in base
volume expansion on breakout
reclaiming 50 SMA
market/sector risk-on regime
revenue growth or sales re-acceleration
margin improvement or loss narrowing
cash/debt survival profile
positive estimate revisions if available

Potentially weaker or less reliable for this niche:

traditional low P/E value
book value
dividends
3-year annual EPS history
institutional ownership alone

Reason: many recent IPOs are young, unprofitable, and do not have long public records.

Analysis Approach

Create groups:

Top 20% breakout outcomes
Middle 60%
Bottom 20% / failed breakouts

Then compare the features at the breakout date.

Questions to answer:

Do winners have higher sales growth?
Do winners have better RS before breakout?
Do winners show volume dry-up before breakout?
Do winners show stronger breakout volume?
Do winners have tighter bases?
Do winners have better cash/debt?
Do winners happen in better market regimes?
Do winners have improving margins or narrowing losses?
Do winners have stronger institutional accumulation?

Analyze by:

mean return
median return
win rate
average winner
average loser
payoff ratio
profit factor
failure rate
max favorable excursion
max adverse excursion

Important:

Mean can be distorted by huge winners.
Median shows what usually happened.
Right-tail behavior matters because CAN SLIM-style systems may rely on a few huge winners.
Possible CAN SLIM Score

Start simple:

C score: 20 points
A score: 10 points
N score: 20 points
S score: 15 points
L score: 20 points
I score: 5 points
M score: 10 points

For IPO tank/base breakouts, overweight:

C = current growth
N = breakout/new base high
L = relative strength
M = market regime
S = volume behavior

Underweight:

A = annual history
I = institutional sponsorship

Reason: recent IPOs often lack clean annual history and institutional data may be noisy.

But do not rely only on a composite score. Also test individual traits separately.

Avoiding Bias

Major backtesting pitfalls:

survivorship bias
look-ahead bias
ticker-change issues
using current tickers only
using current market cap/liquidity for past dates
using period_end instead of filing_date
using future highs/lows to define historical setups
using today's fundamentals for past breakouts
overfitting too many parameters
ignoring delisted names
ignoring splits/corporate actions

Correct rule:

On each scan date, only use information available up to that date.

The system should answer:

What would I have known on this exact historical date, and what trade would I have taken using only that information?

Not:

What looks obvious today after knowing the future?
