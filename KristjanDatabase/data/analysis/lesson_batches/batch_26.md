# Batch 26 — Kristjan Kullamägi Livestream Lessons (2022-01 through 2023-05, bear market period)

## Entries & Setups
- Episodic Pivot (EP): a stock gaps 8-10%+ on high-volume surprise news; the news itself doesn't need to be significant, what matters is the size of the volume/price surprise. Good EPs gap up *over* all resistance/declining moving averages; EPs that gap into overhead declining MAs are prone to failing (2022-01-14, 2022-02-04, 2022-02-08, 2023-05-18).
- There is no such thing as an EP on a chart sitting below all its declining moving averages — that's just a downtrend, not a setup (2022-04-14, 2022-02-04).
- Never trade EPs short — the odds are stacked against you on that side (2022-02-04).
- Big-volume range breakouts are powerful; buy tightness before the breakout when possible (2022-01-14).
- Some names (e.g., ZIM) are mean-reverting rather than breakout stocks — buy them on dips to a rising moving average instead of chasing new highs (2022-02-01).
- In the first leg of a rally off a bottom there are no real breakouts yet, only bounce/reversal setups off oversold lows; for bounce candidates check weekly/monthly support (50 or 20 MA) even with no daily support, then look for reversal candles (2022-02-01).
- Mean-reversion ("meaner version") setups — stocks down/up several days straight that reverse hard — are called his favorite day-trading setup, working long or short depending on how extended the move is (2022-01-14, 2022-01-21).
- Small-account EP scan: stocks up 50%+ within any 14-day window over the past ~6 months with 5-day avg dollar volume above ~$1M — used to build a study list of the biggest historical movers (2023-05-18).
- Simple EP entry mechanics: use a 5-minute chart, buy when price takes out the high of the first 5-minute candle, stop at the low of day (2023-05-18). Position size cap: stay under ~1% of a stock's average daily volume to avoid brutal slippage (2023-05-18).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Moving averages (10/20/50, daily/weekly/monthly) act as real support and resistance in both directions — "no joke," they work relentlessly on the way up and the way down (2022-01-25, 2022-02-01, 2022-02-11).
- The exact MA lengths don't matter (10 vs 15, 50 vs 60) — what matters is stacking a short-term, medium-term, and longer-term average (2022-02-08).
- Confirm a setup top-down: check the daily, then the weekly, then confirm the actual entry trigger on an intraday/tick chart — if it doesn't show there, there's no trade (2022-01-12).
- "Tennis ball action": a fundamentally strong stock held underwater by broad market weakness will snap back hard once selling pressure lifts (2022-03-01).
- Individual stocks/sectors starting to decouple (hold up or turn up) while the index is still weak or declining is an early bullish tell (2022-01-14, 2022-02-17, 2023-05-18).
- A healthy uptrending index/stock has the 10-day above the rising 20-day, both sloping up — that's "where the big money is" (2022-11-04, 2023-05-18).
- Laggards: within a strong sector, names that haven't participated yet aren't automatically bargains — approach with caution (2022-02-04).
- Stage analysis (Weinstein): don't hold through stage 4 decline — you never know how low a stage-4 stock can go, same as you never know how high a stage-2 stock can go (2022-04-28).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Never average down — "that's for losers" (2022-01-18).
- Cap max size on any single stock around 20-25% of the account, even at scale (2022-01-27).
- Half-Kelly is a reasonable ceiling if you can actually estimate your edge (2022-04-12); overall portfolio leverage capped around 1.5x (2022-04-12).
- With a small account, going all-in on rare high-conviction setups is viable since losses are quickly recoverable — that logic breaks down once an account is large (2022-04-12).
- Use wider, weekly-based stops (not daily) and correspondingly smaller position sizing on inherently choppy commodity ETFs/stocks (2022-03-01, 2022-04-12).
- Don't hold small/illiquid biotech through unknown binary data readouts unless deliberately trading it as an EP — check biotech catalyst calendars first (2022-11-04).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Sell-rule discipline is repeatedly cited as the single biggest factor separating good and bad outcomes; violating his own stop rule (MU, Roku, BOIL, GUSH) cost him some of the best trades of the year (2022-01-25, 2022-02-01, 2022-04-13, 2022-04-18).
- After a huge move (~100%+ in weeks), tighten up: switch from a daily close-below-10-day stop to a tighter intraday rule (e.g., 10 EMA on the 60-min), or simply take partial profits (half or two-thirds) and let a runner ride (2022-04-18).
- "Defend your risk" — once a stop is set, own it and don't second-guess it mid-trade (quoting bond investor Bill Gross) (2022-04-12).
- Re-entering immediately after being stopped out on a setup that's still valid should be automatic — "it's just a click of a mouse" (2022-04-06).
- On slower commodity-linked positions, trail with a longer-term average (e.g., 10-week) instead of the daily 10/20 to avoid getting chopped out by noise (2022-04-06, 2022-04-12).
- Fine to hold through earnings when sitting on a profit cushion and the stock has shown relative strength (2022-04-19).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Big winners can run 10-15x+ of initial risk — cites X, FCX/COPX, and NUGT all running well over 10x his entry risk (2022-04-12).
- The Pareto principle applies strongly: roughly 95% of a swing/position trader's profits come from about 5% of trades (vs. ~80/20 for day traders, ~60/40 for scalpers) — you only need a handful of great trades per year if sized correctly (2022-01-27).
- After a parabolic move, taking partial profits (half or two-thirds) while keeping a smaller runner is a reasonable middle ground versus an all-or-nothing exit (2022-04-18).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- The biggest opportunities come after sell-offs/washouts — the faster and deeper the decline, the bigger the eventual setup; he repeatedly says he wants sharper crashes to get to the bottom faster (2022-01-21, 2022-01-25, 2022-01-26, 2022-02-04).
- Bear markets can run for years with multi-month rally phases inside them; don't mistake a two-month decline for the whole cycle — study 2000-03 and 2007-09 for the shape (2022-01-25).
- "Easy money" windows occur even in bear-market years, usually once or twice — be ready to size up hard when one appears (2022-02-01, 2022-05-10).
- In highly correlated, choppy markets, individual stock-picking is very hard; leveraged/triple ETFs are easier since everything moves together anyway (2022-01-25, 2022-02-01).
- The word "can't" is dangerous — markets regularly do what "can't" happen (e.g., a stock up 50% on "just okay" earnings) (2022-02-04, 2022-02-11).
- A market that's roughly up 50% of days and down 50% of days doesn't need a news reason for each move — don't over-attribute daily action to headlines (2022-02-04).
- During deep/long corrections, the most beaten-down names tend to lead the first leg of the recovery; during shallow pullbacks in an uptrend, the names showing the most relative strength/resilience lead once pressure lifts (cites AMD/NVDA, Oct 2021) (2022-01-18).
- EPs and breakouts simply don't work in a bad market backdrop — "you need the market in your back" (2023-05-18). Use a simple traffic-light filter: index 10-day above rising 20-day = green light to trade long; declining = red light, sit out or downsize (2022-11-04, 2023-05-18).
- Reaction to earnings matters more than the headline beat/miss — "it's not about good or bad, it's about better or worse than feared"; the market rewarding bad news (dip-buying working again) is a bullish tell for regime change (2023-01-26).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Favor higher-ADR (average daily range) names, especially with a smaller account — low-ADR slow movers waste focus that should go to fast movers (2022-01-14, 2022-02-08, 2022-04-06).
- Prefer sector ETFs over individual commodity/energy names to avoid single-company risk when the thesis is sector-wide (2022-01-14, 2022-02-01, 2022-02-03).
- Avoid crowded, hyped names — "never a good sign when everyone and their mom is watching the same stock"; prefer having a broad buffet of setups over one popular idea (2022-02-11).
- One broad, well-built scan across the whole market beats multiple narrow sector scans; more than 3-4 scans (or piles of indicators) signals over-complication (2022-02-01, 2022-04-19).
- Small accounts should specifically hunt the thin micro/small caps that make the biggest percentage moves — "small account edge" — since larger accounts can't size into them (2022-01-18, 2022-04-12, 2023-01-26, 2023-05-18).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Extreme-ownership framing: every loss is your own fault, not the market's — a mindset he credits as key to improving (2022-04-06).
- Struggling traders usually lack a real, well-studied edge, not "trading psychology" issues — fix it by mastering one or two specific setups over hundreds of hours rather than doing self-help work (2022-04-06).
- Compulsive over-clicking/trading for "dopamine" is a recognized personal weakness managed rather than eliminated (2022-02-04); boredom/addiction to activity drives forced trades in dead markets — best countered with outside hobbies (2022-01-21, 2022-01-26, 2022-01-27).
- It's fine, even correct, to do nothing when there's no edge — "no one is forcing you to make a bet" (2022-01-27).
- Deliberately increasing holding period and cutting trade frequency in favor of fewer, higher-conviction trades was an explicit self-improvement goal (2022-01-18, 2022-04-12).
- Ignore macro forecasts, news, and other traders' opinions; listen only to price action — being permanently bearish "because the world is ending" has cost people money for years (2022-01-18, 2022-02-08).
- Expect strings of losses as part of the game; a few outsized winners (10-20x risk) more than offset many small losses (2022-04-12).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Covering short positions on a "feeling" without a rule-based reason is called out repeatedly as his worst mistake of the year (PLTR/ARKK shorts) (2022-01-10, 2022-01-14, 2022-01-21, 2022-01-25, 2022-04-06).
- Anticipating breakouts before confirmation is generally discouraged, even though he occasionally breaks this rule on very strong names (2022-01-14).
- Overthinking and ignoring your own moving-average sell rule ("thinking you're smarter than the 10/20-day MA") repeatedly gives back the best trades of the year (2022-04-13, 2022-04-18).
- "Diamond hands" holding a stock into a 90%+ drawdown with no stop is the classic retail mistake sell rules exist to prevent; bag-holders are simply "people who don't use stops" (2022-02-01, 2022-05-10).
- Buying a "random" candle with no real trend or pattern behind it is low-odds — randomness "will get you killed" (2022-02-08). Anchoring to 1-minute-chart noise instead of the daily/weekly picture is a common beginner error (2022-02-08).

## Other Notable Lessons
- The recommended way to learn setups: go through every U.S. stock that has ever made a big move, and study the pattern, catalyst, and market conditions behind each one — no certification or designation needed (2022-01-18).
- Trade U.S. markets in USD rather than a weak home currency, even if it means eating a higher tax rate — a weak local currency is itself a hidden risk (2022-02-01, 2022-02-17, 2023-01-26).
- Keep a backup charting/trading platform so a data outage doesn't leave you unable to manage open positions (2022-02-03).
