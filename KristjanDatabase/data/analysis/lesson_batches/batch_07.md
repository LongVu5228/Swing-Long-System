## Entries & Setups
- Core intraday method: let an opening range (1/5/10/15/60-min, flexible) form first, then enter only on a confirmed break of that range with the opposite side as the stop; never pre-guess a breakout level. (2020-04-16, 2020-05-04, "Big time divergence..."/"Can the indices hold...")
- Rarely shorts "day one" news/earnings movers — waits for a stock to build a range over several days first; first-day shorting has burned him repeatedly over the years. (2020-04-17, 2020-05-05, "Sell the news..."/"Many new long and short setups...")
- After a severe multi-day crash, watches for a reclaim of the declining 20-EMA (60-min) as a bounce-momentum trigger, risking a small amount to potentially make 10-20x that (used on CHK, Tilray). (2020-04-23, 2020-04-24, "A market of bull and bear flags"/"30-50% moves coming")
- Never holds a position through an earnings report, long or short, regardless of how good the setup looks — calls that "gambling," not trading. (2020-05-05, "Many new long and short setups")
- Distressed/bankruptcy-risk names with company-specific backing (e.g., CCL's Saudi stake and financing) are preferred within a weak group over peers without it, since it lowers near-term blowup risk. (2020-04-17, 2020-04-20)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Constantly compares relative strength/weakness between correlated instruments (SPXL vs TQQQ, a stock vs its index) each morning to decide which one to trade that day. (recurring, most videos)
- Treats the declining 50-day (and rising 10/20-day) as the key rejection/support zones on both daily and 60-min charts. (2020-04-15 through 2020-05-08, recurring)
- A bear flag that fails to break down and instead bounces off a rising moving average is "morphing into a bull flag" — treats that as a signal to flip bias from short to long. (2020-04-27, "Beaten down names 50-100% upside")
- Rule of thumb: never short relative strength — if a stock holds up while its group/index is falling, it's not a short candidate. (2020-04-20, "Bear market rally or 1st leg...")
- Divergence between a resilient index and weakening underlying stocks (or vice versa) is read as an early sign "something has to give." (2020-04-16, "Big time divergence")
- Considers low-volume rallies and higher-volume selloffs structurally normal/bullish behavior for an uptrend, not a red flag. (2020-04-27)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Default full-position sizing heuristic: roughly 1% of a stock's average daily volume. (2020-04-20)
- Builds starters at quarter/half size on speculative or lower-conviction setups, only sizing up on confirmation. (recurring)
- Actively tracks total dollar long vs. short exposure through the day and trims to reduce net exposure before the close rather than holding it all overnight. (2020-05-06, 2020-05-08)
- Will cut a position simply because the dollar risk on the stop is too large for a non-core setup, even if the chart still looks fine. (2020-04-16)
- Margin/leverage should be built up gradually over weeks or months as results improve — never go from flat to fully margined in a day or even a week. (2020-04-27)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard scale-out method on winners: sell small fixed increments (e.g., 5% of the position every 50 cents, or a fixed share clip every few points) rather than exiting all at once. (2020-04-27, 2020-05-05, 2020-05-08)
- Once a trade is working, moves the stop to breakeven ("free trade") and then trails it using the rising 10-day or 20-day moving average. (2020-04-20, 2020-05-08)
- Default stop reference is the low of the day (or the prior red candle) — states plainly he needs a stop somewhere and that's his fallback. (2020-04-17)
- Only adds to a short on confirmed weakness (a broken range/opening low), never pre-emptively into strength.

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- On stocks up huge (CHK, Tilray, W), keeps trimming small amounts all the way up instead of picking one exit target, to bank gains while still participating in further upside. (2020-04-27, 2020-04-30)
- Won't carry a swing short overnight unless the stock closes genuinely weak that day — a tight range that never breaks down intraday gets covered rather than held. (2020-04-22)
- Uses profit-taking triggers pegged to key levels (declining 50-day, round numbers) as scale-out points rather than arbitrary percentages. (2020-04-20, 2020-04-27)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Flags "peak micro-cap euphoria" (a flood of sub-dollar/penny stocks doubling-tripling in a day) as a recurring precursor to short-term market tops, historically happening about 2-3 times per year. (2020-04-17)
- Treats an unbroken vertical rally with no pullback as inherently suspicious — believes healthy uptrends need periodic multi-week digestion, and defaults to calling a bounce off oversold lows a "bear market rally" until proven otherwise by a base that holds. (2020-04-15, 2020-04-16)
- Watches for "tale of two markets" behavior — growth/momentum stocks rallying while reopening/value/cyclical names break down (or vice versa) — as a rotation signal worth acting on. (2020-04-16)
- In a market where fundamentals have stopped mattering (heavy Fed/stimulus backdrop), says price action is king and earnings misses/beats can produce opposite-of-expected reactions. (2020-05-05, 2020-05-07)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Builds watchlists from simple scans: biggest gainers over 5-day, 3/6/12/18/24-month lookbacks, filtered for price-growth rank, a minimum daily dollar volume (~$45M+), and adequate average daily range (~2.2+) so the stock isn't too slow to trade. (2020-05-08)
- Avoids illiquid/sub-dollar names for real size, though will occasionally take a token "for fun" position in an extreme-revenue-growth microcap he can't scale into. (2020-05-06)
- Uses triple-leveraged index ETFs (SPXL, TQQQ) to express a market-wide view without taking on single-stock/news risk when individual setups aren't clean. (2020-04-15)

## Psychology & Mindset
- Names the core tension of momentum trading directly: chasing repeatedly can blow up an account, yet in a strongly trending market chasing is often what makes the most money — he manages this rather than resolving it. (2020-04-20)
- Notes that a disciplined stop will frequently look wrong in hindsight (the stock reverses right after being stopped out) but the process still protects against the rare catastrophic loss. (2020-04-20)
- Describes results as cyclical — stretches where "everything turns to gold" tend to precede give-back, and treats his own urge to screenshot a big P&L as a personal warning sign of a local peak. (2020-04-30)
- Says being too cautious during a strong rally cost him a large amount of missed profit over several weeks, even though that same caution is what keeps him in business long-term. (2020-04-17)
- Considers day trading mentally easier to stay disciplined at than swing/position trading, because day traders get frequent feedback and should rarely have a losing month, while swing traders must tolerate long stretches of small losses before a big winner pays for them. (2020-05-06)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Never buy breakouts in a choppy, high-volatility market — cites same-day failed breakouts as the reason. (2020-04-15)
- Never hold a position through earnings purely out of habit or because the setup looks great — explicitly separates this from disciplined trading. (2020-05-05)
- Flags his own recurring error of not sizing up to full size (the ~1% ADV heuristic) on his cleanest, most "textbook" setups. (2020-04-20)
- Avoids CFDs entirely, calling the embedded leverage "poison," and argues a broker should never make a popular ETF hard-to-borrow. (2020-04-15)

## Other Notable Lessons
- In distressed-stock rallies, repeatedly stresses that fundamentally worthless, heavily-shorted companies can produce the largest percentage moves purely from short covering and momentum, fully disconnected from fundamentals — "price action is always king" in these stretches. (2020-04-24, 2020-04-27, 2020-05-05)
