## Entries & Setups

- Buys the first breakout candle: enters on a break of the opening-range high or the prior candle's high, using that candle's low as the stop (2017-03-07, "Feb 2017 Tracking Watchlists Cleaning Pt 1").
- Bull-flag breakouts are his single best-performing setup across his whole career and work on any timeframe — daily/weekly swing flags or intraday flags follow the same logic (2019-10-01, "My FIRST trading stream").
- Five-star setups share traits: a narrow, tight-range candle the day before the breakout, high-tight-flag shape, and volume expansion on the breakout day (2017-03-07).
- One of his favorite short setups: a stock up 4-5+ days in a row is "due" for a red day; he watches for the reversal on day five or six (2017-03-07; 2017-04-02, "Feb 2017 Tracking Watchlists Pt 2").
- On strong-news earnings gappers, buys the high of the first 1-minute candle, then the high of the first 5-minute candle if it holds, rather than the open itself (2017-08-08, 2017-08-09).
- Technical analysis and momentum patterns work unusually well on recent IPOs since they have no trading history creating overhead resistance (2017-03-13, "My scanning process").
- Bottom breakouts from multi-year, deeply beaten-down bases are a recurring favorite theme, especially during regime shifts when leadership rotates into laggards (2019-10-01).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- Treats rising moving averages (10/20/50/100/150-day) as dynamic support and looks to buy pullbacks/bounces off them; a stock that never even touches its rising 20-day before ripping higher is a sign of real underlying strength (2017-03-22, "$101K profits... Review of mistakes").
- Judges quality of a breakout by "follow-through" duration — wants moves lasting at least 3-5 days, ideally weeks; a single-day pop (even a large one) is dismissed as a "one-day wonder" (2017-04-02).
- A breakout that falls back into its prior range is a bad sign for "a breakout guy," and happens more often in choppy/weak markets (2017-03-09).
- Screens using ADR% (average daily range, ex-gaps) to quantify a stock's inherent volatility/tradeable range, and a proprietary trend-intensity indicator (fast MA % above slow MA) to find stocks already trending hard (2017-03-13).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Frames entries with explicit dollar risk-to-reward, e.g. risking 10-40 cents to make several dollars, and only takes trades with a clearly favorable ratio (2017-03-07; 2019-10-01).
- Deliberately caps size on illiquid names rather than end up with 20 tiny scattered positions — prefers concentrating into fewer, more liquid names (2017-03-10, "March 2017 tracking watchlist Pt 1").
- Core rule from a costly squeeze: never short a low-float micro-cap unless it has already run up hundreds/thousands of percent, and never average into a losing short in these names (2019-03-10, "How to trade... insanity stocks").
- Lost roughly $700K (~20% of equity) staying on the wrong side of one stock for weeks, but recovered it within three weeks — framed as evidence that a loss capped to a percentage of equity is survivable if sizing discipline holds (2019-03-10).
- Applies a hard liquidity floor (~$15-20M average daily dollar volume) before a stock even enters his tradeable universe (2019-10-01).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- Recurring self-diagnosed pattern: stopped out right at support just before the stock reverses and runs — leads him to conclude some setups (esp. earnings positions) need a wider stop like breakeven rather than a tight technical one (2017-03-22).
- On a name he was highly convinced would keep running, he ignored his own technical warning signs (VWAP breaks) and gave back three-quarters of an open profit — lesson to honor exits even under strong bias (2017-08-09).
- Uses staggered/partial stops — sells half at one level, the remainder at a second level — instead of one all-or-nothing exit (2018-08-17, "I'm back doing videos").
- When shorting a violent micro-cap squeeze, waits for a defined trigger (first red 1-minute candle breaking the prior candle's low) instead of shorting randomly and adding into a loser; this discipline turned a $52K loss into a $267K profit in a single session (2019-03-10).
- During extreme, halt-driven moves there is often no safe way to add to a position — each candle can represent only seconds of trading, so the only viable action is to cover, not scale in (2019-03-10).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy

- Habitually sells partial size into strength on winners rather than holding the full position for one target — done on nearly every position across the batch.
- Uses trailing stops on runners that are deep in profit instead of fixed price targets, to let winners keep extending (2017-08-08).
- States he "always" wants to book large profits once he has them, even when the setup still looks constructive, rather than risk giving the gain back (2018-08-29, "August 29 - Weed stocks").

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- Explicitly ties trading approach to broad-market regime: during a choppy, sideways index (Russell 2000 flat Sept-Dec 2018), breakouts fail more often, so he cuts exposure and raises cash (2019-03-10).
- Uses historical base-rate reasoning: 20%+ index declines occur roughly every 3-4 years and have historically been buying opportunities; 30-50% declines are rare (2019-03-10).
- Rebuilds long exposure as breakouts start holding and tight setups resolve upward; cuts long exposure by as much as 50% and adds shorts once breakouts start failing again — explicitly "follows price" rather than predicting (2019-03-10).
- Adapts his whole watchlist to whatever regime is working — shifted from buying strength/high flyers to buying beaten-down flag setups once market leadership rotated in 2019 (2019-10-01).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- Runs a battery of scans: min. dollar volume (~$1.5M+), min. ADR% (~2.2), biggest 1-month/1-year gainers by relative strength, biggest 1-2 year losers (mean-reversion shorts-turned-longs), and a Friday-gainers scan that he calls his best source of short ideas (2017-03-13).
- Reviews every notable breakout/breakdown daily in a "tracking watchlist," regardless of outcome, purely to build pattern recognition for what gets follow-through (2017-03-07, 2017-04-02, 2019-10-01).
- Passes on roughly 95% of earnings gap-ups; only buys earnings movers with strong beats and real growth, and prefers the first or second earnings pop in a name over the third/fourth (2017-04-02).
- Generally distrusts analyst-upgrade gap-ups — most fail as one-day wonders unless from a fund-respected analyst with new information (2017-04-02).
- Avoids holding into binary biotech catalysts (FDA data/PDUFA dates) since the timing is a gamble (2017-03-13).

## Psychology & Mindset

- Reviews trades monthly specifically to catalog mistakes, estimating he could make 50-100% more in a given month by eliminating his top five recurring errors (2017-03-22).
- Admits a personal mental block against buying extremely hyped, high-valuation momentum names (e.g., cannabis stocks at ~200x sales) even when the chart is textbook, and that it costs him money (2018-08-29).
- Finds it psychologically very hard to flip from a large realized short loss straight into going long the same stock, even recognizing the long setup — describes it as a costly mental block (2019-03-10).
- After a heavy loss while mentally drained, deliberately stepped away from the screen rather than keep trading (2018-08-29).
- On his career's biggest loss (~$700K from stubbornly holding one stock the wrong way), stresses the real lesson was as much psychological — letting go of a losing thesis — as technical (2019-03-10).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- "Getting stubborn" — refusing to cut a loss quickly and clinging to a fixed thesis — is his most frequently cited recurring error (2017-03-10, 2019-03-10).
- Warns strongly against ever assuming a heavily-shorted or bankrupt-looking stock "must" reverse because it's up "too much" — these can run thousands of percent regardless of fundamentals (2019-03-10).
- Avoids shorting explosive gappers on day one, preferring day two or three, since day-one shorts on runners are typically low-probability (2019-10-01).
- Being away from the desk at a critical premarket moment cost him a five-figure trade (2017-03-22); a silently auto-cancelled stop order also cost money — a reminder to verify working orders (2017-08-22).
- Only partially "boxing" (fully locating) a short position left him exposed to a squeeze on the unhedged remainder (2017-03-22).

## Other Notable Lessons

- Explains his shift from day trading to swing/position trading as his account grew past ~$1-2M — day-trading strategies don't scale with size, forcing a move to holding periods of days-to-months (2019-10-01).
- With correlated-catalyst groups (e.g., several stocks hit by the same news), compares relative strength between names to decide which is the better long or short (2019-10-01).
- Trades Bitcoin-proxy instruments (e.g., GBTC) on relative strength/weakness versus the underlying asset rather than the proxy's own chart alone (2017-08-08, 2017-08-22).
