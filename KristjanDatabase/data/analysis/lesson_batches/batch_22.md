# Batch 22 — Kristjan Kullamägi Trading Lessons (2021-04-13 to 2021-05-20)

## Entries & Setups
- EP (episodic pivot) criteria: gap of roughly 10%+ (less for slower mega-caps, more for fast movers — it's relative to the stock's normal ADR, not a fixed number), unusually large volume right out of the gate, and a real news catalyst (earnings/guidance surprise ideally). A gap into a still-declining stock with no range broken is not an EP, no matter how big the gap. (2021-04-13 "New project!"; 2021-04-19; 2021-05-10 "ETF insight")
- Preferred entry is buying an actual breakout of a tight range, ideally near a moving average (10/20/50 day) — not "chasing" days after the move already started, and not anticipating a breakout before it triggers. Anticipated entries have a much higher fail rate. (2021-04-13; 2021-04-16 "Patience"; 2021-05-04)
- Best breakouts happen off tight, orderly, linear price action; wide "barcode"-looking candles with no order should be skipped entirely — "if the setup looks like a barcode, don't trade it." (2021-04-20 "zzz"; 2021-05-04)
- For micro-cap/low-institutional-support stocks, only trust bounces off the rising 10- or 20-day; the 50-day works for mid/large caps with real institutional sponsorship but is often "dead" for micro caps. (2021-04-14; 2021-04-27 "Slowest bull run")
- Earnings breakouts (stock gapping/breaking a multi-month range on an earnings surprise) are considered the most powerful type of breakout. (2021-05-10)
- The "high tide flag" — a big, fast move followed by a short, tight pullback that surfs a fast moving average (often the 10-day) — is rated his best swing setup after the EP; the mean-reversion parabolic short is rated the single best day-trading setup. (2021-05-10 "ETF insight")
- Commodity/cyclical names (steel, oil, copper, lithium) behave differently from growth stocks — they're better bought on a pullback/undercut-and-reclaim of a rising moving average than chased on a straight breakout, which tends to fail for these names. (2021-05-07 "Seeing some big opportunities"; 2021-05-10)
- A pullback's percentage depth doesn't matter to him — what matters is where the stock finds support (which moving average) and how it behaves there. (2021-04-20 "zzz")

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Core regime filter: the 10-day and 20-day moving averages (watched on Nasdaq or Russell) should be rising, with the 10-day above the 20-day, before buying breakouts; if either is declining or the 10-day is below the 20-day, don't buy breakouts — "it will keep you in most of the good periods and out of most of the bad ones." (2021-04-14; 2021-04-30 "Slow but big bases"; 2021-05-04)
- "Resistance" as a concept is reserved for downtrending stocks (where a declining moving average rejects price); an uptrending stock testing a prior high isn't "resistance," it's just a range. (2021-04-30)
- Tightness = edge; randomness = no edge. He constantly screens out stocks that are "all over the place" even if the general shape looks like a flag — real setups are linear/orderly with clean support at moving averages, not choppy. (2021-04-14; 2021-04-19 "Henry Cavill"; 2021-05-20)
- Violent, sharp sell-offs make better bounce/mean-reversion candidates than slow "orderly" declines — the violence itself is what creates the statistical edge. (2021-05-18 "Bottoming action")
- "Tennis ball action" (Minervini's term) — a stock that gets pushed down and immediately pops back — signals real relative strength. (2021-05-20)
- Watch relative strength between a stock and its sector/underlying asset (e.g., look at silver before HEQ, Russell before TNA, bitcoin before GBTC) rather than trading the derivative blind. (2021-05-07)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Never trade stocks below roughly 2.5–4 ADR (average daily range) if the account is under a few million — slow stocks waste time and capital regardless of account size. (2021-04-19 "Henry Cavill"; 2021-04-27)
- Keep position size to no more than ~1-2% of a stock's average daily volume to avoid becoming "too big a fish in too small a pond" (cites LTCM's blowup as the cautionary extreme). (2021-05-20)
- Anything "underperforming" or that makes you nervous while on margin needs to be cut — don't hold shaky positions just because they haven't hit a hard stop yet. (2021-04-14)
- Excessive leverage is the common thread in almost every historical trading blowup; a little leverage with well-defined exits is fine, margin without a real exit plan is how accounts (and crypto traders on 100x+ leverage) get liquidated. (2021-05-20 "Indices strong")
- Position sizing should scale with conviction and setup quality — small "starter" positions on lower-conviction ideas, up to a third or half the portfolio on the rare "10-million-dollar" conviction setups (index/sector ETFs like TNA or GBTC breaking major bases). (2021-05-07; 2021-05-10)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard sell rule: trim a third to half of a position after 3–5 days, then trail the remainder with the 10-day moving average; smaller accounts should sell more aggressively (half after 3 days), larger/more established accounts can sell less (a third after 5 days). (2021-04-16 "Patience")
- Give small "starter" positions more room (e.g., a wider stop or a slower moving average like the 50-day) since the dollar risk is trivial; scale in as a base develops rather than requiring full size on day one. (2021-04-15 "Patience and Deep Fundamental Analysis"; 2021-05-07)
- He now uses manual price alerts instead of hard market stops on some names specifically to reduce slippage in slow markets, planning to exit by hand near the alert level. (2021-05-10; 2021-05-11)
- If you get stopped out of a genuinely good setup and the stock later re-sets up (builds another higher low), you should be willing to re-buy it — repeatedly declining to re-enter a stock that keeps proving itself is a recurring, costly mistake he flags in himself (FCX, U.S. Steel, SQM all cited). (2021-05-11 "S&P500 index looks super vulnerable")

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- If a breakout closes weak on the breakout day itself, it's often better to exit immediately rather than mechanically wait for the stop — sometimes discretion beats the rule. (2021-04-14)
- Selling into strength/breaking your own sell rules "for no reason" and watching the stock double afterward is a repeating, painful pattern — the lesson is to trust the pre-set rules rather than override them emotionally. (2021-05-06 "My portfolio looks like...")

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- The best time to buy breakouts is right as the market emerges from a multi-month sideways range or correction — not after the market has already run straight up for months (odds on breakouts degrade the longer/older the uptrend is). (2021-04-16; 2021-04-27)
- A mediocre setup in a strong market environment will outperform a great setup in a weak/choppy market — market condition matters more than setup quality. (2021-04-20 "zzz")
- A typical year has only 2–3 genuinely tradable multi-month uptrends; the rest of the time is chop where "your job is not to trade, it's to wait." Being able to sit on your hands during dead stretches is a core skill. (2021-04-14; 2021-04-20)
- Range-bound/choppy markets are the hardest to trade (harder than clean uptrends or even clean downtrends, which at least require no action); they eventually resolve strongly in one direction, so patience through the range pays off. (2021-04-27; 2021-05-19 "Higher lows or start of a bigger correction")
- When only one or two stocks look good in an otherwise weak tape, that's a bad sign, not a bullish one — you want breadth (many things setting up) before getting aggressive. (2021-05-17 "Deep fundamental research on Nat Gas"; 2021-05-19)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Build a historical watch-list by screening for stocks that made big moves on high dollar volume (~$500M+/month) over many years, then studying the chart pattern before each big move to train pattern recognition — a process meant to take hundreds of hours over a year, a few names a day. (2021-04-19 "Henry Cavill")
- Want to see the whole sector/industry moving together (oil names, steel names, etc. all setting up), not an isolated random stock — sector confirmation reduces randomness risk. (2021-05-10 "ETF insight")
- Liquid ETFs (even ones with modest daily share volume, since market makers create/redeem shares) can absorb enormous size with minimal slippage, sometimes more scalable than individual stocks — he shifted much of his trading toward ETFs (TNA, GBTC, HEQ, EDC, BOIL, SMH) for this reason during this period. (2021-05-10; 2021-05-17; 2021-05-18)
- Avoid trading volatility instruments (UVXY/VXX) or other exotic instruments unless you specifically know what you're doing — they can shred accounts. (2021-04-13; 2021-04-16)

## Psychology & Mindset
- "Get the randomness out of your trading" is a recurring mantra — most losing trades trace back to buying setups that aren't statistically distinct from noise. (throughout, e.g. 2021-04-14; 2021-05-20)
- Don't lower your standards out of boredom in a slow market ("don't go down in quality") — it's tempting to rationalize mediocre setups as good ones when nothing else is happening. (2021-04-27 "Slowest bull run")
- Paper trading is considered useless: if you don't understand how stocks actually move, trading with fake money doesn't teach you anything — the fix is studying historical price patterns, not "reps." (2021-04-14)
- Preserving mental capital during dead markets (staying healthy, not forcing trades) so you're fresh and aggressive when a real opportunity arrives is treated as a competitive edge in itself. (2021-05-04 "Very cautious here")
- It typically takes 12–18+ months of dedicated study before a trader can expect to find their edge — there's no single "aha" moment. (2021-04-14)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Anticipating a breakout before it actually triggers (buying inside a range hoping it breaks) has a much higher failure rate than waiting for confirmation. (2021-04-13; 2021-04-16)
- Chasing stocks that are already extended well past a clean entry point, or buying "the only stock that looks good" in an otherwise weak tape. (2021-04-19; 2021-05-19)
- Trading low-ADR ("boomer") stocks just because they showed up in a scan wastes both time and capital regardless of account size. (2021-04-19; 2021-04-27)
- Overtrading in choppy/uncertain conditions — struggling traders reviewed on-stream routinely made 4-6 trades a day of low-quality setups; his standard advice was "no more than one trade per day" until quality improves. (2021-04-19)
- Trading unfamiliar or thin/illiquid vehicles (parabolic shorts on non-parabolic stocks, volatility ETPs, weather-driven commodity ETFs) without deep understanding of that instrument's behavior. (2021-04-13; 2021-05-17; 2021-05-20)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Almost none of his methodology is original — he describes deliberately "copying" concepts like opening-range breakouts, ADR, the EP setup, and the parabolic short from other traders/books rather than inventing anything himself, framing imitation of what already works as more efficient than reinventing it. (2021-05-11 "S&P500 index looks super vulnerable")
- Commodities/cyclicals historically lead entire market cycles for years at a time (e.g., 2003–2007, or the 1970s), then go dormant for a decade — being aware of which asset class is "in favor" matters for where opportunity concentrates. (2021-05-10; 2021-05-20)
- Micro/small-cap earnings season opportunities exist almost independent of the broader market's health, because it doesn't take much buying pressure to move a thinly traded stock on a genuine surprise. (2021-04-29 "Whack a mole"; 2021-05-17)
