# Batch 18 — Kristjan Kullamägi Livestream Lessons (2021-01-08 to 2021-01-27)

## Entries & Setups
- He only trades three core setups: breakouts, episodic pivots (EPs), and parabolic shorts — everything else is a "variation" of one of these (2021-01-08, "Many stocks are starting to speed up!"; 2021-01-21, "Bitcoin nearing the 50-day! Major support?").
- A "high tide flag" is his term for a stock that goes straight up, consolidates sideways for days/weeks while surfing the rising 10- and 20-day moving averages, then breaks the range for another leg higher — his single most-repeated pattern this batch (multiple dates, e.g. 2021-01-08, 2021-01-15, 2021-01-25 "Did I trade $100M worth of stocks today? YES!").
- A "VCP" (volatility contraction pattern) is just tightening ranges/higher lows into a breakout — he avoids jargon and calls it "higher lows" (2021-01-21).
- Episodic pivots (earnings/news gap-ups) work best on stocks that gap into a pre-existing multi-month base with big volume and strong forward guidance; mega-cap EPs are messier and rarely stay above VWAP cleanly on day one (2021-01-20 "Setups starting to emerge again!," 2021-01-21).
- Bounces off major daily moving averages (20, 50, 100, 150, 200-day) are a distinct, tradeable setup separate from his main breakout style — good for lower-risk, patient position trades (2021-01-12 "EV Gold Rush!").
- Pocket pivots: a breakout that occurs inside an existing base/range rather than off a clean multi-week consolidation (2021-01-20, 2021-01-21).
- Best bases for mid/large caps run about one month to four-five months; anything longer usually needs a catalyst (EP) to break out. Micro-caps can pop from much shorter bases (2021-01-19 "Crypto strengthening, everything else is meh").
- For breakout setups there must be a pre-existing trend — a stock that's been flat for a year has no trend to break out of, so it doesn't qualify (2021-01-21).
- Parabolic short rules: the stock must actually be parabolic (extended, multi-leg, straight-up move); never short day one — always wait for the move to stall (a lower high, a VWAP fail, a broken intraday range) before entering (2021-01-21, 2021-01-26 "You can be a victim of the markets or CHOOSE to be in control").
- For micro/nano-cap parabolic candidates, percentage moves must be judged in context — a 120% move on a micro-cap is trivial (proportionally like a 5% move on a large cap) and not yet short-worthy; wait for genuine outlier moves (300%+) (2021-01-21).
- Building a watch list of the ~30-50 fastest EPS/revenue growers in the market and only trading setups within that list is, by his account, enough of an edge to outperform the market most years without any scans (2021-01-20).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- VWAP (he calls it "viva"/"weave up") is his key intraday reference for both entries and exits — failing/reclaiming VWAP repeatedly used as a trigger (recurring across nearly every session).
- Standard trailing/exit tools: 10-day and 20-day moving averages, with faster/higher-ADR stocks generally trailed on the 10-day and slower ones on the 20-day — though he admits it's "tricky" and he mis-picks it often (2021-01-26).
- He explicitly rejects fixed-percentage stops (e.g., "3% stop on everything") because ADR varies hugely by stock; stops should be roughly a third to two-thirds of the average true/daily range (2021-01-13 "Throw a dart and you will make money in this market," 2021-01-26).
- Candlestick patterns alone are "totally useless" without broader chart context (2021-01-19).
- He rarely anticipates breakouts with full size — buying confirmed breakouts compounds capital faster than anticipating, since anticipated setups may never trigger (2021-01-21).
- On the intraday level he watches 60-minute and 5-minute charts primarily, occasionally the 1-minute for very fast/parabolic names, but dislikes 1-minute charts because they generate too many false signals (2021-01-25 "Did I trade $100M worth of stocks today? YES!").
- "Volume Buzz" style projected-volume indicators are flawed because they extrapolate from the first 5-10 minutes of trading, which is usually the highest-volume period of the day — he ignores it (2021-01-20).
- Weekly charts can reveal a clean setup that looks choppy on the daily (e.g., DQ) — always check multiple timeframes before dismissing a stock (2021-01-08, 2021-01-25).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Normal per-trade risk is about 0.25-0.5% of account equity; on a very high-conviction (five-star) setup he'll size up to 1-2%, though he says he's become more conservative about doing this as his account has grown (2021-01-13).
- Keep stops proportional to volatility: roughly one-third to two-thirds of a stock's average daily/true range; using a full 100% of ADR as a stop is reserved only for his highest-conviction setups (2021-01-26).
- Small accounts (<$50k) should run concentrated — 20-25% position sizes and no more than five or six positions total; there's no benefit to over-diversifying a small account (2021-01-21).
- He treats ~30 open positions as a personal warning indicator: historically, every time his portfolio hit 30 positions the broader market pulled back within days — not proof of causation, but he uses it as a signal to start trimming/hedging (2021-01-08, 2021-01-13, 2021-01-14 "Heavily long on margin and everything going up up up").
- Using margin is only appropriate for traders who fully understand and respect its risks; those who use it "because it's there and convenient" are exactly the people who shouldn't (2021-01-11 "Healthy crypto pullback...").
- Fundamental/long-term shorting is dangerous specifically because a small allocation (even 1-3% of a portfolio) can blow up an entire year's performance if the position squeezes thousands of percent, as happened to funds short GME (2021-01-26, 2021-01-27 "When is Gamestop going to stop?").
- On genuine short squeezes he trades tiny "feeler" size and explicitly avoids being one of the "95-99% of traders" who shouldn't be shorting these names at all (2021-01-25).
- Running multiple brokers lets you "box" a position (offset a stuck short with a long in another account) if one broker's systems fail mid-trade — a real risk-management tool, not just a backup (2021-01-27).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- His stated system: sell a third-to-half of a position around day three-to-five of a move and let the rest ride with a trailing stop — but he candidly admits he breaks his own sell rules constantly and it has cost him large amounts of profit over the past year (2021-01-08, 2021-01-11, 2021-01-14, 2021-01-27).
- On shorts, always scale into strength/weakness with separate, progressively tighter stops for each add — never treat a scaled short as one blended position (2021-01-26).
- When adding to a short during a parabolic move, tighten the stop on each incremental add rather than just widening overall risk (2021-01-26).
- He often keeps different stop levels for different lots of the same position (initial entry vs. add) rather than a single stop for the whole position (2021-01-11).
- On shorts you always have a defined maximum target (the stock can't go below zero, and realistically parabolic shorts rarely give more than 40-60% before mean-reverting to the 10/20-day), so cover partial size once ~20-25% of that expected move is captured, to withstand the shakes along the way (2021-01-26).
- "You cannot outsmart the 10- and 20-day moving averages" — repeated lesson after repeatedly selling early into strength and watching stocks run another 50-150% (2021-01-11, 2021-01-14).
- If stopped out during pre-market/after-hours on real volume, get out — don't wait for regular hours; but only exit on illiquid after-hours prints if there's genuine volume backing the move (2021-01-08).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- His self-identified single biggest weakness in this market environment is not following his own sell rules on stocks that go straight up — not any entry-related issue (2021-01-14).
- He repeatedly cites examples (Growth Generation, Celsius, Billy/BILI, DQ) where selling into apparent extension cost 30-150% of additional upside — reinforcing the "let the moving average stop you out" discipline (2021-01-08, 2021-01-11, 2021-01-14, 2021-01-25, 2021-01-27).
- Small, already-trimmed-down positions often get closed out completely just to reduce mental clutter, even when the trend/stop hasn't technically been violated (2021-01-11, 2021-01-19).
- When a five-star setup or trade doesn't work, he reframes losses in the context of total account size — a $900K loss on one trade was immaterial next to a $1.5M gain on another the same weekend (2021-01-25).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- When too many long setups are firing and the portfolio balloons past ~30 positions, or "everything is going up," he treats that as a signal to start trimming exposure and preparing to flip net short (2021-01-08, 2021-01-13).
- A widening gap between how "hot" the indices look and how few genuinely good individual setups he can find (narrowing market breadth) is treated as an early warning sign, historically preceding pullbacks (2021-01-20, 2021-01-27 "When is Gamestop going to stop?").
- Previous market leaders/high-beta winners are typically the first and hardest hit in any pullback — the same stocks that rallied most on the way up fall the most on the way down (2021-01-27).
- A shift from fundamentally-driven "growth" leadership to pure "meme"/short-squeeze-driven leadership (as in the GME/AMC period) is itself a regime signal — he treats it as a sign the broader rally may be entering its later, more dangerous stage, without necessarily calling a top (2021-01-27).
- In genuine bear markets, "there is no such thing as a breakout" — nearly everything is correlated to the index, momentum/EP strategies stop working, and the correct response for a swing trader is to trade far less, not to force setups (2021-01-15 "stonks olny gö up," 2021-01-20).
- Shorting is structurally harder in a bear market than a bull market because individual-stock idiosyncrasies get swamped by broad index correlation (2021-01-15).
- A pullback after a long, multi-month rally is healthy, not something to fear — the best buying opportunities historically come right after a base-building pullback, not while everything is already extended (2021-01-19, 2021-01-21).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Core liquidity/quality filter: prefers stocks with dollar volume of at least a few hundred million, ideally $500M-$1B+; a stock can have a perfect chart pattern (a "good EP") and still be un-tradable at size if it's a micro-cap with too little liquidity (2021-01-13, 2021-01-21).
- ADR (average daily range) is central to stock selection — he wants high-ADR ("fast") names because they can deliver outsized moves; a stock with ADR of 2-3% is unlikely to ever produce a 20%+ move regardless of setup quality (2021-01-25, 2021-01-27).
- Building a list of the fastest-growing (triple-digit EPS/revenue growth) stocks and tracking it religiously is presented as a low-effort, repeatable edge — no scans required (2021-01-20).
- Fundamentals matter to him only in combination with a technical setup; he never buys purely on fundamentals or a growth story alone (2021-01-20).
- Watches sector rotation closely (department stores, airlines/cruise lines, EV/battery names, crypto-proxies, uranium, cybersecurity/software) and prioritizes stocks in whatever sector is currently showing the most relative strength rather than staying loyal to any one theme (2021-01-08, 2021-01-11, 2021-01-12, 2021-01-13).
- Wants relative strength — a stock resisting a broad-market or sector pullback, or one that stalls rather than falls when everything else is dropping, is flagged as a leadership candidate (2021-01-11).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Selling into strength ("fear of giving back profits exceeding fear of missing out") is presented as an emotionally hard but often correct decision, exemplified by selling RIOT before a weekend that avoided a big gap-down (2021-01-11).
- He describes "playing rocket scientist" — trying to be clever/outsmart simple trailing-stop rules — as a recurring, costly mistake, and says simply following the mechanical system would have made him much more money (2021-01-26).
- Believing you can achieve outsized returns matters: he attributes part of his own scaling to rejecting self-imposed "limiting beliefs" (e.g., someone telling him 10%/year was a ceiling) and studying proven, audited traders instead (2021-01-08).
- No regrets policy: acknowledges frustration/venting is natural, but treats every loss purely as a learning opportunity rather than something to dwell on (2021-01-27).
- Discipline isn't a separate personality trait to cultivate — he argues it emerges automatically once you deeply know a specific setup, know when it works, and know when it doesn't; without that knowledge, no amount of "discipline" training helps (2021-01-21).
- Getting reckless is most dangerous exactly when trading feels easiest (during a hot momentum stretch) — deliberately reminds himself and viewers not to overreach when a market is "gifting" easy wins (2021-01-25).
- Warns against day trading emotionally reactive stock-mentions in chat rooms/social media — recommends ignoring "random" chatter and sticking strictly to your own pre-defined setups (2021-01-13, 2021-01-14).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Never short day one of a parabolic move — his single most emphatic rule this batch, illustrated by a chat member who lost heavily shorting GME/CHNM on day one (2021-01-26).
- Adding to a losing position without a defined setup ("randomly shorting") is called out explicitly as something that ruined him early in his career (the 2014 PLUG short, where he lost a third of his account) (2021-01-15).
- Trading a demo/paper account gives misleadingly good results and doesn't prepare you for the psychological reality of live capital — don't over-index on paper-trading success (2021-01-20).
- Chasing hot stocks already up huge intraday without a specific, pre-defined setup is "gambling," not trading (2021-01-14, 2021-01-25).
- Relying on trading coaches, psychologists, or paid "hand-holding" services is dismissed as largely a waste of money; the fix for most trading problems is mastering one or two specific setups, not general mindset coaching (2021-01-21).
- Technical/platform outages (broker lag, delayed quotes) materially erode your edge — better to reduce size or stand aside than trade blind during outages (2021-01-27 "When is Gamestop going to stop?").
- Holding onto a losing thesis out of stubbornness (not taking a loss) is called one of the most common and costly trader failure modes; he treats hitting stops via market orders (creating visible "wicks" on the chart) as evidence he consistently forces himself to exit (2021-01-25).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Best trading period of the day is typically the first hour to hour-and-a-half; he does very little trading later in the session (2021-01-08).
- He keeps a trading journal (entries, exits, stop levels, setup/methodology tags) and periodically reviews historical watch lists to reinforce pattern recognition — recommends this practice explicitly to viewers (2021-01-13, 2021-01-26).
- Studying the best available traders (Dan Sanger's audited newsletters, old Market Wizards books) rather than unverified social-media "gurus" is repeatedly recommended as the correct way to build conviction in a method (2021-01-08).
- Believes swing trading is structurally superior to day trading for compounding an account, and says he would have started swing trading immediately if restarting his career, despite day trading offering more "instant gratification" (2021-01-22 "Setups starting to emerge again!").
- Distinguishes a genuine short squeeze (a stock like GME with a broken business model rising purely off supply/demand and short covering) from a fundamentally justified rally — the trading approach (technical, setup-based) is identical either way; the "why" behind the move is irrelevant to execution (2021-01-25, 2021-01-26).
