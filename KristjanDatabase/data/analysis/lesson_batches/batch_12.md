## Entries & Setups

- The one setup he keeps teaching: a stock makes a big momentum move, pulls back or goes sideways, finds support on the rising 10-, 20-, or 50-day moving average, builds a series of higher lows, tightens into a narrow range, then breaks out on high volume. Every "five-star setup" example given on stream fits this template (2020-08-10 x2, 2020-08-12, 2020-08-13, 2020-08-19, 2020-08-21).
- He buys as early as possible — right as a stock starts breaking its range, not after confirmation (2020-08-10, 2020-08-13).
- He calls himself a breakout trader, not a pullback buyer: he waits for a breakout *out of* a pullback rather than buying the dip itself, because the strongest stocks rarely give a clean pullback entry (2020-08-17).
- Tightness (narrowing daily ranges) before the breakout day is what separates a good setup from a mediocre one; wide, loose candles going into a breakout are a red flag (2020-08-20, 2020-08-26).
- Earnings/news breakouts (episodic pivots) work best when the stock is emerging from a long sideways base, not when it's already extended from a recent big run (2020-08-17).
- When a whole sector is running, an individual setup doesn't need to be perfect (3.5–4 stars can act like 5 stars) because sector-wide momentum raises the odds (2020-08-10).
- Rule of thumb: never buy a stock within a few days of earnings unless deliberately taking the earnings gap as the trade (2020-08-10).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- Only three moving averages matter for this system: the 10-, 20-, and 50-day (daily chart); for large caps and short-side work he also watches the 10/20 EMA on the 60-minute chart (2020-08-10, 2020-08-19).
- A stock "surfing" one of these averages with a series of higher lows is the visible footprint of institutional accumulation, distinguishing real momentum leaders (funds buying every dip) from retail-only pump stocks (no natural buyers, need news to move) (2020-08-19).
- On the 60-minute chart, whether the 10/20 EMA is acting as support (uptrend intact) or has flipped into resistance (early sign of backside/reversal) is his main tool for both adding to winners and timing short entries on extended names (2020-08-10, 08-24, 09-01).
- An undercut of a key moving average followed by a quick reclaim is read as a bullish continuation signal, not a failure (2020-08-13, 2020-08-19).
- Best stocks often "show commitment from the start" and barely pull back at all — that inability to dip is itself a bullish tell (2020-08-13, citing Livermore).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Typical per-trade account risk is small: roughly 0.2%–1%, rarely above 1–1.5%, occasionally 2% on high-conviction setups (2020-08-13, 08-21, 09-01).
- Never put more than ~15–25% of account equity into one stock; never include margin/buying power when calculating position-size percentages (2020-08-13, 09-01).
- Target asymmetric reward: at least 10x risk on longs (ideally 20–30x) and at least 5–10x on shorts — a 1:3 or 1:5 setup "just isn't good enough" for a swing trader (2020-08-21, 08-24).
- A 25% win rate can still generate huge returns if winners are trailed to run 10–50x the initial risk; needing a 70%+ win rate to be profitable signals a weak edge (2020-08-21).
- Dollar-volume cutoff for scans should scale with account size (roughly 50–100x account size), with a hard floor around $5–10M/day for anyone regardless of account size (2020-08-10, 08-12).
- Size down to token amounts ("fifth or seventh size") on purely speculative/gambling trades rather than skipping the urge entirely (2020-08-13).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- Standard rule, repeated in nearly every session: after 3–5 days, sell a third to half the position, move the stop to breakeven, then trail the remainder with the 10-day moving average (2020-08-10, 08-12, 08-13, 08-19, 08-21, 08-26).
- Adjust the rule to the market: in choppy tape, trim earlier and more (half by day 3); in strong trending tape, wait longer (day 5) and trim less (a third) (2020-09-01).
- A stop should never be wider than the stock's ADR/ATR — if it is, the entry price was wrong (2020-08-10).
- On fast movers that have already doubled or tripled in weeks, tighten the trailing stop faster than the default 10-day rule (2020-08-24).
- When shorting a stock that's up huge, take partial profits every 5–10% down move rather than waiting for one large target, since even a strong decline rarely exceeds 40–60% in a few weeks (2020-08-24).
- Never hold a near-full position into earnings without a profit cushion and prior trims — he narrates a ~$200K loss on PDD from breaking this exact rule (2020-08-21).
- Flags his own mistake of sometimes abandoning the trailing-stop discipline on very extended stocks out of fear, noting those are often the trades that go on to double again (2020-08-10, 08-13).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy

- The trailing 10-day (or 20-day on slower/large-cap names) moving average replaces price targets entirely — exit only on the first close below it (2020-08-10, 08-21).
- The rare multi-hundred-percent runner you don't get shaken out of (because you trailed instead of taking a fixed target) is what actually compounds an account — "these are the trades that grow your account exponentially" (2020-08-10, 08-19).
- Sell into strength in small pieces rather than all at once, especially in size that could move the stock ("trim and trail is the name of the game") (2020-08-26).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- Breakout trading only works when the market's own 10- and 20-day moving averages are rising with the 10 above the 20; once they roll over, stop trading breakouts and sit out entirely (2020-08-12).
- The hardest regime isn't a market that tanks (no setups trigger, easy to sit out) — it's one that keeps producing legitimate-looking setups that then fail to follow through, which is what much of this batch describes (2020-08-19).
- A euphoric, "everything straight up" tape (late Aug 2020) is called the most dangerous time to be an aggressive long swing trader, even though it feels the easiest (2020-09-01).
- Sector-rotation attempts into laggards (airlines, cruise lines, casinos) during a strong growth-led tape are worth small exposure but tend to fail and snap back quickly, while software/internet leadership persists (2020-08-10, 08-17, 08-19, 08-26).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- ADR (average daily range, in %) is the primary filter — avoid anything under ~4–5%; sub-2% ADR stocks are dismissed outright regardless of how good the chart looks ("low ADR is equal to [garbage], high ADR is equal to gold") (recurring theme across nearly every session).
- Run three scans (1-, 3-, and 6-month gainers) filtered by dollar volume and ADR and ranked by relative strength, tightened until each returns roughly 50 or fewer names (2020-08-10, 08-12).
- Maintains manual A/B/C watchlists: A/B for liquid, high-ADR momentum leaders he can trade at size; C for stocks with a good story or strong earnings that are too illiquid or too slow to trade seriously (2020-08-19).
- Fundamentals (revenue/earnings growth) are treated only as supporting "fuel," never a standalone buy trigger — a setup still has to be present (2020-08-21, 08-26).
- Insider buying/selling is explicitly irrelevant to his decisions — price momentum is what matters, not insider activity (2020-08-10).
- Short-seller hit pieces (Citron, Muddy Waters, etc.) are treated skeptically; many are wrong, and shorting on a bearish thesis without a technical setup is a losing approach — "follow the price" (2020-08-19).

## Psychology & Mindset

- Walks through Jesse Livermore's rules from *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* almost line by line, endorsing: don't trade every day, distrust your own opinion until price confirms it, never let a trade turn into an "investment" by ignoring your stop, never average down, never buy just because something fell a lot, and don't overanalyze news behind a move — "it's all supply and demand" (2020-08-13).
- Recurring refrain: "your opinion is [worthless] — the less money you've made, the more [worthless] your opinion is." Trade the setup, not a market view (2020-08-10, 08-13).
- Frames obeying stops without exception as the actual foundation of a trading career: "if I didn't obey my stops, I wouldn't be streaming, because I wouldn't be a trader" (2020-08-21).
- Narrates his own real-time mistakes (early-stopped winners, oversized earnings holds, fat-fingered orders) on stream as teaching material rather than hiding them (2020-08-13, 08-21, 08-27).
- Describes trading as a grind 70–80% of the time, with rare home-run trades (caught only via disciplined trailing stops) providing most of the actual account growth (2020-08-19).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- Trading random tickers without a defined, repeatable edge is called the direct path to blowing up an account (2020-08-13, 08-17, 08-19).
- Dismisses "short squeeze" as a myth for almost every case — legitimate short squeezes are very rare, and shorts are too small a share of the market to reliably drive a big move either way (2020-08-14).
- Discourages trading illiquid micro-caps and OTC tickers for anyone without a very small account; dollar volume is the single most important liquidity filter (2020-08-10, 08-12).
- Trying to pick tops on strong/parabolic momentum stocks instead of waiting for confirmed weakness repeatedly leads to premature, losing shorts — "better to short after weakness than anticipate the top" (2020-09-01, 08-24).
- Advises against shorting or trading options before you're already consistently profitable on the long side (2020-08-13, 09-01).

## Other Notable Lessons

- Recommends *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* as his all-time favorite trading book (2020-08-13).
- Says the core skill-building activity is studying thousands of historical winning stocks to build a personal, memorized "setup database," organized by ticker, year, or theme (2020-08-19, 08-26).
- Recommends marking the next-earnings date on every chart as a visual reminder not to buy immediately before it (2020-08-10).
- On tools: values speed, stability, and simplicity over features — Interactive Brokers, Sterling Trader, and TC2000 praised as reliable; thinkorswim criticized as slow and buggy since TD Ameritrade acquired it (2020-08-18, 08-19).
- Dismisses stock-picking social media and paid "guru" services that pump illiquid stocks as worthless compared to studying setups directly (2020-08-10, 08-19).
