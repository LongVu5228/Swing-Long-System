# Batch 25 (2021-10-22 to 2022-01-06) — Kristjan Kullamägi Trading Lessons

## Entries & Setups
- An EP (episodic pivot) works best when a stock was already trending/building higher lows for months before the catalyst, gaps up on volume that clears the stock's key moving averages into "clean air" rather than into overhead resistance, and shows heavy volume (multiples of average daily volume) within the first minutes of trading (2021-10-27, "Earnings season is here!"; 2021-11-09, "Eventually you need to be able to stand on your own 2 legs"; 2021-11-12).
- The best EPs combine strong fundamentals (ideally triple-digit, at least high-double-digit EPS/revenue growth) with clean technicals converging at once (2021-11-09).
- He trades three core setups: EPs, momentum breakouts from bases/flags, and short-side reversals — nothing else (2021-11-12).
- No need to be first into a breakout; you can buy the 5-minute or 60-minute confirmation instead of the 1-minute, letting the stock "prove itself" (2021-10-27).
- Favorite reversal short: after a 2-3 leg parabolic run stalls, price builds lower highs into a range, then breaks that range — a "textbook" repeatable short pattern (2021-12-09, 2021-12-10 "Santa stopped buying").
- Mirror-image long-side reversal short: a beaten-down stock rallies 3-4 days into its declining moving averages and fails there (2021-12-09).
- A shakeout below a key moving average that's reclaimed within a day or two is a strong bullish tell and often precedes a big leg higher (2021-12-29 "Many bear flags").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Strong stocks "surf" the rising 10- and 20-day (or weekly/monthly equivalent); a close below one of these with no other reason to sell is not a sell signal (2021-11-11, 2021-11-12).
- The same moving-average logic scales to any timeframe — daily, weekly, monthly, even a "decade" chart (used jokingly to describe Buffett's style) (2021-11-11, 2021-11-12).
- "Tennis ball action" — a fast dip below support immediately reclaimed — signals real underlying strength (2021-11-09, 2021-12-09).
- A stock can be green on the day and still be technically weak, or red and still show strength; judge by moving averages and relative behavior, not the daily percentage (2021-11-09).
- Distinguish genuine parabolic moves (100%+ intraday) from ordinary breakouts before shorting — most things chat calls "parabolic" aren't (2021-11-12).
- Head-and-shoulders is called one of the least reliable, most useless patterns (2021-12-15).
- ~90% of textbook technical analysis is "crap" and only the fraction applied to the right (liquid, in-favor, momentum) stocks actually works (2021-11-09, 2021-11-05).
- Relative strength/weakness vs. sector and market matters more than absolute price action: names unable to rally on days the market bounces are broken; leaders that hold up during a pullback tend to lead the next rally (2021-12-29, 2021-12-31).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Typical stop size is roughly 1.5x ATR/CDR (2021-11-11).
- A ~$10k swing account should hold no more than 6-7 positions; smaller accounts can concentrate more but take on more volatility (2021-12-29).
- Small position size is what lets you hold a name through a large drawdown (e.g., holding a lithium stock through a 36% pullback because it was only 1-2% of the account) — impossible at full size (2021-12-15, 2021-12-31).
- Target at least 1:5, ideally 1:10 risk/reward per trade; that ratio is much harder to achieve shorting than going long (2021-12-29).
- Avoid stocks under ~5 ADR (never below 4) for active swing trading in a small account; low-ADR names are "an epic waste of time" (2021-10-27).
- Don't get over-margined into a straight-up market; track what's acting well vs. poorly so you're not caught exposed on a reversal (2021-11-09).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Move stops to breakeven or better on most positions once they're working, but leave your highest-conviction holdings extra room (2021-12-29).
- For choppy or commodity-type names, use a wider stop and manage off the weekly chart rather than the daily (2021-12-30).
- Repeated regret across many streams: not adding enough size on breakouts/continuation flags of stocks already working (2021-10-22, 2021-10-27).
- Use the 20-day or 50-day (not the tighter 10-day) as a trailing stop for most positions; the 10-day is reserved for very extended names (2021-11-11).
- It's never wrong to cut exposure on both the long and short book at once when the market's action doesn't match either (2021-12-29).
- Sell rules matter more than being right — lack of sell rules is why traders bag-hold losers for years (2021-12-15, 2022-01-04).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Sell partials into rare, outsized one-day spikes rather than relying purely on a trailing stop, since moves of that magnitude are uncommon (2021-10-27).
- Selling everything into strength is a "loser's strategy" — always keep a trailing runner for the home-run outcome (2021-10-27, 2021-11-09).
- "Better to sell 10% too late than 100% too early" on a strong, high-volume leader (2021-11-09).
- In choppier markets, take profits faster and don't assume you can hold winners for weeks/months the way you can in a strong trend (2021-12-17).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- The biggest gains historically cluster right after corrections or long consolidations, not during grinding chop (2021-12-03, 2021-12-07 "Santa Rally starting now!").
- Expect to be in a portfolio drawdown roughly 95% of the time even in a strong year — that's normal, not failure (2021-12-03, 2021-12-07).
- Simple regime rule: be long when stocks are above the rising 10/20; be in cash when they fall below — ignore Fed/rate/inflation headlines (2021-11-12).
- Rate-hike cycles historically don't kill bull markets immediately; markets often keep rising 6-9 months after hikes begin, making "rates up = bearish" a lazy assumption (2021-12-30).
- Crashes are usually caused by something nobody was already worried about (COVID cited); risks that are already common knowledge rarely cause the next crash (2021-11-12).
- When only a handful of mega-caps are holding the index up, that's a warning sign requiring more selectivity, not confirmation of health (2021-12-15, 2021-12-29).
- When there are genuinely no setups, stop forcing trades; flip back to aggressive the moment setups reappear — the market can go from dead to opportunity-rich in a day or two (2021-12-07, 2021-12-14, 2021-12-16).
- Momentum rotates: leaders move first, then secondary names, then low-quality "tertiary" names — junk starting to move is itself a lateness signal (2021-11-09).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Filters roughly follow CAN SLIM (current/annual earnings growth, new catalyst, supply/demand, leadership, institutional sponsorship) (2021-11-11).
- "Our job is to be in the stocks other funds want," not stocks you personally think are secretly undervalued (2021-11-12).
- Rising institutional ownership is a lagging indicator (funds buy because price already rose) and shouldn't drive a buy decision (2021-11-09).
- Sector strength is judged visually — several stocks in a group breaking out from long bases at once is confirmation enough (2021-10-27).
- Separate real growth/story stocks with actual revenue from pure hype names with no fundamentals; both can move, but reliability differs (2021-12-30).
- To build a leaders list, check which of the prior cycle's winners are still respecting their moving averages during a weak market — those are the strongest longs going forward (2022-01-01, 2022-01-04).

## Psychology & Mindset
- "You can steal ideas, you can't borrow conviction" — every technique used was borrowed (O'Neil, Minervini, Sanger, Pradeep); real edge comes from independently verifying ideas, not just copying calls (2021-11-05, 2021-11-09).
- Study thousands of historical winners yourself rather than taking anyone's rules as gospel — that's how conviction and pattern recognition are actually built (2021-11-05, 2021-11-09).
- Avoid permabull or permabear commentary; bearish takes "sound smart" but following any biased narrative against the prevailing trend costs money — respect price action over opinion (2021-12-30).
- It took about two years of consistent losing before trading clicked; most people quit before that point, which is exactly why most never make it (2021-12-15).
- Making money matters more than being "right" — he's been structurally bearish on Tesla's valuation for years yet made money on it by trading the price action (2021-12-09, 2021-12-10).
- Trading well is mostly about doing fewer dumb things each year, not being brilliant (2021-12-07).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Day trading is called a near-total waste of time for most people versus swing trading: worse risk/reward, no margin for error, not scalable to size (2021-10-22, 2021-10-27).
- ~99% of gap-ups are not real EPs — most chat-flagged "setups" should be discarded (2021-11-09, 2021-11-11).
- Don't mean-revert slow, grinding downtrends — that approach only works on names that had an explosive prior move; applying it to steady bleeders is a fast way to blow up (2021-12-15).
- Overtrading mediocre (3-star) setups instead of waiting for 5-star ones is a self-identified core weakness and a general lesson: fewer, bigger, better trades beat constant activity (2021-12-30, 2021-12-31).
- "Love your stops, not your stocks" (via Dan Zanger) — don't hold out of attachment (2021-12-03, 2021-12-16).
- Avoid financial media for decisions; it exists to sensationalize, and its "crash is coming" narrative has been wrong almost every time since 2011 (2021-11-12).
- Don't buy breakouts in a choppy, no-trend market — failure rates spike and it can "decimate your account" within a couple of months (2021-12-17).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Broker/platform outages are singled out as one of the only things besides the trader that can seriously damage an account — always run at least two brokers as backup (2021-10-22, 2021-11-05, 2021-11-09).
- 24/7 crypto access is called an illusion of edge, not a real one; being forced to step away is a form of useful discipline, and overnight gaps often "fill" by the time markets reopen anyway (2021-11-09).
- Complex/short-dated options strategies (spreads, iron condors) are dismissed as a loser's game for a discretionary trader; if using options, prefers deep-in-the-money long-dated calls (LEAPs) on slow mega-caps, sized small, as a stock-replacement (2021-11-11, 2021-12-29, 2021-12-31).
