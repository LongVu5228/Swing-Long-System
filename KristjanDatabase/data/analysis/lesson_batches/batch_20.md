## Entries & Setups
- Episodic Pivot (EP): a stock gapping 15-30%+ on a genuine earnings/news surprise; the entry is opening-range highs (1- or 5-min), and the best ones trade their full average daily volume within the first 5-15 minutes — that volume speed is itself a quality filter. News that's already "known" to the market (expected numbers, old contracts) doesn't qualify as a surprise (2021-02-19 "Lots of strength...", 2021-03-01 "Basebuilding period", 2021-03-10 "Will this bounce stick?", 2021-03-11 "SETUPS ARE BACK").
- Recent IPOs get more leeway than established stocks — scarcity value alone can drive a big move even off a mediocre-looking setup (2021-03-11).
- High-tide flag doesn't require the stock to be at all-time/52-week highs; what matters is recent momentum/velocity, not proximity to highs (2021-03-04 "Bear flags follow through").
- Judge a pattern by its overall shape, not one candle — ignore single-day undercuts/overshoots of a trendline (2021-02-24 "Newbie traders...").
- Breakdown/short setups are the mirror image of breakout longs (lower highs, loss of a rising key MA, then a range break to the downside) (2021-02-19, 2021-02-25).
- Never short day one of a big parabolic move — wait for real signs of exhaustion (2021-03-04 "Bunch of bear flags").
- Mean-reversion long/short (buying a failed breakdown or shorting a failed bounce at a key MA) was called his most profitable day-trading setup (2021-02-18 "Know when your setups work", 2021-03-11).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Tightness beats almost everything; wide-and-loose ranges are "for losers" even when momentum looks fine — though extreme relative strength (a stock ignoring a market-wide selloff) can occasionally substitute for tightness (2021-02-24, 2021-02-25 "Trade with rules").
- Key moving averages (10/20/50/100/200-day) flip between support and resistance depending on trend direction; momentum names often find support on the rising 10/20-*week* MA, not just daily (2021-03-10, 2021-03-17).
- Extreme volatility tends to be followed by extreme tightness, which then resolves in another violent move — this was his explanation for GameStop's later squeeze (2021-03-12 "Serious talk").
- ADR (average daily range) measures volatility only, not relative strength or trend quality (2021-03-10).
- Watching where the heaviest dollar volume clustered on a chart shows where the crowd is trapped/exposed (applied to ARKK) (2021-03-04).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Typically risks well under 1% (often ~0.5%) of account per trade even after huge gains; the entire return comes from catching multiples (5x-50x) of that initial risk on a small number of winners (2021-03-12).
- A real edge needs "margin of error" for fat-finger mistakes, forgotten stops, and rule-breaks — if it can't absorb human error, it isn't a robust edge (2021-02-22 "Easy money over for now").
- Removing just his five biggest losing trades in a year would have doubled or tripled annual profit — cutting losers fast matters more than almost any other optimization (2021-02-25 "Memestocks back in play").
- Shorting math is asymmetric: max gain is 100%, loss is theoretically unlimited; he's personally lost 25-50% of an account in under an hour shorting parabolic movers multiple times, and generally advises most traders avoid shorting altogether (2021-02-18, 2021-03-10 "Bounces everywhere", 2021-03-17).
- Position size is capped by a stock's liquidity, not by conviction — illiquid/thin names get skipped or under-sized no matter how good the setup looks (2021-02-25, 2021-03-01, 2021-03-04).
- Never use a limit order as a stop-loss — it defeats the stop's purpose (2021-03-17).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Add to a position only on confirmation (breaking the intraday high, reclaiming VWAP), never pre-emptively (2021-02-18).
- Trail longer swing winners with the 10-day or 20-day moving average (2021-02-18, 2021-03-04).
- Size down or exit ahead of known event risk (earnings, broker/margin changes) rather than holding full size blind into it — forgetting a position had earnings, or forgetting an overnight short, has cost him real money (2021-02-25 x2, 2021-03-01, 2021-03-10).
- Bounce/mean-reversion trades should be treated as shorter-duration (days, not weeks) and exited faster than fresh breakout trades (2021-03-10).
- Don't override your own sell rules on a "feels too extended" gut call — repeatedly cost him gains when the stock kept running after he sold early (2021-02-22, 2021-03-01, 2021-03-12).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Simple rule he recommends to newer/smaller traders: sell a third-to-half of a breakout position after 3-5 days of follow-through, and trail the rest with the 10- or 20-day MA — imperfect, but simple enough to actually follow (2021-03-12).
- In choppy "hard penny" markets, take profits faster and more aggressively than in trending "easy dollar" markets (2021-03-10).
- He is a "home-run" trader: most individual trades roughly break even, and the large majority of career profits come from a small fraction (under ~10-15%) of trades that become outliers — the job is to not cap those (2021-02-25).
- Selling everything into one pop is a "loser move" on momentum names; always keep a runner because outlier moves are unpredictable in size (2021-02-25).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Core distinction: "easy dollar" markets (trending, broad participation, breakouts work) vs. "hard penny" markets (choppy, low win rate, breakouts fail) — learning to tell them apart takes years and matters more than knowing the setups themselves (2021-02-24, 2021-03-04 "Bear flags follow through", 2021-03-08).
- Best time to be aggressive buying breakouts is right after the market exits a correction or long consolidation — the deeper the prior pullback, the better the follow-on breakouts (2021-02-22, 2021-02-25).
- Warning signs of a topping rally: fewer stocks participate in each new leg higher even as the index grinds up; a rising rate of failed breakouts; a single "genius" fund/manager getting cult media attention (a pattern he says recurs every cycle and never repeats for the same manager) (2021-02-18, 2021-02-19, 2021-02-22, 2021-03-04).
- Personal informal indicator: whenever his own open-position count crept up near 30, it has preceded market corrections at least three separate times in his history (2021-02-26 "Market in limbo").
- Distrusts lagging sentiment/breadth indicators — they flashed "overbought" for roughly a year straight during a huge bull run; he watches only the "leading indicator": are good setups appearing and actually following through (2021-02-23, 2021-03-12).
- No alerts triggering in the first 30 minutes of the session is an informal internal signal of a low-opportunity day (2021-02-26).
- Some of the best rallies happen inside bear markets, but they are harder to trade and shorter-lived than bull-market rallies (2021-02-22).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Prioritize high-ADR (volatile) stocks over low-ADR ones when setups are otherwise comparable — repeatedly cited as a core, simple edge ("ADR matters, get it into your scans") (2021-02-19, 2021-02-25).
- Between two similar setups, prefer the one in the currently-hottest sector (2021-02-19).
- Maintains a dedicated "triple-digit growth" watchlist: consistent (not one-time/"bio-bucks") triple/quadruple-digit EPS and revenue growth, an existing uptrend, and an easy-to-understand business — traded as multi-week/month position trades rather than fast swings (2021-03-08 "Waiting for the next big opportunity").
- Avoids random/choppy stocks with no clean trend regardless of story — "random stocks give random results, superstar stocks give superstar results" (2021-03-01, 2021-03-10).
- Avoids financial/bank/asset-manager names — earnings too unpredictable and opaque for real conviction (2021-03-11).
- Liquidity test: place an order for ~10% of average volume and see if it fills without slippage; some ETFs are far more liquid than headline share-volume suggests (2021-02-25, 2021-03-11).
- Whether a company is a "fraud" or has garbage fundamentals is irrelevant if momentum/setup is real — the one exception is genuine "halt risk" from a credible short-seller report (fabricated revenue, faked factory traffic, etc.), which is a real reason to avoid holding a name overnight (2021-03-12).

## Psychology & Mindset
- The hardest skill isn't learning setups — it's knowing when to trade a given setup aggressively versus stepping back, which took him years of losses to learn and remains a lifelong weak spot (2021-02-22, 2021-03-04).
- "The market will make you a victim quickly if you don't have rules" — rules, not opinions, should drive every decision (2021-02-23, 2021-02-26).
- His own single biggest recurring leak is overtrading in unfavorable/choppy conditions — pushing to find trades when there's genuinely nothing to do (2021-02-22, 2021-03-04).
- When no good setups exist, the correct move is deliberately doing nothing and studying instead of forcing trades — treats slow periods as unpaid R&D time and a competitive edge over traders who keep pushing (2021-02-22, 2021-02-24, 2021-03-01).
- "Love the setup, not the stock" — end the relationship the moment price action or the setup breaks down, regardless of prior attachment (2021-02-24).
- A market flooded with five-star setups (e.g., Nov 2020-Feb 2021) is itself the signal to size up aggressively; conversely, few setups means stay small (2021-02-25, 2021-03-01).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Chasing breakouts deep into an already-extended rally is the most reliable way to blow up — "this is not the time to buy breakouts" was a running theme once participation started narrowing (2021-02-18, 2021-02-19, 2021-02-26).
- Don't mix trading and long-term investing mentality or capital — they require opposite behaviors and time horizons (2021-02-18).
- Traders often "become" long-term investors by accident: they simply stop honoring stops and relabel the loss a long-term hold (2021-02-18).
- Confusing a strong bull-market track record with real skill is common and dangerous; being a "bull-market genius" and being a good trader are different things, distinguished only by how you handle drawdowns (2021-02-18).
- Buying into earnings without a specific setup is gambling, not trading (2021-03-01).
- Don't buy something purely because a Twitter/chat personality is hyping it; once you have a following, be careful commenting on illiquid names since your own audience can move the stock, creating false signals (2021-02-25).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Per-share commission brokers are vastly cheaper than percentage-based commission brokers for active traders — do the math on total dollar volume traded, not the headline rate (2021-02-19).
- Shorting triple-leveraged bear ETFs tends to work better than going long triple-leveraged bull ETFs, due to volatility decay (2021-03-04, 2021-03-08).
- Building a personal database of hundreds/thousands of historical chart examples (he uses Evernote) by sector/setup is how real pattern recognition and patience get built — it can't be learned from a book (2021-02-24, 2021-02-25, 2021-03-04, 2021-03-10).
- Keeps trading platforms deliberately minimal (buy/sell/short buttons, basic quotes); more hotkeys and features tend to correlate with more distraction, not more edge (2021-02-22).
