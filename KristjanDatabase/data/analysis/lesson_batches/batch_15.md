# Batch 15: Kristjan Kullamägi Trading Lessons (2020-10-28 to 2020-11-24)

## Entries & Setups
- Episodic pivots (EPs) require a gap up on a genuine surprise (earnings/guidance beat, biotech data, macro news); best found by scanning pre-market/after-hours gappers rather than intraday scans, and the entry is opening-range highs after waiting ~1 minute to see direction, not buying blind at the open. His scan criteria: up 7.5%+ on the day, above prior day's high, above a dollar-volume floor (he uses $35M, suggests retail can use $10M) (2020-10-28, "Finally getting some fear...").
- The "high tide flag" (a tight flag after a strong prior move) is called the single most powerful pattern he trades — he says he's missed huge money by not adding every time one breaks (2020-11-19).
- With longs you need very specific, narrow entry points — you can't chase an extended move; wait for the range/flag to actually break (2020-10-30).
- A stock closing near its high despite being down on the day, after building a higher low, is a bullish "nuance" — don't read a red day as automatically bearish (2020-10-29).
- Stocks that gap down on genuinely good earnings aren't broken — it just means the stock isn't ready yet; they often set up again and break out weeks later (2020-10-29, 2020-11-02).
- The best breakouts happen when many stocks and sectors break out together, not just one or two names in isolation (2020-11-04, 2020-11-17).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Uses the 10-day MA as trailing reference for higher-ADR/faster stocks and the 20-day for slower, more liquid names he's forced to trade at size (2020-10-28).
- Simple market-regime filter: if the index's 10-day MA is above a rising 20-day, it's a good market; once the 10-day crosses below a declining 20-day, stop buying breakouts entirely (2020-10-30).
- VWAP is his go-to intraday reference for parabolic shorts — failing at VWAP is the short signal, reclaiming VWAP is the cover signal (used constantly, e.g. 2020-11-04, 2020-11-06).
- "Frontside" vs "backside": once the 10/20 EMA on the 60-minute chart flips from acting as support to acting as resistance, that's the tell a momentum stock has turned — this is what he waits for before shorting (2020-11-10).
- An undercut of a rising major moving average (e.g., 100-day) followed by a higher low is a classic bullish reversal tell on both individual stocks and the indices (2020-10-30, 2020-11-04).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Repeated mantra: "the key to this game is tight stops, not wide stops" — if a setup would require a stop wider than ~5-8%, skip the trade entirely rather than widen risk to fit it (2020-10-28).
- Never trade sub-$1 stocks, and use a minimum liquidity filter (roughly $10-15M+ average daily dollar volume) even with a small account — thin names aren't worth the slippage (2020-11-02, 2020-11-17).
- Only add heavier size/margin once existing positions are already profitable — "you need to deserve it before you can add margin" (2020-11-20).
- Don't hold positions through binary catalysts (earnings, macro events) unless there's already a real profit cushion; otherwise sell or trim beforehand (2020-11-04).
- 20% drawdowns are a normal cost of aiming for 100%+ annual returns — the key is that drawdowns should follow big up-legs, not come from a flat or losing base (2020-10-30).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Add to positions only as they prove themselves with strength — never average into weakness on a long (2020-11-04).
- Trail winners with the 10-day/20-day MA (daily) or the 10/20 EMA on the 60-minute chart for faster, more active management (2020-10-28, 2020-11-04).
- Once a stock is up 50-60% in just days, move the stop up aggressively instead of relying on the normal trailing MA, since mean-reversion risk rises sharply (2020-11-06).
- Sell rule for winners: take a third to half off 3-5 days after a strong breakout, then trail the rest with the 10-day (2020-11-06, 2020-11-23).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Core exit rule demonstrated repeatedly with real trades (Beyond Meat, SILO): exit once price closes firmly below the 10-day MA (fast stocks) or 20-day MA (slower/large-size names) — "no stock goes up forever" (2020-10-28).
- Admits repeatedly that discretion cost him money versus just following his own mechanical sell rules — several stocks (NVAX, SPWR) he sold early on gut feel later ran another 60-120%+ (2020-11-13, 2020-11-17).
- On sudden news-driven crashes (e.g., vaccine-headline days), the lesson is to cut immediately rather than hope, and never hold a position with little profit cushion into a scheduled binary catalyst (2020-11-04, 2020-11-09).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- The best time to get aggressive buying breakouts is right after a 10-20% market correction — not after the market has already run for many months, which is when he says stocks get bought at the worst time (2020-10-28).
- Choppy, directionless markets (rally-fail, rally-fail) are the hardest and most dangerous to trade; strong trending markets in either direction are easier (2020-10-28).
- Weak/choppy stretches are framed as "a gift" because they cleanly separate the strongest relative-strength names from the rest — use them to build the watchlist for the eventual breakout (2020-10-29, 2020-11-02).
- Deteriorating breadth on each successive rally leg is a normal late-stage tell, but he says he doesn't weight it heavily since his job is only to trade the strongest individual names regardless of overall breadth (2020-11-02).
- Into major binary macro events (e.g., the U.S. election), reduce risk and size modestly beforehand, then react to what price actually does afterward rather than trying to predict the outcome (2020-11-04).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Core earnings screen: year-over-year EPS growth, revenue growth, and forward guidance/estimates — the strongest setups combine a big current beat with a beat-and-raise on guidance (2020-10-28, 2020-10-30).
- Prefers leading, liquid, high-quality names over cheap/beaten-down ones — "focus on the strong stocks, not the pieces of s***, you'll get much better results" (2020-11-23).
- Uses ADR (average daily range) as a speed filter: faster/higher-ADR names get tighter (10-day) trailing stops; slower, lower-ADR but more liquid names (needed to deploy size) get the 20-day (2020-10-28, 2020-10-29).
- Treats "sector after sector" breaking out together (solar, China EVs, crypto miners) as a stronger buy signal than any single stock's chart (2020-11-17).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Most traders fail from lack of patience and craving instant gratification, not lack of intelligence — patience is needed for the right setup, the right market condition, and staying in a working trade (2020-10-28).
- Following your own rules mechanically beats trying to outsmart the market — cites Jerry Parker (a Turtle who simply followed the system) making far more than peers who kept overriding their rules (2020-10-28).
- The market is often short-term irrational, but you can't fight it or assume you're smarter — you have to "play along" with it (2020-10-30).
- Trading is only not gambling if you have a proven edge plus real risk management; with genuine edge and sizing discipline you could even profit gambling at a casino over time (2020-10-30).
- Studying past winning setups for hundreds of hours is what eventually removes hesitation — once you recognize a real setup you should be able to click buy/sell instantly, no second-guessing (2020-10-28).
- Treats large single-day losses (multi-million-dollar swings) as a normal cost of the probabilistic game — "you have to act like it didn't happen, you can't blame anyone" (2020-11-17).
- Warns against mistaking a bull market for skill — that overconfidence is exactly what wipes people out later (2020-11-20).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Buying breakouts in a choppy/deteriorating market is called out as the direct cause of a string of whipsaw losses (2020-10-30, 2020-11-02).
- Holding a losing or barely-profitable position into a scheduled catalyst repeatedly costs money — always trim or exit first if there isn't a real cushion (2020-11-04).
- Chasing a stock that's already extended far beyond its base, instead of waiting for a proper range/flag to form, is flagged repeatedly as a mistake (2020-11-17, 2020-11-23).
- Overriding your own stop-loss or sell rules — either holding past a mechanical sell signal hoping for more, or panic-selling right at support just before a bounce — is cited as costly on multiple specific names (2020-11-09, 2020-11-17).
- Distrusts trading educators/gurus selling subscriptions despite claiming decades of huge returns — "if they can't back it up with audited returns, run the other way" (2020-10-30).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Distinguishes "hard money" stretches (choppy, narrow leadership) from "easy money" stretches (broad, strongly trending) and deliberately scales activity and size to match, rather than forcing trades in a bad tape (2020-10-28).
- His edge is being in the strongest individual stocks during a good market, not predicting macro outcomes or curve-fitting chart patterns — dismisses paid market-prediction services and Elliott Wave as unfalsifiable curve-fitting (2020-10-30, 2020-11-04).
- When an unusually large opportunity appears (a rare cluster of simultaneous setups), it's fine to take on more risk than normal, but he still scales in gradually rather than committing everything at once (2020-11-24).
- Major multi-year themes (EV, solar) are expected to produce many separate multi-month run-and-pullback cycles, so missing the first leg doesn't mean missing the trade — later legs can be just as profitable (2020-11-20, 2020-11-24).
