# Batch 10 — Kristjan Kullamägi Lessons (2020-06-30 to 2020-07-23)

## Entries & Setups
- Trades only about three core setups and variations of them (opening-range breakouts, high tide flags, and breakouts from tight consolidations off rising 10/20-day averages); these patterns have repeated for 150+ years, they're not unique to him (2020-06-30, "If you don't trade the momentum leaders...", also 2020-07-02, 2020-07-09).
- You must identify a setup and set alerts *before* it breaks out — chasing a stock after it's already run is a losing habit (2020-07-01, "Feels like markets are tired...").
- Prefers reacting to confirmed breakouts over anticipating dips/bottoms: "I tell my stocks what to do, they don't tell me" — buying a range low risks the range failing, whereas a range breakout gives a defined stop (2020-07-21, 2020-07-22).
- Rarely shorts a stock on its first big gap/move day — waits to see how it digests before acting (2020-07-08).
- High tide flags and similar aggressive-breakout setups only work in strongly trending bull markets; in choppier conditions the same setups fail more often and require patience or smaller "mini" versions (2020-07-08, 2020-07-18, 2020-07-20).
- Best shorts combine a technical trigger (loss of key moving average, backside break) with a fundamental catalyst — e.g., shorting a vaccine-sensitive stock right when actual vaccine news broke gave both technical and news edge (2020-07-01).
- Frontside shorting (a stock not yet extended, still riding rising averages) is dangerous and has cost him heavily in the past; the two good times to short are after a real parabolic move or once a stock has confirmed backside (below declining MAs) (2020-07-02).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Primary toolkit is just moving averages: 10/20/50-day on daily charts, 10/20/65 EMA on 60-minute charts — nothing more exotic (2020-07-08, 2020-07-23).
- An undercut of a rising moving average that reclaims quickly, *within an established uptrend*, is a bullish continuation signal; the same undercut-and-reclaim on a random/non-trending stock means nothing (2020-07-02).
- "Backside" (price below declining 60-min MAs, former support acting as resistance) vs. "frontside" (still riding rising MAs) is his key framework for short-side risk — shorting frontside has nearly blown up his account before (2020-07-02).
- Relative strength/weakness versus the index or sector peers is a leading signal — a stock gapping less than the market, or lagging its peer group, often breaks before the group does (2020-07-15).
- When previously uncorrelated winners all start breaking down together mid-day, that's a major red flag for a broader top, distinct from the normal one-or-two-stocks-correct-at-a-time rotation of a healthy bull market (2020-07-14).
- Lower volume during a sideways base, followed by a high-volume breakout day, is the ideal base-building signature (2020-07-06, 2020-07-08).
- An extended stock that refuses to dip (buyers step in on every pullback) is itself a bullish tell (2020-07-01).
- Level 2 is dismissed as a waste of time and a tool sold by people who profit from making trading look more complex than it needs to be (2020-07-06).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Position size is set by two variables: target percentage of portfolio (roughly 7–15% per position) and dollar risk tolerance given the stop distance (2020-07-02).
- Position sizes have shrunk relative to earlier in his career as the account grew — he now takes proportionally less risk than when the account was smaller (2020-07-02).
- Commonly builds with a starter position and only adds on confirmation of continued strength rather than buying full size immediately (recurs throughout, e.g. 2020-07-01, 2020-07-08).
- On parabolic/highly extended stocks, tightens trailing stops to the 60-minute 10/20 EMA instead of the daily 10/20-day MA, since a daily-based stop would give back too much of the move (2020-07-06, 2020-07-09, 2020-07-10).
- Considers options a "sucker's game" driven by lottery-ticket psychology; advises against trading them until you've already made several million dollars trading straight equities (2020-07-06, 2020-07-09).
- Avoids CFDs entirely, comparing them to insect traps built to drain retail traders via leverage (2020-07-15).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard stop placement is set on the entry/breakout day and typically trails the loss-of-day (longs) or high-of-day (shorts) intraday, tightening to shorter-timeframe MAs as a position extends (2020-07-06, 2020-07-09).
- Deliberately sells partial size into strength on winners rather than trying to sell the exact top (recurs almost daily).
- Openly admits a recurring personal flaw: he sells winners too early and has repeatedly watched positions he sold continue another 30–100%+ without him (Workhorse $3.60→$10 example, several NIO/other examples) (2020-07-01).
- Whether to hold a position through earnings depends entirely on how it closes that day — a weak close means trim/exit, a strong close means hold at least a partial (2020-07-22).
- Deviating from predefined exit rules to "be smart" in the moment backfired — he got shaken out of SC, Twilio, and Livongo during a scary market dip, all of which then ran without him; lesson stated explicitly: follow the rules rather than improvise (2020-07-20).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Standard practice is staged selling — trimming partials repeatedly as a position runs, not holding for one all-or-nothing exit (recurs throughout).
- When several long positions started showing synchronized weakness mid-day, he proactively sold five to six names outright and flipped from long to heavily net short within the same session, rather than waiting to be stopped out one at a time (2020-07-14).
- On Tesla specifically, flipped a ~$6M short position into a ~$4M long position within seconds once price confirmed a reversal — illustrates trading price over opinion even at large size (2020-07-21).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Tracks portfolio position count as an informal sentiment/exposure gauge: hitting ~30 open positions has twice preceded a market pullback within one to two weeks in his personal history (2020-06-30).
- Explicitly called a regime shift live: on 2020-07-13 he flagged narrowing breadth and fading breakout follow-through after four months of an aggressive bull run; by 2020-07-14 ("the rug pull") he had systematically de-risked and flipped to his largest short exposure in four months.
- The clearest regime-change signal he cites is correlated breakdown — many previously independent winners cracking at once (2020-07-14).
- After the reversal, argued the market needed to "reset" after the Nasdaq's third-biggest short-term run in 100 years, and that a multi-month digestion period would be healthy (2020-07-18).
- In a genuine melt-up/"runaway" market, extended stocks can run "further than anyone thinks possible," but these are also the most dangerous conditions, especially on margin, because reversals can be brutal and fast (2020-07-06).
- Once a range-bound/choppy regime sets in, don't force aggressive breakout setups; be more selective, size down, or sit out, and shift to short-side "mini version" setups in clear downtrends (2020-07-10, 2020-07-18).
- Treated a wave of retail speculative mania (pump stocks, "suckers gaps," Robinhood froth) as a topping signal, drawing parallels to prior blow-off manias where most gains eventually get given back (2020-07-06).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Core scan logic: rank stocks by relative price strength (top 1–2%) across multiple lookback windows (1/3/6/9/12/18 months), filtered by minimum dollar volume and ADR — deliberately kept simple (2020-07-06, 2020-07-08).
- Targets ADR (average daily range) of roughly 4%+ for trading candidates; below ~3% is considered too slow to build real wealth trading (2020-06-30).
- Doesn't screen on float or market cap for longs — only liquidity/dollar volume and a demonstrated history of big past moves matter (2020-06-30).
- Avoids thin, low-priced "chat room pump" stocks with no institutional support; sticks to liquid, institutional-quality momentum leaders for core trading (2020-07-01).
- Distinguishes "institutional quality" names (higher-priced, smoothly obeying 10/20-day MAs) from pure retail "pump leaders" — the latter can still be traded opportunistically in small size but is a different, higher-risk game (2020-07-10).
- When a sector ETF breaks out, the single fastest-moving stock within that sector usually offers better reward than the ETF or the slow mega-cap leader (2020-06-30).
- Maintains a rolling "back watchlist" of stocks that gapped on news or broke out, reviewed weeks later to see what worked vs. failed and build pattern recognition over time (2020-07-08).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Repeats almost daily that traders need to spend at least 1,000 hours studying historical charts before they can develop real setups and conviction — "there are no shortcuts."
- States his edge comes from having studied thousands of historical charts across market cycles going back decades, not from any proprietary indicator.
- Admits real emotional strain during the mid-July reversal, saying he "felt like a failure" even while up 200%+ on the year — evidence that tilt affects even experienced, highly profitable traders (2020-07-20).
- Warns against following permabears or permabulls who trade on ideology rather than a tested methodology; a real edge requires a backtested, repeatable process (2020-06-30).
- Says he switched from day trading to swing/position trading because big gains take weeks or months to develop, not minutes, and swing trading is easier to manage under pattern-day-trader account restrictions (2020-07-02).
- Frames trading like a profession requiring years of dedicated study (comparable to law or medicine), but notes that unlike those fields, mediocrity isn't survivable in trading — you need to be in roughly the top 1–3% of participants to make money consistently (2020-07-10).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Selling winners too early is his most self-acknowledged recurring mistake (Workhorse and multiple other examples) (2020-07-01).
- Fat-finger execution errors near the open are a recurring, named risk he actively tries to guard against (2020-07-06).
- Overriding predefined stop rules to "be smart" produced worse results than mechanically following the system (2020-07-20).
- "Chasing the chase" — adding to a position after already having chased it higher — is explicitly flagged as a bad habit even on the occasions it works out (2020-07-21).
- Buying random, thin, low-institutional-support pump stocks outside of small, deliberate "for fun" size is discouraged for most traders (2020-06-30 and recurring).
- Holding an earnings report without a clear rule, or holding an expensive-to-borrow short over a weekend, adds avoidable risk (2020-07-08, 2020-07-14).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Argues markets are not zero-sum like poker: aggregate equity returns are driven by real value creation (companies combining assets/capital into something more valuable, and distributing free cash flow via buybacks/dividends) (2020-07-02).
- Considers politics/macro news noise largely irrelevant to his process — "if you spend more than two seconds on politics you've wasted two seconds of your life"; follow price action instead (2020-06-30, 2020-07-02).
- Primary tools are TC2000 (Platinum, real-time data) for charting/scanning and Twitter/FlyOnTheWall/briefing.com for news context, backed by a personal notes database (built in Evernote since 2013) tracking stock stories/themes so he isn't trading blind (2020-06-30, 2020-07-06).
