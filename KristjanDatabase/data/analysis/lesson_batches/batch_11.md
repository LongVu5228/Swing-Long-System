## Entries & Setups
- Core repeatable pattern ("high tide flag"): a momentum leader makes a big move, then rides the rising 10- or 20-day moving average (sometimes the 50-day for slower names), tightening into higher lows, then breaks out on above-average volume. Called it the one setup worth mastering to make real money. (2020-07-28, 2020-08-03, 2020-08-04)
- Standard trigger is an "opening range" break — the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle. Faster (1/5-min) entries get you in earlier but fail more often; the 60-min candle is more reliable but later. (2020-08-07)
- Avoid chasing a stock already up more than its average true range for the day; ideal entries are under half the normal daily range. (2020-08-03)
- Gap-downs on good earnings are still buyable if the stock gaps into support (a base or moving average) and then reclaims opening-range highs. (2020-08-06)
- Mirror short setup: a name up sharply several days straight and stretched far from its moving averages is a parabolic-short candidate, but timing matters — you can't be early. (2020-08-04)
- Personally trades only three setups (basic breakout/flag, pullback-to-support buy, parabolic short); tells newer traders to master just the breakout and ignore everything else. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-04, 2020-08-07)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Uses only the 10/20/50-day moving averages plus volume — no other indicators, no level 2, no need to watch intraday charts once a swing position is on. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-06)
- Which moving average a stock "obeys" signals its relative strength: strongest names hold the 10-day, strong ones the 20-day, slower ones the 50-day. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-04)
- A stock that dips below a moving average intraday but closes back above it ("undercut and reclaim") is showing real relative strength worth watching. (2020-07-24, 2020-08-03)
- A red day can still be constructive if that day's low is higher than the prior day's low — don't read every down day as a breakdown. (2020-08-05)
- Former support flipping into resistance (e.g., a 20-day the stock can no longer reclaim) is an early warning sign to watch across leading names for market health clues. (2020-07-27)
- Stocks often move opposite to what the news would suggest ("buy the rumor, sell the news") — reactions depend more on how extended/based a stock was than on the headline itself. (2020-08-06)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Sizing guide by account size: under $100k, cap at ~4 stocks (up to 25% each); $100k–a few million, ~5 stocks (~20% each); larger accounts similar or smaller weights. Never put 50%+ into one name — a single-name shock can end a trading career. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-05)
- "The exit should be clear even before you enter" — holding a position with no defined stop is described as the single biggest way to blow up an account. (2020-07-24)
- Default long stop is the low of the entry day, used roughly 90% of the time. (2020-08-04)
- Uses stop-market, not stop-limit orders on volatile names, since a stop-limit can fail to fill while price keeps falling. (2020-08-03)
- No such thing as market makers "hunting" stops — moves are just algorithms and bad luck, not manipulation. (2020-07-29)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard scaling plan: sell about a third to half after the first 3–5 days of a winning move, move the stop to breakeven, then trail the remainder using a close below the 10-day (not an intraday touch) as the stop. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-04)
- Several of his biggest winners were held through scary intraday dips specifically because price never closed below the 10-day — the daily close is what matters, not the wick. (2020-08-03)
- On fast/pump-type movers, trims roughly 5–10% of the position every 3–7% pop rather than exiting all at once. (2020-07-31, 2020-08-03)
- Admits to routinely breaking his own rules by selling extended-looking winners too early, then watching them run another 40–50% — calls it one of his biggest recurring weaknesses. (2020-08-04)
- Sizes down or exits ahead of earnings unless already sitting on a large profit cushion; only holds through earnings with partial size when the gain can absorb a bad gap. (2020-07-28, 2020-08-05, 2020-08-06)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Profit targets scale with perceived potential: a name expected to move 15–25% gets sold faster, while a name he thinks can double or triple gets sold extremely slowly, a few percent at a time. (2020-07-24)
- No fixed price targets — "let the stock tell you," using the 10-day trailing stop instead of predicting tops or leaning on macro opinions (Fed, elections, etc.). (2020-08-03)
- Repeated theme: a strict first-close-below-the-10-day rule keeps you in winners longer than gut instinct does — his own discretion has cost him more upside than it's protected capital. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-04)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Distinguishes "easy dollar" vs. "hard penny" markets (borrowing Mark Minervini's term) — choppy conditions produce many small/medium losers per winner; trending markets forgive mistakes far more easily. (2020-07-24, 2020-07-30)
- Rebuilt a $1.5M loss within about a week largely because the broad market and leading growth names kept trending up — a strong bull market is very forgiving. (2020-08-04, 2020-08-06)
- When neither breakouts nor breakdowns get follow-through, that's called the worst type of market to trade. (2020-07-30)
- During a market-wide pullback, the key diagnostic is which stocks refuse to break down — those become the leaders to focus on once the tape turns back up. (2020-07-24)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Hard screen: only trade names with an average daily range (ADR) of at least ~4–5%, preferably 6–7%+; slow, low-ADR blue chips (Visa, UPS, Costco, Booking) are explicitly ruled out as "not trading stocks" regardless of chart quality. (2020-07-24, 2020-08-03, 2020-08-05, 2020-08-07)
- Prefers "hot sectors" — several stocks in a theme (vaccine names, crypto miners, Chinese internet/education) breaking out together — over an isolated single-stock mover, since sector-wide strength implies more reliable follow-through. (2020-07-24, 2020-08-03)
- Largely ignores fundamentals for momentum trades; some of the best short-term movers are outright pump/fraud-suspect names — price and volume behavior matter more than the balance sheet. Institutional-quality growth names (triple-digit earnings/revenue growth) get held with more conviction. (2020-08-03, 2020-08-05)
- Notes that each market cycle has its own dominant growth theme (commodities 2003–07, biotech 2013–15, software/internet now) and that traders need to track wherever the fastest current momentum concentrates. (2020-07-28)

## Psychology & Mindset
- On his own $1.5M fat-finger loss: the real skill isn't avoiding every mistake but shrugging off a big loss quickly and returning to process rather than dwelling on it. (2020-07-30, 2020-07-31)
- Deliberately cut size on a name right after a big accidental loss on it, specifically to avoid revenge trading. (2020-07-30)
- Says the difference between traders who "make it" and those who don't is persistence through drawdowns. (2020-07-31)
- Too much story/fundamental knowledge can work against you — passed on a stock partly over fraud allegations he was aware of, and it ran without him; sometimes pure chart focus beats "knowing too much." (2020-08-07)
- Following another trader's picks will never make you consistently profitable — you can borrow ideas but must develop the skill yourself. (2020-08-07)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Buying an extended stock with no basing/no moving-average support and no exit plan is called the single biggest account-blowing error, worse than a bad entry alone. (2020-07-24)
- Chasing breakouts in a choppy market rather than waiting for confirmation — admits having to "relearn this lesson" repeatedly. (2020-07-24)
- Holding through earnings without a profit cushion is labeled gambling, not trading. (2020-07-31, 2020-08-06)
- Avoids options entirely and criticizes commission-free brokers for poor execution quality from payment-for-order-flow — "there is no free lunch." (2020-08-03, 2020-08-07)
- Trading a random non-momentum stock just because it's moving, instead of the highest-relative-strength names in a hot sector — repeated refrain to "stick to the momentum leaders." (2020-07-27, 2020-08-07)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Recommends reviewing past breakouts/gap-ups a month later to calibrate pattern recognition and build conviction for future similar setups. (2020-07-28)
- A high win rate isn't required for profitability — you can win under 50% of trades and still make significant money if winners are managed well; skeptical of educators advertising 70–90% win rates, since that usually signals low edge per trade. (2020-08-05)
- Recommends building ADR into scanning platforms as a standard filter column so low-ADR names never make the watchlist. (2020-08-07)
