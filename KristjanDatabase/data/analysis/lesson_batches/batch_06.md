# Batch 06 (2020-03-02 to 2020-04-14): COVID Crash & Recovery

## Entries & Setups
- Primary entry trigger throughout: break of the opening range high/low (1-min or 5-min candle), or the first red/green 5-minute candle for a fresh short/long. (2020-03-02, "Still getting big opportunities trading virus stocks!"; repeated nearly every session)
- Signature "textbook" pattern: a stock has a sharp multi-day bounce into resistance/a declining moving average, then fades hard on day three — called one of the most profitable day/swing setups there is. (2020-03-04, "Corona stocks momentum leaving?")
- In a corrective/choppy market he waits for a range to actually build (5-15 minutes) before trading, rather than reacting in the first minutes of the open. (2020-03-25, "Bear market rally"; 2020-03-26)
- Pyramids into winners as a trade proves itself instead of putting on full size at once; never adds to a loser ("never average down, only average up/pyramid"). (2020-03-16, "A MILLION DOLLAR LOSS on $TVIX")
- Generally buys the initial breakout rather than a pullback, arguing the strongest breakouts don't give you a pullback to buy. (2020-04-06, "Starting to see breakouts among my favorite growth stocks!!!")
- In fast/illiquid names (halts, wide spreads) the entry window can be under a minute; he explicitly favors higher-priced, more liquid large caps over thin micro-floats for that reason. (2020-03-19, "$100K missed opportunities because I'm too slow!"; 2020-04-13)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Deliberately strips his charts down to price, volume, moving averages (10/20/50/65/100/150/200-day, simple on daily/weekly, exponential intraday) and VWAP; explicitly avoids RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands and Fibonacci, saying too many indicators cause confusion and contradictory signals. (2020-03-23, "$TVIX on its way to sub $300?")
- Argues technical analysis still works on instruments that aren't driven by supply/demand fundamentals (e.g., TVIX) because price action reflects trader psychology either way. (2020-03-23)
- Core read all through the crash: relative strength/weakness versus SPY — a stock that refuses to bounce when the index bounces (or refuses to break down when the index breaks down) is the tell. (2020-03-05, "China and US growth stocks VERY STRONG!"; 2020-03-20; 2020-03-25)
- Uses Average True Range as a chase filter — won't buy a stock that's already moved more than its normal daily range for the day. (2020-04-06)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Typical per-trade risk is about 0.2-0.7% of account, rarely above 1%; during the height of the crash he pushed to ~1%+ on select setups but flagged that as aggressive. (2020-03-05)
- Overnight/swing exposure in any single stock or ETF is usually capped at 10-20% of the account. (2020-03-16)
- Rule of thumb on scaling: only increase size after a strong stretch (from a position of strength); after a losing streak (e.g., down ~20% from 6-7 losses) cut size in half instead of pressing. (2020-03-05)
- Always uses hard market stop orders, never mental stops — "otherwise you're gonna get into trouble." (2020-03-06, "Will $TVIX be my biggest trade ever?")
- Builds large directional bets (e.g., TVIX shorts) over multiple sessions rather than putting on full size at once, accepting repeated small stop-outs while re-entering the same thesis. (2020-03-04; 2020-03-06)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard stop placement once a breakout/breakdown triggers is the high or low of the triggering candle (opening range or the entry candle); stops get trailed tighter as the trade works. (recurring throughout)
- Sizes down / takes partial profits into strength once a single position grows to a large share of the account, even while staying net convicted on it. (2020-03-06; 2020-03-25)
- Distinguishes day-trade management from true swing/position holds — swing convictions get wider stops and are held through overnight/weekend risk, day trades are managed tightly intraday. (2020-03-05; 2020-04-13, "The patience to wait for the next MILLION dollar opportunity")
- Regularly gets stopped out and re-enters the same idea multiple times in one session if the broader thesis is still intact, rather than treating one stop-out as invalidating the trade. (2020-03-04)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Strongly prefers swing/position holds over day trading, saying day trading produces "very little money" by comparison and forces you to sit on top of your entry constantly, which is more stressful. (2020-03-05)
- Scales out in stages at successive price targets on big winners (e.g., sold Tilray in thirds as it hit 2nd/3rd targets) rather than selling all at once or getting too greedy. (2020-03-26, "Market in limbo. Potential monster setups building though"; 2020-03-27)
- Will hold a stock through earnings when already sitting on a solid profit and expecting good numbers, accepting binary gap risk. (2020-03-04)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Repeated mantra through most of March: "this is not a breakout market" — buying breakouts is actively dangerous in a corrective/shock tape; only mean-reversion setups (long and short) have edge until the market proves it has stabilized. (2020-03-06; 2020-03-16; 2020-03-26; 2020-03-27; 2020-04-02, "My favorite growth stock turned out to be a FRAUD!")
- Frames volatility as cyclical and structural — low-volatility stretches are always followed by high-volatility stretches and vice versa, a 200-year pattern. (2020-03-24, "$TVIX sub $300! f'king awesome!")
- Uses relative strength of the best growth stocks as an early bottom signal: when leaders stop going down with the index (or start basing while the index still chops), that's meaningfully bullish. (2020-03-24; 2020-03-25; 2020-04-06)
- Believes markets bottom on bad news, not good news, because uncertainty gets priced in and overreacted to before confirmation arrives. (2020-03-24)
- Treats a ~20-30% index bounce inside a couple of weeks as a classic sharp bear-market rally, not a new uptrend, and expects a retest of the lows even while trading the bounce. (2020-03-25; 2020-03-26; 2020-03-27)
- When most stocks are moving 1:1 with the index ("everything looks the same"), he explicitly says stock-picking edge is temporarily reduced and he stops running normal scans. (2020-03-25)
- Watches TVIX/leveraged-ETF behavior relative to SPY as a regime gauge — when TVIX stops making new highs while SPY makes new lows, that decoupling signals fear is leaving and a violent unwind is close. (2020-03-20, "$1.3M gain on $TVIX"; heavily throughout mid-March)
- By mid-April calls the tape a "major FOMO" market as Nasdaq rallies back above all major moving averages (+27% in a month) and explicitly flags danger in chasing the V-shaped recovery, while admitting he himself was too cautious on the way up. (2020-04-14, "Major FOMO going on right now")

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- During a hot theme (coronavirus plays) he builds and scrolls a large sector watchlist (40-50+ names) sorted by dollar volume, focusing effort on the most liquid subset, and explicitly criticizes viewers for not doing the same during a historic sector move. (2020-03-04)
- For long-term theme exposure, prioritizes businesses with durable multi-year tailwinds (telehealth, remote work/cloud software, China internet/gaming) over one-off beneficiaries like PPE/hand-sanitizer makers. (2020-03-24; 2020-03-25)
- Repeated core long watchlist through the crash and recovery: AMD, NVDA, Zoom, TDOC, Netflix, Sea Ltd ("SC"), GSX, JD, Tencent — named as stocks he wants exposure to once the market stabilizes. (multiple dates, notably 2020-03-24, 2020-04-06)
- After Luckin Coffee (LK) is revealed to have fabricated sales, becomes explicitly distrustful of Chinese ADR accounting and flags GSX as another name that could be "cooking the books." (2020-04-02; 2020-04-03, "I was shaken out of my $TSLA short near the top...")
- For short candidates in a violent tape, prefers higher-priced, highly liquid large caps over cheap thin names for reliable borrows and execution. (2020-03-19; 2020-04-13)

## Psychology & Mindset
- Reframes a $1M overnight loss on TVIX by noting he's still "outperforming ~98-99% of market participants" who are down for the month — deliberately reframes setbacks positively, saying that's a requirement to survive as a trader. (2020-03-16)
- States plainly that it's fine not to trade when unsure: "there is nothing wrong with not trading." (2020-03-16; 2020-04-03)
- Says trading psychology books are largely unnecessary if you already have a working system — better to spend time understanding exactly when your setups do and don't work. (2020-03-16)
- Admits to catching himself being "greedy" — holding for a bigger target instead of derisking — and calls it out as a bias to watch rather than a virtue. (2020-03-06)
- Says real skill-building came from screenshotting and journaling both the chart and the "market psychology" context of standout setups, not just the pattern. (2020-03-06)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- "Never buy stocks that are going down" unless you're a long-term investor, not a trader. (2020-03-02, "Still getting big opportunities trading virus stocks!")
- Names buying breakouts during a correction as his own main mistake of the period, citing several five-figure losses directly caused by it. (2020-03-06)
- Hesitating even a few seconds on fast, halting low-float names cost him six-figure trades multiple times; flags staying fast/attentive as a hard requirement for that category. (2020-03-19)
- Warns that leveraged/inverse ETFs (TVIX, SPXL, etc.) are not normal stocks — they're mathematically programmed to decay/go to zero over time via contango, and many traders have blown up shorting or longing them without understanding the mechanics. (2020-03-04; 2020-03-06)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Recommended reading list: "How to Make Money in Stocks" (O'Neil/CAN SLIM), "Reminiscences of a Stock Operator" (Livermore), the Market Wizards series, "Trend Following" (Covel). (2020-03-16)
- Explicitly invokes CAN SLIM logic: look for stocks combining price momentum with earnings momentum. (2020-03-16)
- Keeps accounts at multiple brokers (Centerpoint, Cobra, Interactive Brokers) for redundancy and easier/cheaper short locates as account size grows. (2020-03-04)
- Says the only way to really learn to trade is by trading real (if small) size — dismisses paper trading as not building real discipline. (2020-03-19)
