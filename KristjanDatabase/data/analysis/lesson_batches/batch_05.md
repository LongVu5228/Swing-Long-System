## Entries & Setups

- Preferred entry is a breakout from a tight intraday range (often on the 60-minute chart) after a stock builds higher lows for several hours; the tighter the pre-break range, the better the risk/reward. (2020-01-23 "Test driving a Tesla Model X"; 2020-02-05 "Shorting Tesla, a potential MILLION dollar opportunity"; 2020-02-13 "Tesla is flagging")
- On fast-moving momentum names he'll anticipate a breakout a little early once a stock has built several hours of higher lows, rather than waiting for the exact level to print — accepting a slightly worse average for a much better overall size/entry. (2020-02-19 "Tesla going to $1500?")
- A recurring day-trade signal on Tesla during its speed-up run was the "first red 5-minute candle" after a strong morning push — used repeatedly as an add point since the stock tended to rally hard at the open and fade later in the day. (2020-02-06, 2020-02-10, 2020-02-13, 2020-02-25)
- He avoids chasing gaps; the ideal entry was almost always the prior session, not the gap-up morning itself (noted repeatedly with Beyond Meat, Shopify, and Emphasys). (2020-01-29, 2020-02-19, 2020-02-13)
- Buying dips/pullbacks is explicitly called a weakness — "I pretty much never buy dips, I'm a very bad dip buyer" — he only buys strength/breakouts. (2020-01-27, 2020-01-31)
- Long setups he favors: a stock breaking out of a multi-month/multi-year base with a long stretch of higher lows on the weekly chart (cited repeatedly for AYX, DXCM, Roku, Tandem, GDS/Chinese names). (2020-02-14)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- VWAP is his key intraday pivot: reclaiming it is bullish, failing/rejecting at it after a retest is the cleanest short signal. (throughout, esp. 2020-02-05, 2020-02-13, 2020-02-25)
- On the 60-minute chart he tracks a 65-period EMA as major trend support/resistance for extended momentum stocks like Tesla — the first close below it after months of holding is treated as a major regime-change signal. (2020-02-13, 2020-02-25)
- Which moving average he uses as a stop depends on the stock's character: fast, thin, small-cap/"pump" names get daily moving averages; institutional-quality names coming out of long bases get weekly moving averages (10-week/20-week) so they aren't shaken out prematurely. (2020-02-14 "So many good earnings reports!")
- He deliberately avoids RSI, MACD, and Bollinger Bands — considers them noise since price and moving averages already contain that information; RSI in particular is "worthless" as an overbought signal because the strongest stocks stay overbought for most of their move. (2020-02-25 "Now is the time to stalk for RELATIVE STRENGTH")
- A megacap stock trading 100%+ above its 200-day and 40-50% above its 50-day is flagged as historically rare (compared only to Volkswagen 2008) and treated as unsustainable in the short term even while still tradeable. (2020-02-05, 2020-02-12)
- When the market pulls back, that's the best window to spot real relative strength/future leadership — stocks that barely dip or make new highs during a market-wide selloff are noted as the ones to focus long exposure in once the tape turns. (2020-02-21, 2020-02-25)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Typical risk per trade is roughly 0.5%–1% of account equity; he does the math in round numbers (e.g., 1,000-2,000 share blocks) specifically to keep position sizing mentally simple during fast-moving trades. (2020-02-05 "Tesla half a MILLION trade")
- Starter positions are used to establish a foothold in a new idea (often 1/4 to 1/2 of eventual size), then added to only once the setup confirms with a range break or follow-through. (throughout, e.g. 2020-02-11, 2020-02-13)
- He avoids instruments he doesn't fully understand (options, futures) specifically because adding more variables would make it impossible to manage his already-diverse book of stocks at different price points and sectors. (2020-01-29)
- He weighs holding volatile/thin momentum names overnight against borrow-fee cost — once covered a large SPCE-adjacent short before a weekend specifically to avoid ~$20K in borrow fees, even though staying short would likely have been profitable. (2020-02-24)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- On backside (post-top) shorts he explicitly names this as his biggest weakness: he is "never aggressive enough" shorting into weakness because his instinct is to nail tops rather than press winners once a downtrend is confirmed — he calls this a recurring, costly pattern. (2020-01-31, 2020-02-11)
- Rule for shorting parabolic/momentum stocks: don't be early. Wait until a name is already down 10-20% from its high and has built a clear backside setup (lower highs, range break) rather than trying to catch the exact top. (2020-02-28 "EPIC 7-figure profit week")
- He raises stops incrementally as a position works, and sizes down (selling 5-10% tranches) into strength on winners rather than trying to hold full size to an exact target. (2020-02-06, 2020-02-18)
- For long-term core holdings (Alibaba, DXCM, AYX) he gives much wider room — sometimes months — using weekly rather than daily stops, distinguishing "swing/position" management from fast intraday-style stops on cheaper speculative names. (2020-02-14)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy

- Standard practice on extended winners: scale out in pieces on strength (often 10-20% at a time) rather than selling all at once, to avoid giving back a big move while still capturing more upside. (2020-02-19, 2020-02-25, 2020-02-26)
- For the Tesla short, he set concrete staged profit targets at the rising 10-day, 20-day, and 50-day moving averages, planning to hold a partial position toward the lowest target while covering incrementally along the way. (2020-02-05)
- He acknowledges regularly leaving money on the table by covering/selling too early out of fear of giving back profit, then watching the position run much further without him. (2020-01-27, 2020-02-06, 2020-02-13)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- After a multi-month strong uptrend, he explicitly forecasts a shift into a choppier, harder-to-trade environment where breakouts stop working — and advises trading less and being more selective in such stretches, even though it's hard to follow through on in practice. (2020-01-27, 2020-02-03)
- Distinguishes two market types: one where upside targets are consistently underestimated (blow-off/momentum phase) and one where downside is consistently underestimated (distribution phase) — the skill is recognizing the transition early. (2020-02-19)
- Uses the 200-day moving average on major indices as his primary "line in the sand" for how much downside risk he's willing to hold through, and sizes down broadly across his book when that level is at risk. (2020-02-24, 2020-02-25)
- Treats extreme one-day/one-week volatility (like the late-Feb coronavirus selloff) as a sign that a durable low is being put in and prepares scenarios (e.g., a violent flush to the 200-day) rather than trying to predict the exact bottom. (2020-02-25, 2020-02-28)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- Prioritizes liquid, higher-priced growth names over illiquid micro-caps because size can actually be deployed and risk/reward stays clean (repeatedly contrasted AYX/Roku-type names against thin biotech pumps). (2020-02-11, 2020-02-19)
- For sympathy/theme plays (coronavirus stocks, fuel cells, lithium), he draws a hard distinction between "legitimate business" names capitalizing on a real catalyst (e.g., a mask maker with real demand) versus pure pump stocks whose only business is share issuance — sizing and conviction differ accordingly. (2020-02-26)
- Actively maintains a running sector watchlist (33 names in his coronavirus list at one point) and rotates focus as leadership passes from primary to secondary to tertiary names within a hot theme. (2020-02-28)
- Explicitly states a high short-float/short-interest reading is not something he seeks out as a bullish signal — he considers it largely irrelevant/a negative, since he'd rather see organic buying demand than a squeeze setup. (2020-02-19)

## Psychology & Mindset

- Describes trading emotions in real time — frustration, anger, FOMO, fear of loss — as unavoidable, and stresses that discipline (correct sizing, correct stop placement) has to function despite those feelings, calling this "the hard part" that takes years to develop. (2020-02-05)
- States a specific promise to himself on the Tesla short: not to get scared out of backside positions, because backside is where the largest money is made if he can tolerate the stress. (2020-02-05)
- Frames losses (including fat-finger errors, worth an estimated ~$100K over a couple weeks in this period) as simply the cost of trading with size and speed, not something to dwell on. (2020-01-31, 2020-02-05)
- Says he stopped following bearish financial media/Twitter accounts because "no one knows anything," and prefers to let price action alone dictate his stance rather than macro opinions or narratives. (2020-02-13, 2020-02-24)
- Emphasizes that big trading money is made from patience and holding through the boring stretches, not from constant activity — calls himself a "chronic overtrader" and says cutting his trade count in half would likely make him more profitable. (2020-02-11)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- Repeated costly fat-finger order errors (accidentally covering/closing positions) are called out multiple times as an avoidable, expensive habit tied to trading too much size across too many tickers simultaneously. (2020-01-28, 2020-01-31, 2020-02-05)
- Warns against holding day-one breakout trades aggressively — considers day one out of a range/IPO to statistically be where accounts get hurt, from painful personal experience day-trading years earlier. (2020-02-03)
- Flags chasing extended low-float micro-cap "pump" names on the long side (rather than waiting for a proper base/range break) as unsustainable and dangerous once a sector's momentum fades — these are day-trading vehicles, not swing positions. (2020-02-28)
- Criticizes promoters/influencers who tout illiquid microcap positions to huge follower counts as unethical, noting that such tweets can move thin stocks and mislead retail followers who can't actually exit. (2020-02-24)
