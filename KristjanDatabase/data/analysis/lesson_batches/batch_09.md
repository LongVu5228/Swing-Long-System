# Batch 09 — Kristjan Kullamägi Lessons (2020-06-09 to 2020-06-29)

## Entries & Setups
- He trades essentially three setups (and variations of them): (1) high-tide-flag breakouts out of multi-week bases, (2) parabolic shorts on stocks that have gone vertical and gap up further, and (3) the mirror-image long version — a beaten-down cyclical that gaps down hard and is bought on opening-range-highs as it reverses (2020-06-09, 2020-06-11, 2020-06-17, "The market doesn't care...").
- Best long bases run roughly 3 weeks to 2 months of sideways action before the breakout; he lists SC, Livongo, Tesla, Net, Beyond Meat as examples (2020-06-17).
- Buy as close to the base/breakout point as possible — chasing a stock that's already run a few dollars past the trigger kills the risk/reward edge (2020-06-18).
- Standard triggers are opening-range highs/lows on the 1-, 5-, or 60-minute chart; for shorts he often waits for the first red 5-minute candle to break (2020-06-09).
- He almost never buys pullbacks/dips mid-range — only breakouts out of a range, or a pullback that is itself resolving out of a small range (2020-06-09, 2020-06-12).
- For thin/low-float, hyped names, he wants the move to be genuinely extreme before shorting — a merely "up a lot" stock isn't enough edge; needs to be clearly parabolic (2020-06-23).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Core tools are the rising 10-day and 20-day moving averages (EMA on the 60-minute chart, SMA on daily); the 50-day is reserved for slower or large-cap names, and even the "10-week" (~50-day) is too slow for the fastest momentum stocks — he prefers the 20-day there (2020-06-17, 2020-06-18, 2020-06-12).
- Average Daily Range (ADR) is his main speed filter: fast names like Tesla (~4.2%) or Nikola (~20%+) offer far more "bang for the buck" than slow ones like Microsoft or Apple (~2.2%); he built a custom ADR indicator for his charting platform (2020-06-18, 2020-06-23).
- Relative strength is read by comparing a stock's action to the index on red days — a stock barely dipping, or printing a higher low while the market makes a lower low, is "screaming" its strength (2020-06-19, 2020-06-24, 2020-06-29).
- Down days are valuable diagnostic tools that separate genuinely strong holdings from weak ones — "a gift," not something to fear (2020-06-24).
- Multiple leaders breaking their moving averages at the same time (not just one or two) is an early warning sign for the broader market, distinct from a single stock's normal pullback (2020-06-23, 2020-06-29).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Typical position size is roughly 10–20% of buying power per name, occasionally 20–30% with high conviction (2020-06-23).
- Never put 100% of an account into one stock or ETF overnight — that's how accounts blow up (2020-06-18).
- Holding many positions (20+) during a strong uptrend is fine and actually reduces single-name blowup risk (bad news, lost contract) versus concentrating in 3–4 names (2020-06-19).
- Runs a partial hedge — shorting index proxies (e.g., TQQ) against a heavily long book — without needing conviction in the short thesis itself, purely for balance (2020-06-09, 2020-06-12).
- Factors in borrow cost/availability before committing to a short: he once paid ~$20–23K to locate 30K shares of Nikola, and estimated holding a large short over a weekend could cost six figures in fees — sometimes he passes on a "perfect" setup purely because the borrow is too expensive (2020-06-09, 2020-06-22).
- Triple-leveraged ETFs don't trade on normal supply/demand — treat them as a different, riskier animal (2020-06-24).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Initial stop is usually the low of the breakout day (longs) or high of day (shorts); as the trade works, he ratchets the stop up to the rising 10-day, then 20-day (2020-06-09, 2020-06-19, 2020-06-24).
- Distinguishes "hard stops" (live resting orders) from "mental stops"; he mostly runs mental stops and only sets a hard stop once price nears his actual exit level, to avoid unnecessary algo-driven whipsaw fills (2020-06-24).
- A stop placed at the literal high/low of day is often too wide and wrecks the risk/reward — tighten to a more precise nearby level instead (2020-06-09).
- Scales exits in stages tied to support breaks: close below the 10-day triggers a partial sell, close below the 20-day is typically the final exit (2020-06-24, 2020-06-29).
- No such thing as market makers "hunting" your stops — algos move price around randomly and sometimes clip a stop, but it isn't personal (2020-06-24).
- Corrects sizing or execution mistakes (fat-finger trades, wrong-direction orders) immediately once spotted, rather than letting them compound (2020-06-29).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Consistently trims into strength — selling a small piece every 5–10% up move — while trailing the remainder with a moving-average stop rather than trying to call a top (2020-06-18, 2020-06-23).
- Avoids selling an entire winner on its first big day; keeps a runner because parabolic moves routinely go further than seems rational (2020-06-24).
- Once a stock is extremely extended (up several hundred percent in weeks, 30–50% above its 10-day), shifts from adding to actively trimming and starts watching it as a short candidate instead (2020-06-22, 2020-06-23).
- Proactively sizes down into weekends or ahead of heavy overnight event risk, even on setups he still likes (2020-06-19).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Historically, real market crashes start below the 50-/200-day moving average, never from new highs above rising 10-/20-day averages — a scary one-day drop near highs isn't itself a crash signal (2020-06-11).
- Best regime for a swing trader is a runaway momentum uptrend where nearly everything works; worst regime is a slow sideways grind lower that lures you into good-looking setups that then fail — in that regime, the right move is to do less and size down (2020-06-17).
- Narrowing market breadth (fewer sectors/names participating even while indices hold up) is an early caution sign that the easy trending phase may be ending (2020-06-22).
- Compares periods of extreme retail speculation to the dot-com mania, which lasted about 18 months — a reminder that froth can run much longer than feels reasonable (2020-06-09/10).
- Leadership rotates in waves between "growth" names and beaten-down cyclicals; when one group stalls, the other often takes over (2020-06-19).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Prioritizes high-ADR, high-dollar-volume, liquid "institutional quality" names because they move more linearly and predictably than illiquid, retail-driven stocks (2020-06-18, 2020-06-23).
- Runs a stack of momentum scans (gap-ups over 7.5% with $15M+ volume, biggest 5-day/1-month/6-month/1-year gainers, etc.) — all built around price/volume momentum, never value screens (2020-06-18).
- Passes on random illiquid pump stocks even when the chart looks decent — without institutional participation there's no real edge, just noise (2020-06-09, 2020-06-18).
- Avoids mega-cap "slow" names (Apple, Microsoft, Merck) for trading regardless of story quality — ADR is simply too low (2020-06-18, 2020-06-23).
- Tracks sector themes explicitly (software/internet growth leaders, COVID-vaccine plays, EV/SPAC names, cyclicals) and will trade the most liquid name(s) within a hot theme rather than every look-alike (2020-06-12, 2020-06-15).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Reframes a stop-out as the market simply saying "not ready yet," not a personal attack — this removes the emotional sting and makes it easier to follow stops mechanically (2020-06-19).
- "Love your stops, not your dogs" — a line he credits to trader Dan Sanger, on prioritizing discipline over being right (2020-06-19).
- Notes his own biggest historical losses have come right after his best stretches — easy-money periods breed complacency and oversized risk-taking (2020-06-19, 2020-06-22).
- Warns that getting rewarded for sloppy execution in an unusually forgiving market can build bad habits in both new and experienced traders (2020-06-10).
- Insists real trading lessons are only learned "the hard way," through the pain of a bad trade — books give you concepts, not conviction (2020-06-24).
- Stresses verifying rules yourself (e.g., which moving average actually works) against thousands of historical charts rather than taking any mentor's word at face value — that's how genuine conviction is built (2020-06-10, 2020-06-12).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Don't chase a stock once it's run well past its base — buying "the second day after it's already up a bunch" destroys the edge (2020-06-18).
- Don't concentrate an entire account in one stock or ETF overnight (2020-06-18).
- Don't keep fighting a strong short thesis in a name with no real edge just because "everything else is going up" — his repeated attempts to short an extended Fastly cost him roughly $135K across multiple tries in one session (2020-06-23).
- Don't fight the tape with macro opinions: early in this rally he tried shorting overextended names and lost money every day until he switched to trading with the trend, after which he says he more than doubled his lifetime trading profits in about two months (2020-06-22).
- Don't ignore borrow cost/availability before committing capital to a short thesis — several good-looking setups were skipped purely because shares were unborrowable or too expensive (2020-06-09, 2020-06-22).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Roughly half his lifetime trading profits have come from short selling (closer to two-thirds to three-quarters back when he was a pure day trader), and he finds strong short setups easier to identify with confidence than strong long setups, since an extended stock's eventual pullback is more predictable than how far a fresh breakout will run (2020-06-09/10).
- For every clean parabolic long setup he sees roughly five decent parabolic short setups — shorting opportunities are simply more abundant (2020-06-12).
- Considers day trading a "beginner's game"; the real money is in swing trading because it captures full-sized multi-week moves instead of small scalps (2020-06-10, 2020-06-17).
- Views Level 2 order-book data as largely useless for liquid mid/large caps, with marginal value only on illiquid micro-cap/pink-sheet names (2020-06-26).
