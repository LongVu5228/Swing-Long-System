## Entries & Setups

- Prefers a small set of recurring chart patterns (flat top + higher lows, triangles, channels, high tide flags, bear flags) that repeat across stocks and decades once you've studied thousands of examples (2020-09-23, "Leaders leading"; 2020-09-30, "Lack of good setups again").
- Wants tightness, not volatility, before a breakout — chasing a name that's already had a big volatile move without first consolidating is a common newbie mistake (2020-09-11, "Are we going to hold the 50-day?").
- Entry triggers are typically opening-range highs/lows (first 1-min, 5-min, or 1-hour candle) for both long breakouts and short breakdowns (recurring across nearly all sessions, e.g. 2020-09-03, "Finally a froth reset!").
- Buying breakouts is favored over dip-buying: the strongest movers rarely pull back, so waiting for a dip guarantees missing the best trades; dip-buying only makes sense at major moving averages (10/20/50-day) (2020-09-23).
- A failed/faded breakout that resets and rebuilds higher lows can become an even better setup the second time around (2020-09-30, VSTO example).
- Wants clear volume confirmation (well above average) in the first few minutes of a breakout, especially on news-driven "newscappers" (2020-09-29, "Major indices getting rejected...").
- Simple recent-IPO scan technique in TC2000: filter US stocks by price growth over the trailing year, then apply volume/volatility filters to cut down false positives (2020-09-02, "Tesla and $TQQQ multi million dollar shorts?").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- Core toolkit is just the 10/20/50/100/200-day moving averages on daily charts and 10/20/65 EMA on the 60-minute chart; explicitly avoids Fibonacci, Bollinger Bands, RSI, head-and-shoulders, and cup-and-handle patterns (2020-09-04, "Finally a washout..."; 2020-09-22).
- Judges relative strength/weakness constantly — between a stock and the index, or between related pairs like Tesla vs. TQQ — as the primary read on which side has real conviction (recurring, nearly every session).
- A "bad market for breakouts" is defined technically as price/10-day/20-day sloping down and stacked below each other; but a sharp parabolic run can look bullish on that test while still being dangerous and overextended (2020-09-04).
- Triple-leveraged ETFs (TQQ, SPXL, TNA) should be analyzed off their unlevered underlying (QQQ/SPY/IWM), not their own moving averages, since leverage decay distorts the levered chart (2020-09-22, "Leaders leading").
- Market cap and float are irrelevant; only momentum and dollar volume (not share volume) determine whether a stock is worth trading (2020-09-03; 2020-09-25).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Typical risk is 0.3–0.7% of account per trade, sized up when conviction is higher — the process stays mechanical regardless of account size, and emotions shouldn't scale with dollar amounts (2020-09-02).
- Never be more than about 1% of a stock's daily volume, and cap overnight exposure to any single position around 10-20%, never more than 20-25% of the account (2020-09-11; 2020-09-23).
- Position size scales with a stock's upside potential — bigger size on names that could double, smaller size on slower movers with 20-30% potential (2020-09-04).
- Shorting is structurally riskier than going long: a losing short grows as a percentage of the account as it moves against you, while a losing long shrinks — described using a leveraged-ETF short trade where being early on a short cost roughly $1M overnight on a 50% gap (2020-09-02; 2020-09-04).
- Stops generally can't be automated pre/post-market; the workaround is manual, immediate execution the moment a stop level is violated (2020-09-17, "Chop chop chop").

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- Signature "3-to-5-day rule": once a breakout is confirmed and hasn't stopped you out, wait 3-5 days, then sell a third to half the position and move the remaining stop to breakeven (2020-09-23).
- Always uses market orders for stops (never bracket/limit stop orders), but uses limit orders when scaling out of winners (2020-09-25; 2020-09-11).
- Trails stops using the prior day's low or the relevant moving average depending on how strongly the stock is acting (recurring).
- Adds to a winning position only on fresh confirmation (a tight intraday range breaking further in the trend's direction), not simply because a name is up (2020-09-02).
- Will cut a position outright — not waiting for the stop — the moment it stops performing relative to peers, to reallocate capital to stronger names (2020-09-02).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy

- No fixed price targets on swing trades; targets are the next major moving-average zone (e.g., shorting into the 20-day, then the 50-day) (recurring across index-short examples, 2020-09-02 through 2020-09-11).
- Lets high-conviction trades run for the "home run" rather than scalping — held a large leveraged-index short for weeks rather than covering into early strength (2020-09-02).
- Position/longer-term trades get much wider stops (50 or 100-day MA) and patience with normal percentage swings treated as noise (2020-09-04; 2020-09-08).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- Frames markets as either "easy dollar" (strong trend, mistakes get forgiven) or "hard penny" (choppy, only the best setups work); considered the market to have shifted into a hard-penny phase after the March–August 2020 run (2020-09-23).
- Cited historical base rates: the Dow has been up/sideways ~86% of the time since 1915, and the Nasdaq has been in a "favorable" condition ~62% of the time since the 1970s, with average bull runs of 9.4 months versus 7.2-month unfavorable stretches — used to push back on "stocks only go up" thinking (2020-09-02).
- Strongly prefers a fast, violent washout (a sharp undercut of the 100- or 200-day) over a slow grinding decline, because slow chop is the hardest environment — it repeatedly "sucks in" swing longs on brief bounces before failing again (recurring, 2020-09-08 through 2020-09-29).
- During corrections, avoids buying any breakouts and instead tracks which leaders hold up (stay green while the index is red) to build a long watchlist for when the correction ends (2020-09-04; 2020-09-24; 2020-09-25).
- September/October flagged as historically the worst months, with market bottoms often forming around mid-October (2020-09-21; 2020-09-24).
- Deliberately ignores macro news, Fed policy, and elections — calls it all noise; only price action matters (recurring).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- Baseline screening criteria: minimum ~5% Average Daily Range (ADR) and enough dollar volume (~$10-15M+/day) to avoid being "too liquid" to move size in; low-ADR names are called a flat-out "waste of time" regardless of story (recurring, e.g. 2020-09-08; 2020-09-23).
- Larger accounts get pushed into slightly slower (3-4% ADR) names for liquidity reasons, but recommends smaller accounts stay strictly above 5% ADR (2020-09-11).
- Prefers sector ETFs (e.g., gold miner or index ETFs) over individual small/micro-caps when a sector move is playable, to avoid single-company risk (2020-09-08; 2020-09-09).
- Strong, consistent revenue growth (more so than EPS growth) correlates with the size of a stock's future move and is used as a secondary conviction check, never a primary trigger (2020-09-08).
- Recommends focusing on only one or two setups, especially in the first few years, rather than spreading across many strategies (2020-09-03).

## Psychology & Mindset

- "It's not about how many times you fall, it's about how many times you get up" — after freezing on a trade, the correct response is to define specifically what you'll do differently next time, not just feel bad (2020-09-04).
- Emotional control can't be taught remotely; it's built through years of pain and thousands of hours, not from books or streams (2020-09-04).
- Patience is repeated as the top skill: "it's not the most active trader who wins, it's the most patient trader" — sitting in cash with nothing to do is correct, not a failure (2020-09-02; 2020-09-08; 2020-09-24).
- Intuition/gut-feel trading is only valid after roughly 5 years and thousands of hours; before that, what feels like intuition is usually noise (2020-09-02).
- Describes roughly $800-900K in losses for every $1M in gains as a normal ratio — big winners require absorbing frequent losses (2020-09-04).
- Recounts a 9-month sideways drawdown in 2015, even after becoming consistently profitable, to frame drawdowns as "a feature not a bug" — the goal is minimizing them, not avoiding them entirely (2020-09-24).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- Buying breakouts in a downtrending/choppy market (price and short MAs sloping down) is described as a fast way to blow up; only get aggressive with breakouts once many setups are confirming across the market at once (2020-09-04; 2020-09-08; 2020-09-11).
- Freezing / not executing a pre-planned stop is called out directly as unacceptable — always have a plan for every scenario and execute without hesitation when it triggers (2020-09-04).
- Deep/dip-buying outside of defined moving-average support is generally a loser's approach, since it guarantees missing the strongest movers that never pull back (2020-09-23).
- Warns newer/smaller traders specifically against shorting parabolic/extended stocks — most blow up attempting it without sufficient experience/capital cushion (2020-09-02).
- Day trading is discouraged for beginners: it demands more skill for a smaller, more fragile edge than swing trading, and can permanently impair a trader's ability to hold positions longer term (2020-09-04; 2020-09-25).
- Following analyst commentary or financial media is largely wasted effort (2020-09-25).

## Other Notable Lessons

- Platform stack: TC2000 used only for scanning/charting historical data (not order execution), Sterling Trader Pro for execution on the two main accounts, Interactive Brokers for a smaller account; avoids ThinkorSwim/TWS day-to-day, citing heavy CPU/battery drain (2020-09-03; 2020-09-30, "Lack of good setups again").
- Building a personal database of thousands of historical winning-stock chart setups is framed as the single most valuable study exercise — more useful than any course (2020-09-09; 2020-09-30).
- "Sympathy plays": when one stock in a sector gets news, other same-sector names often rally in sympathy even without their own news (2020-09-29).
- Heavily shorted stocks (very high short interest as % of float) are prone to violent squeezes once shorts start covering into a thin float (2020-09-22, "Setups are here, but will the market help?").
