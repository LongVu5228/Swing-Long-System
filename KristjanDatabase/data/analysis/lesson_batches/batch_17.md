## Entries & Setups
- The core long setup: a big momentum move, an orderly pullback to a rising 10/20/50-day MA, higher lows building into a tight range, then a breakout on volume — repeated as the "five-star" template across nearly every video (2020-12-18 "Why do I keep selling too early?"; 2020-12-21 "Gap down found rising support"; 2021-01-05 "Let's make this a triple digit year").
- "Strength means nothing without a setup" — chasing a stock just because it's hot with no defined structure is throwing darts, not trading (2020-12-21).
- Best window to buy breakouts is right as the market exits a correction or a long tight range (cites early and late Nov 2020); after 6-8 weeks of nonstop rallying is the worst time to keep chasing new breakouts (2020-12-21; 2020-12-22 "Markets are giving away free money"; 2020-12-23 "Gap down ruined my short setups").
- A second or third flag/setup in the same name in a short window is treated skeptically — prefers to wait for the 20-day to "catch up" before trusting a repeat pattern (2020-12-18).
- Mean-reversion (bounce) trades on names down hard for many days need a specific low-risk trigger (opening range high, VWAP reclaim) — don't anticipate a bottom, wait for actual strength to show up, and a big gap in either direction ruins the setup's edge (2021-01-05).
- Buy the setup at the actual pivot/range low, not after it's already extended off that level — several self-critiques of buying "up here" instead of at the real entry (2020-12-29 "No long setups in growth land").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- The 10/20/50-day moving averages (and their 60-minute EMA equivalents) act as recurring support/"rocket fuel" lines on momentum stocks; calls this pattern's persistence across 100+ years and every market a core edge once internalized (2020-12-18).
- "Backside" = former support (10/20/65 EMA on the 60-minute) flipping into resistance; this is the confirmation signal for shorting extended names rather than guessing a top (2020-12-30 "Brandi Love wishing me a Merry Christmas!").
- Match the moving-average stop to stock speed: fast/high-ADR movers get trailed on the 10/20 EMA on the 60-minute chart (daily MAs would give back too much profit); slower large caps get trailed on the daily 20-day (2020-12-21; 2020-12-22).
- Judge a stop by the closing price, not intraday wicks — undercuts of the 10/20-day that close back above the level don't invalidate the trade (2020-12-21).
- Market health should be judged by how many individual stocks have valid setups, not by how the index looks — an index can look fine while almost nothing underneath is actually tradable (2020-12-23; 2020-12-30).
- For momentum trading, Nasdaq/Russell (IWM)/COMPQ are the relevant indices; the S&P 500 is largely irrelevant except as proxied through Tesla (2020-12-22).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- "Rule number one of a momentum short seller: you don't add to a loser" — used another trader's public account of averaging into a losing short as a cautionary example (2020-12-22).
- Only use margin once you've "earned" it through a run of good setups, and be unusually quick to cut exposure the moment things stop acting right while on margin, since leverage cuts both ways (2020-12-22; 2021-01-07 "I may have a $10M week going on here").
- Keep the portfolio to a manageable number of positions (repeatedly cites wanting fewer than ~20-26); too many small positions become untrackable and dilute focus (2020-12-23; 2020-12-30; 2021-01-06).
- The whole edge is asymmetry — risk a small, defined amount to make many multiples (examples: risk $1 to make $10-50); a strategy that needs a high win rate to be profitable is a weak edge (2020-12-28 "BITCOOOIN!!").
- Never short small/thin/low-priced stocks — bad risk/reward; reserve shorting for liquid mid/large caps and ETFs, and only after they've gone genuinely parabolic (2020-12-29; 2020-12-30).
- Filter every scan by ADR (average daily range); stocks under ~5% ADR are a waste of time no matter how good the setup looks (2020-12-21; 2020-12-30; 2021-01-04 "870% returns in 2020!").

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- "You cannot outsmart the 10-day moving average" — tested overriding trailing stops based on opinion many times and it consistently loses over a large sample, even though it occasionally "works" (2020-12-18; 2020-12-30; 2021-01-05).
- In a euphoric/extended market, deliberately shifts into "profit protection mode": tightens trailing stops, prunes laggards first, and accepts missing further upside rather than giving back gains (2020-12-22; 2020-12-23; 2020-12-28).
- On a big one-day vertical move, considers selling a partial (e.g., a third) into strength, moving the remainder to breakeven, then trailing the rest with the 10-day (2020-12-21).
- If a breakout goes red or a breakdown reclaims the range, exit — the stock is telling you it changed its mind, and there's no edge in staying (2020-12-21).
- A clean exit trigger for a parabolic short is the stock turning green intraday (2020-12-21).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Repeatedly names "selling too early" as his own chronic weakness, contrasting it with near-perfect entries — cites specific regretted sells (TAN, SPCE-type names, meme-adjacent runners) across multiple sessions (2020-12-18; 2020-12-22; 2020-12-30; 2021-01-04).
- Advocates sticking to predetermined sell rules mechanically rather than trying to time a top by feel, even when a position "looks" extended (2020-12-18; 2021-01-05).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Gauges regime health by the number of legitimate setups available, not price action alone — very few good setups, even in an up-trending index, is itself a warning sign ("that's usually when bad things happen") (2020-12-22; 2020-12-23; 2021-01-05).
- Feeling "like a genius" or euphoric after a hot streak is treated as a personal danger signal to size down, since he associates that feeling with the run-up to past drawdowns (2020-12-22; 2020-12-23; 2020-12-28).
- Tracks sector rotation closely and is willing to abandon prior winners: as growth/software stalled in early Jan 2021, money rotated into oil & gas, gold, department stores, and crypto/blockchain-adjacent names, and he moved his focus with it (2021-01-04; 2021-01-05; 2021-01-06 "2021 not showing any signs of slowing down!").
- Macro narratives (Fed printing, low rates, new year, stimulus) don't override technical rules or stops — "the market exists to screw over most people most of the time" regardless of the backdrop (2020-12-28; 2021-01-04).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- In a hot sector, low-quality or even scammy/dilutive names can make the biggest moves — trades the sector heat and the setup, not the fundamentals ("the shittiest stocks make the biggest moves") (2020-12-18; 2020-12-22; 2021-01-05; 2021-01-06).
- Maintains manually built theme watch lists (crypto/bitcoin miners, marijuana/psychedelics, EV/solar/fuel cell, vaccine names), sourced from price action and Twitter — says there's no shortcut, it has to be done by hand (2020-12-23).
- Prefers large, ultra-liquid names that still carry a high ADR relative to their size, since they let him deploy large size with tight, low-risk stops (2020-12-18; 2020-12-21).
- Treats "the only good setup in an otherwise dead market" as a caution flag rather than a green light (2020-12-24 "Merry Christmas!"; 2021-01-05).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Draws a hard line between trader and investor mindsets — a technical breakout buyer sells on a sell signal no matter how good the underlying business is, and warns against buying a breakout and then deciding to "hold forever" once you like the company (2020-12-18).
- "Never fall in love with or hate a stock" — it's a trading vehicle that doesn't know you exist (2020-12-22).
- "Your job is to react, not predict" — let price action tell you what a stock/market wants to do rather than forcing an opinion onto it (2020-12-21; 2020-12-22).
- Notes trading is boring/grinding roughly 95% of the time; easy, euphoric stretches are rare exceptions that warrant more caution, not less (2020-12-22).
- Deliberately narrates his own mistakes on stream as teaching material — broken sell rules, chased entries, oversized bets are shown repeatedly rather than hidden (recurring across nearly all sessions).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Chasing hot funds/ETFs (e.g., ARK) after they've already had a huge multi-year run — the average investor in a hot fund underperforms the fund itself because they pile in near the top (2021-01-04).
- Getting overly concentrated/margined (e.g., 50% of an account in four names at 200% margin) — cites Dan Sanger's history of a large drawdown from over-concentration as a cautionary tale (2021-01-07).
- Setting an unnecessarily high minimum share-price filter on scans, which excludes legitimate $3-5 setups that can be the best movers for a small account (2021-01-05).
- Confusing trading and investing within the same position or account — pick one mentality per trade and stick to it (2020-12-18; 2020-12-30).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Twitter is his primary idea-sourcing tool, but warns that many popular "Twitter traders" post only small size or don't actually trade what they preach — verify before trusting a persona's results (2020-12-22; 2020-12-23).
- Broker choice matters: warns against percentage-based commission brokers (devastating at size) and favors flat/per-share pricing brokers like Interactive Brokers, CenterPoint, and Lightspeed (2020-12-21).
- Presents studying historical market leaders across eras and countries — building a personal chart database of big winners — as the core method for developing pattern recognition and edge (2020-12-18; 2020-12-23; 2020-12-29 "No long setups in growth land...").
