# Batch 24 — Kristjan Kullamägi Lessons (2021-06-16 to 2021-10-21)

## Entries & Setups
- A real setup requires a visible range with higher lows you could draw a triangle/wedge around ("VCP" = just a tightening range); if you can't draw one, there's no edge there. (2021-06-16, 2021-06-17 "$AMC infinity squeeze starting")
- The prior move (flagpole) needs to be genuinely explosive — a stock that already doubled/tripled in days or weeks is far more likely to deliver another 50-100%+ leg than a slow grinder breaking a mediocre range. (2021-06-16, 2021-06-22 "$DDD super breakout")
- Best pattern: stock surfs the rising 10/20-day MA, builds higher lows, tightens into a narrow range, then breaks out on range expansion — described repeatedly as "textbook" or "five star." (recurring across nearly every session)
- Buy the actual breakout (price clearing the defined range/opening-range highs), not inside the range hoping it breaks out later — the latter is "anticipation" and is explicitly discouraged. (2021-06-16, 2021-07-20 "Leaders breaking out", 2021-10-14)
- Undercut-and-reclaim of a key moving average or support level is a strong tell that a stock isn't ready to go down, even if it's not an immediate signal to go straight up. (2021-06-16, 2021-06-24 "the greatest flag I've ever seen", 2021-06-30)
- Episodic Pivots (EP) — huge-volume gap on a catalyst/earnings — are a distinct, valid setup type; volume matters much more on EPs than on ordinary range breakouts, where volume can even show up late. (2021-06-16, 2021-09-17, 2021-10-21 "So much opportunity")
- Mean-reversion longs (a stock down 50-90% showing its first real bounce days) are a separate, rarer setup from standard momentum breakouts and can produce fast 30-50%+ bounces. (2021-06-22, 2021-07-20)
- Buying incomplete flags — before the 10/20-day MA has caught up to price — causes repeated, avoidable stop-outs; wait for the moving averages to catch up first. (2021-06-22)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- "Barcode" charts (wide, choppy, no clean range) are bad setups by definition — a barcode literally can't be tight. (2021-06-22 "Many many setups")
- Only 10-, 20-, and sometimes 50-day moving averages are needed; a 150-day is unnecessary noise for small/micro caps. (2021-06-24, 2021-06-28 "the 20-day vs Kenny boy")
- Avoid logarithmic charts when actively trading momentum names — they compress recent price action and obscure what matters right now; pick one chart type (log or arithmetic) and stay consistent. (2021-07-09, 2021-07-20)
- ADR (average daily range in %) is the key stock-selection/volatility metric because it lets you rank stocks against each other regardless of price; ATR is useful mainly for sizing stops, not for comparing stocks. (2021-10-14, 2021-10-20 "Autopilot mode")
- Institutional-ownership indicators (e.g. in MarketSmith) are lagging and effectively useless since they update only once a quarter — by the time it shows up, the move already happened. (2021-10-14)
- A stock can be down on the day and still be showing underlying strength/"positive price action" if it's building higher lows intraday or holding up better than the index. (2021-10-20)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Cap any single position around 25% of the account — you never know what an overnight gap could do, and over-concentration turns a bad gap into a disaster. (2021-07-07 "No sun = stream")
- A per-trade risk of about 0.5% of account is described as "plenty"; risking meaningfully more is a fast way to blow up. (2021-09-28 "Can we get a real correction")
- When unsure how to size a new setup, size all positions equally rather than trying to get clever — removes overthinking. (2021-06-30 "5-star flag")
- On margin, actively trim into strength to stay "in a position of strength" mentally; margin "can be your best friend or your worst enemy" — the trader's job is to keep margin on a leash, not the other way around. (2021-10-18 "Market back to autopilot mode?")
- Never short small/micro caps on pure technicals — they can get bought out overnight at a huge premium and blow up the short; reserve short breakdowns for large caps/ETFs. (2021-09-16 "Strength and weakness")
- The ADR≥5% filter itself functions as risk management: it eliminates ~95% of "time-wasting" stocks before any discretionary analysis is even needed. (2021-09-16)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard trim/trail framework referenced repeatedly: sell a third to half of the position around day 3-5 of the move, move the stop to breakeven, then trail the remainder with the 10-day MA, exiting fully on the first close below it. (2021-06-16, 2021-06-29 "Lots of setups but NASDAQ getting extended")
- If you choose to anticipate a breakout before confirmation, only do it on partial size (a third or half) — going full size on an anticipated entry is a rule break that gets punished quickly. (2021-06-22)
- Keep some kind of trailing stop on runners at all times; failing to trail cost a chance to fully capture a stock that doubled after being sold too early (NVAX story — sold near a double thinking it was "extended," it then ran another 150%). (2021-07-20)
- On stocks going parabolic intraday, switch to a faster trail (e.g., 9/10 EMA on the 5-minute chart) instead of the daily 10-day, since the eventual reversal will be sharp and fast, not a gentle pullback. (2021-07-07)
- Don't hold laggards just because they're not yet losers — get out of anything showing relative weakness, even at breakeven, and redeploy into what's actually strong. (2021-10-18, 2021-10-21 "So much opportunity")
- When a "leader" asset a group of stocks is sympathetic to (e.g. Bitcoin for miners, Ethereum for related plays) stalls, its laggard sympathy names should be sold — they break down hard once the leader loses momentum. (2021-10-21)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Prefers to sell "a little too late" (e.g., ~20% off the highs, using a trailing stop) rather than "a lot too early" — repeatedly states this trade-off explicitly as the reason to trail rather than cap winners with a fixed target. (2021-07-20)
- Selling because a stock "looks extended" is a self-acknowledged recurring mistake — cited concrete examples of leaving 40-50%+ additional gains on the table in MRNA and UPST by selling early instead of trailing. (2021-09-30 "Major indices looking very vulnerable", 2021-10-21)
- There's no single "correct" sell system — as long as you have some consistent trailing methodology and follow it, "sell whenever you want" is fine. (2021-07-20)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Ideal conditions for buying breakouts: the 10-day MA above the 20-day, both flat or rising; once the 10-day starts rolling over after a long run, that's a caution ("orange light") even though individual setups can still trigger. (2021-07-20)
- Market breadth is critical — a rally driven by only a handful of mega-cap names is fragile and much harder to trade profitably than a broad one; compare the % of stocks off 52-week highs to the index level to see the real picture (e.g., noted the index near highs while ~40% of S&P names were down 10%+ from highs). (2021-09-17 "Buy my course for $9999.99")
- In choppy, low-breadth conditions where "rallies get sold," aggressively buying breakouts has a high fail rate — better to wait for a real flush/reset. (2021-09-28, 2021-06-24)
- A genuinely healthy tape feels like FOMO across many names at once — lots of simultaneous breakouts, not just one or two stocks everyone is watching. (2021-10-14 "Small caps end of year run?", 2021-10-18)
- Actively wants to see the market undercut the 200-day and reset rather than keep grinding sideways, because a proper flush sets up a cleaner, more tradeable rally afterward. (2021-09-28, 2021-09-13 "Back after a break")

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- ADR ≥ 5% is the standard cutoff for any account under ~$10M; sub-5% ADR names are called "a waste of time" regardless of how good the setup looks. (repeated constantly, e.g. 2021-06-16, 2021-06-24, 2021-06-28/29, 2021-09-16/17)
- Low-float, high-ADR stocks are exactly what smaller accounts should focus on (the "small account edge") — low float is what enables outsized % moves, not something to avoid. (2021-06-22, 2021-07-07)
- Within a hot theme/sector, focus only on the momentum leader; laggards in the same group rarely catch up in time and waste screen time and capital. (2021-06-17, 2021-10-20/21 — bitcoin miners, uranium, EVs)
- You don't need to know a company's business in detail, but you should know its sector/theme, since sector-wide momentum tends to move a whole basket of related names together. (2021-10-20 "Autopilot mode")
- For biotech/small-mid caps, track known binary catalyst dates (data readouts, earnings) and stay out ahead of them rather than holding blind. (2021-10-20)
- A hand-built, screenshot-based chart pattern database is described as superior to a spreadsheet/statistical scan for developing real pattern recognition, since trading edge comes from spotting outliers that statistical screens miss. (2021-09-17)

## Psychology & Mindset
- "You need to want it" — most people who claim they want to succeed aren't willing to do the disproportionate work (deep study of other traders' books/blogs/historical trades) that's actually required; talent is developed, not innate. (2021-07-09 "You need to want it. Most only think they want it")
- Don't ask successful traders for shortcuts — go find and study the material yourself in depth instead of expecting a condensed answer. (2021-07-09)
- Frustration after one bad day is normal; if it persists after a full weekend of reflection, that's a sign to reconsider whether trading is the right game for you. (2021-06-22 "Many many setups")
- Blaming algos, market makers, or "manipulation" for losses is a cop-out — losses are the trader's own responsibility, not an external villain's. (2021-09-17)
- Anticipating trades (entering before confirmation) is a repeatedly self-identified bad habit that gets punished almost immediately when broken — "the market has an ability to instantly punish you." (2021-06-16/22/24/29, 2021-10-14)
- Imagination/conviction that a stock can make an enormous move matters as much as discipline; an inability to imagine further upside is what causes premature selling of winners. (2021-10-21)
- Better to have no opinion than a forced, low-conviction opinion on ambiguous price action. (2021-06-17)
- Real trading success requires being a "leader" of yourself; needing a crowd or a following for validation undermines independent decision-making. (2021-09-16)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Buying a random stock having a random up day, with no defined range or higher-low structure, is called out as a fast way to "bleed out" an account. (2021-06-16)
- Buying broken micro-caps once they've lost the 20-day MA — no institutional support, no reason to expect a bounce; once lost, ignore them. (2021-06-16/18)
- Overtrading — taking many mediocre setups in a single session instead of waiting for genuinely tight, high-quality ones — is described as a direct path to blowing up. (2021-06-17)
- Chasing pumps from other finance "influencers" who hype illiquid microcaps to followers while quietly trading against the crowd themselves. (2021-09-17)
- Paying for expensive trading courses from people who claim decades of elite returns but were never actually wealthy from trading itself — a red flag their real business is selling education. (2021-09-17)

## Other Notable Lessons
- There are only a handful of genuinely distinct ways to make money trading (buy breakouts, buy short exhaustion, buy breakaway gaps, buy support in an uptrend, short resistance in a downtrend); nothing about the method is secret or proprietary. (2021-09-17)
- Deep fundamental knowledge isn't required to make large sums trading — stock selection and reading momentum is what matters; fundamentals mainly just add personal conviction on top of a technical decision. (2021-09-17)
- Simpler technical analysis usually outperforms complexity — you don't need to be "super advanced" to beat most so-called experts. (2021-06-18 "$SPY super vulnerable, growth names great strength")
