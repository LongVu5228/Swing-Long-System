# Kristjan Kullamägi — Trading Lessons (Batch 21: 2021-03-17 to 2021-04-12)

## Entries & Setups
- Episodic Pivots (EPs) are held for weeks/months, not day-traded; volume is the critical filter — needs to come in heavy in the first few minutes (ideally pre-market/after-hours too), or he passes. EPs work best when they gap into fresh highs or over a long multi-month/multi-year consolidation rather than into the middle of a moving-average cluster (2021-03-19, "Crypto setups for next week!"; 2021-03-22, "More patience needed"; 2021-04-01, "Semiconductors nice follow through").
- EPs perform best when they're early — coming out of a long base or off a period of weakness — not late in an extended bull run when most stocks are already stretched; that's why EPs mostly failed in the March 2021 earnings season (2021-03-18, "Setups developing but they need more work"; 2021-03-26, "If $AMAT and $LRCX...").
- His biggest long winners are stocks that have already doubled or tripled in the prior few months before he buys them — i.e., he buys continuation, not bottoms. Parabolic shorts follow the same logic on the short side (2021-04-08, "Portfolio strong..."; repeated 2021-04-09).
- Favorite long patterns: high-tide flags, channel breakouts, and flat-top-style flags after a big prior move; these repeat identically across decades of charts (studied via old William O'Neil/market-leader chart archives) (2021-04-08; 2021-04-12, "New project on the stream...").
- Pocket pivots are breakouts of a tight mini-range that occur *inside* a larger base (an "early anticipation" type breakout) — distinct from an anticipation setup, where you buy before any breakout occurs at all (2021-03-31, "NASDAQ is tightening up..."; 2021-04-01).
- Mean-reversion (short) setups are only taken after multiple legs down (ideally day 3+ of a leg, after a prior leg and pause) — waiting for extended, multi-leg exhaustion produces much higher win rates; the same logic applies to mean-reversion longs off multi-leg selloffs. He calls this a hard, high-suffering style he doesn't recommend to most traders (2021-03-26, "If $AMAT and $LRCX...").
- Commodity/ADR (foreign-listed) names tend to be choppier and are generally better bought near moving averages / on pullbacks than bought as high-tide-flag breakouts; some are better traded off the weekly chart entirely rather than daily (2021-04-05, "Semiconductors big follow through..."; 2021-04-07, "Need lots of patience...").
- He never anticipates breakouts (maybe 1% of the time) — he buys once the level actually breaks (2021-03-30, "Bullish signs emerging").
- A gap-up on good news alone isn't a buy; there has to be a real catalyst (fuel) capable of driving the stock for weeks/months, not just a one-day pop (2021-03-18).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Core repeating bullish pattern: undercut of a major moving average (50/100/150/200-day), reclaim, then building higher lows into a tightening range — this shows up over and over in his best trades (roku, sq, mrna, nvda, pdd, etc.) (2021-03-30, "Bullish signs emerging"; 2021-03-31; 2021-04-07).
- On the way up, a rising 20-day (or other key MA) acts as support; once trend reverses, that same MA becomes resistance ("20-day acts as support on the way up, excess resistance on the way down") (2021-03-19).
- Tightness = readiness: the tighter a stock's range and the more its moving averages have "caught up" to price, the closer it is to a real move; wide-and-loose price action is avoided (2021-03-22; 2021-04-07, "wide and loose ... you want nice and tight").
- Failed breakouts aren't necessarily bad — if price reverses down but forms a higher low, that's valuable information and can become part of a legitimate base (2021-03-19).
- For swing entries he mostly ignores intraday time frames once the open passes; he primarily works off daily/weekly charts, only using intraday (60-min, 5-min) for scanning and the open (2021-04-07; 2021-04-08).
- He avoids most indicators/oscillators/breadth tools — moving averages plus price action are basically all he uses; "the more complexity you add, the worse your performance" (2021-03-17; 2021-03-24, "Chop chop chop..."; 2021-04-08).
- A stock's older (10+ year) chart history is irrelevant for a current swing trade — share count and business change over time; what matters is price action in the last 3–12 months (2021-03-30, "Bullish signs emerging").
- Relative strength matters at the sector level: watch for names holding up while indices barely dip, or breaking down harder than indices — stocks moving more than the index (in either direction) is meaningful signal (2021-04-12, "New project on the stream...").

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Never disrespect risk / low-probability events — those are what wipe traders out; the "ultimate risk manager is position size" (2021-03-17).
- He deliberately diversifies across ~15-21+ positions/sectors, mixing longs and shorts, specifically to avoid being wiped out by a single low-probability event or a concentrated blow-up (like Bill Hwang/Archegos) (2021-03-17; 2021-04-05, "Semiconductors big follow through...").
- Concentration is "mostly for losers" unless the account is very small, in which case more concentration is appropriate (2021-03-17).
- Liquidity/position-size discipline: he keeps single-name exposure well below what would make him "the market" in a stock (e.g., capping a micro-cap position rather than risking 10% of the account in an illiquid name) — this and hard exit rules and controlled leverage are what separate him from famous blow-ups (LTCM, Niederhoffer, Archegos, Nick Leeson-type stories); nearly every trading blow-up traces to no exit rules, too much leverage, or being too big in a small/illiquid market (2021-03-31, "NASDAQ is tightening up..."; 2021-03-17).
- Margin has to be "deserved" — only use it once things are already working in your favor; positions on margin have to show immediate follow-through or get cut/sized down fast, because margin punishes hesitation (2021-03-30, "Bullish signs emerging"; 2021-04-01, "Semiconductors nice follow through").
- Stops are typically sized to roughly a quarter to half of a stock's average daily/true range, rarely more; a stop that's wider than the average daily range produces a skewed risk/reward (2021-03-31).
- Stops for EPs/faster movers: 10-day MA; for slower/larger names sometimes trails with the 20-day; can also use a specific intraday low (e.g., an anticipation-day low) as the stop (2021-03-26; 2021-04-08; 2021-04-12).
- For newer/smaller accounts, if you're still losing money after already being down significantly, bank most of the remaining capital and trade only a small working slice; scale the working amount back up only after doubling it (this "grow the base only on strength" approach protects against blowing the whole account) (2021-04-09, "Long bases forming...").
- With a small account, only trade high-ADR (volatility) stocks — a small account has no business trading anything under ~5–6 ADR (2021-04-07; 2021-04-05).
- Never trade U.S. stocks through a non-USD account due to FX conversion fees eating returns (2021-04-12).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- He raises stops (to breakeven or trails with 10/20-day) as a position works in his favor, and lowers/loosens stops when he sees broad market weakness bottoming, giving positions room during a likely reversal (2021-03-30; 2021-04-12).
- When many positions are hitting stops in a short window "one after another," that's the market doing what markets do — not a reason to panic, but he does treat repeated closely-spaced stop-outs as a signal to size down / go more defensive (2021-03-24, "Chop chop chop...").
- On margin, positions that aren't performing get cut immediately even if they haven't technically hit a stop — margin is for making money, not for holding dead weight while other things are working (2021-04-01, "Semiconductors nice follow through").
- Standard rule for episodic pivots: sell some into strength, trail the remainder like any other long trade (2021-03-17).
- He often overrides/tightens stops intraday based on how a name is behaving relative to sector peers (e.g., cutting a laggard early if a sector leader starts failing) (2021-03-24; 2021-04-12).
- Willing to take repeated stop-outs on the same name (no cap on number of attempts) if the setup keeps re-forming, especially on high-conviction names (2021-03-17: "no maximum times"; 2021-03-26 on eth/other names taken 4–5 times before it finally worked).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Standard scaling rule: sell partial size into strength on a big move, then trail the remainder with a moving average (10 or 20-day depending on stock speed) — he says it doesn't matter much whether you take partials on day 3–5 or trail to the close; what matters is having a consistent rule and following it regardless of what the market does afterward (2021-03-17).
- On a large account, he must "get out when I can, not when I want to" — with size, you can't wait for the exact top the way a small account can; sell in chunks as liquidity allows (2021-03-18, "Setups developing but they need more work").
- If a stock breaks out and then goes sideways without stopping you out, it's fine to hold — but only if nothing better is working. In a good market with many strong setups, sitting in an underperforming name instead of rotating into a leader has an opportunity cost (2021-04-08, "Portfolio strong...").

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Best time for breakout/swing traders is when both the 10- and 20-day MAs are trending strongly higher with the 10 above the 20 — "the market goes up 20%, you should be up 200%" (2021-03-24, "Chop chop chop...").
- A healthy market shows breadth expanding sector by sector — breakouts in one sector, then another, then another — not a handful of isolated winners; seeing sectors break down one after another (instead of breaking out) is a warning sign (2021-03-23, "Not great action beneath the hood"; 2021-03-31).
- The "easiest money" is made right after a market pullback/washout — but you have to survive the pullback first; a real flush (e.g., an index undercutting its 200-day) resets for a much better rally than a shallow "cartoon pullback" (2021-03-19; 2021-03-23).
- A normal year (even a bear-market year) usually has 2–4 genuine bull runs; almost all of a swing trader's annual profit comes from those handful of windows (a few months per year) — the rest of the time you're roughly breakeven or slightly down if not careful (2021-03-24; 2021-04-07, "Need lots of patience...").
- Getting stopped out of most/all positions in a short window is a recognizable early-warning pattern that has preceded several major corrections in his own trading history (Feb 2020 COVID crash, 2018, 2019) — when everything starts tripping stops together, that itself is information about the macro environment, and it also naturally keeps you from buying new names in a weakening tape (2021-04-08, "Portfolio strong...").
- A "free money" market (like Nov 2020–Feb 2021) is actually bad for developing traders, because it breeds bad habits and false confidence; normal/harder markets like April 2021 force you to actually develop skill and selectivity (2021-04-12, "New project on the stream...").
- Bitcoin isn't a reliable leading indicator for equities — it's more a gauge of overall risk appetite/speculative money flow, and using it as a market-timing signal is inconsistent (2021-03-24).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Two archetypes make the biggest moves: "global leaders" (dominant real companies) and "hype stocks" (pure momentum/story, sometimes outright frauds) — he intentionally holds a mix of both, but knows which bucket each position is in (2021-03-31, "NASDAQ is tightening up...").
- Liquidity/ADR filters are non-negotiable: he screens for adequate dollar volume and volatility (ADR) so every name on the watchlist is inherently tradable in size; sub-liquid microcaps get flagged and mostly avoided regardless of how good the chart looks (2021-03-22; 2021-04-07).
- Fundamentals matter for conviction, not for triggering the trade — his scans are built around momentum/technical criteria, not fundamentals, because "momentum is going to make you money, not the fundamentals," but knowing the story helps you hold through the shakeouts (2021-04-01, "Semiconductors nice follow through").
- Building a personal "setup database" by scrolling every liquid U.S. stock on a monthly chart, tagging every historical big mover, then studying what the setup looked like right before the move — this is described as the core method for developing pattern recognition and an actual edge (2021-03-24; 2021-04-12).
- He watches for sector rotation/thematic catalysts (chip shortage → semiconductors, reopening trade → airlines/cruise lines, vaccine names, solar) as a way to pre-identify which group is about to have the next batch of setups (2021-03-26; 2021-04-05; 2021-04-07).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Being able to change your mind quickly is essential — being bearish one day and aggressively long the next (as market conditions shift) isn't wishy-washy, it's required; "you can't fight yesterday's battles" (2021-03-26, "If $AMAT and $LRCX...").
- Trading is described repeatedly as a severe psychological grind ("the biggest mind-f*** you'll ever encounter") — the best traders are simply the ones who handle the emotional whipsaw of getting stopped out best (2021-03-23, "Not great action beneath the hood").
- Boredom tolerance is an edge: being patient through slow, low-opportunity stretches (including literally trading almost nothing for days) is what allows you to be maximally aggressive when real setups appear; "the trader who can withstand the boredom best is the one who wins" (2021-03-19; 2021-04-09, "Long bases forming...": three or four trades in a whole week during a choppy stretch).
- Drawdowns (10, 20, 30%+) are a normal, unavoidable part of even the best track records (Medallion, Berkshire) — an equity curve that looks like a straight line is a red flag for fraud, not skill; real returns are lumpy (2021-04-08, "Portfolio strong...").
- He explicitly studies famous blow-ups (Bill Hwang/Archegos, Niederhoffer, Soros's Black Monday loss, Dan Sanger's leverage losses) not to feel superior but to reverse-engineer the recurring failure patterns (leverage, no exit rules, ego, over-concentration) and avoid repeating them (2021-03-30; 2021-03-31; 2021-04-05).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Buying breakouts during a market correction is one of the fastest ways to lose money — breakout strategies specifically need a market that's actually trending, not chopping (2021-03-17).
- Chasing every random ticker mentioned in chat/social media, or every "good-looking" stock, without a specific setup is how accounts get churned in a difficult market — "just because something looks good doesn't mean it's a buy; gotta be super selective" (2021-03-22, "More patience needed").
- Averaging down is explicitly called "for losers" (2021-03-22).
- Day trading does not scale — it's fine for building a small account or for cash flow, but it becomes very hard to compound past the high-single/low-double-digit millions because you can't deploy size intraday the way you can swing/position trading; most people on lists like Market Wizards are swing/position traders, not day traders (2021-04-08, "Portfolio strong...": extended discussion).
- Short-only or overly short-heavy strategies aren't scalable — almost nobody has gotten rich purely short; shorting mainly functions as a hedge/volatility-smoother for long portfolios, not a primary money-maker, and it "doesn't even add alpha" per most long/short hedge fund managers he cites (2021-04-08).
- Following someone else's trades blindly (including his own, live) without understanding the setup first is explicitly called out as a bad idea — several of his "funny money" gambles were framed as things nobody should copy (2021-03-18, "Setups developing but they need more work").
- Trading illiquid microcaps for a large account is a trap: you become the volume, you get shaken out constantly, and even a double/triple doesn't move the needle on account size — better to wait for liquid, larger setups (2021-03-22; 2021-03-23).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Realistic return expectations: a genuinely good swing trader can average roughly 100%/year on the long side without leverage over time; an excellent year in a strong market can be 300–1000%; a small, nimble account trading high-ADR names can compound faster (2021-04-05, "Semiconductors big follow through...").
- Macro/news noise (Fed meetings, quad-witching, bond yields, daylight savings, etc.) is treated as almost entirely irrelevant — "if something like tech weakness is real, you'll see it reflected in the setups; you don't need to know why" (2021-03-18; 2021-03-19).
- Volume matters differently by setup type: largely irrelevant for standard breakout buys (it often comes in after you've already bought), but critical and non-negotiable for EPs, where big volume in the first few minutes is the whole signal (2021-03-19; 2021-03-30; 2021-04-01).
