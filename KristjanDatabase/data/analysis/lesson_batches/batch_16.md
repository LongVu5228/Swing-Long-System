# Kristjan Kullamägi — Trading Lessons (2020-11 to 2020-12 batch)

## Entries & Setups
- Three core setups: standard breakout from a tight range, the episodic pivot (large gap, 10%+, on a real surprise), and the parabolic short (stock up huge over several days, entered on the breakdown/backside). (2020-11-25, "Happy thanksgiving!"; 2020-12-08, "Will QuantumScape $QS change the world?")
- Best breakouts come out of long, multi-week/multi-month "solid" bases, not one- or two-day flags — these produce the biggest, most durable moves (Shopify, Doku, AMD cited). Buying a random update days after the actual breakout day has no edge. (2020-12-03, "Mini earnings season today!"; 2020-12-15, "Neverending BULL MARKET")
- The mean-reversion ("meter version") bounce — a crashed/parabolic stock bouncing intraday into resistance — is called one of the most profitable day-trading setups, but needs a level of daily/60-min resistance to bounce into. (2020-12-03)
- Distinguishes a plain "earnings breakout" (small gap breaking a base) from an "episodic pivot" (large surprise gap) — both traded the same way: buy opening-range high, sell opening-range low. (2020-12-03)
- Chasing a stock with no formal setup, purely on story/sector conviction, is occasionally justified but only in small size ("don't do it at home"); used this to buy QuantumScape and THCB pre-market. (2020-11-27, "Insane markets"; 2020-12-09, "$QS what a beast!")
- Tightness before a breakout ("linear and orderly" price action) is a required ingredient — wide, loose, choppy pre-breakout action is treated as a warning sign, not a setup. (2020-12-15; 2020-12-17, "Everything going higher!")

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Only two indicators are needed for momentum stocks: the 10- and 20-day moving averages (SMA vs EMA doesn't matter), plus VWAP if shorting parabolics intraday. More indicators "sets you up for failure." (2020-12-07, "Should I retire from options trading?"; 2020-12-15; 2020-12-17)
- Strong stocks rarely close below their 10-day; the first violation is often the earliest tell of weakening. In an uptrend the 10/20/50-day act as support; in a downtrend the same lines flip to resistance. (2020-12-02, "Euphoria fading hard"; 2020-12-17)
- A red day on the tape can still be bullish price action if the stock reclaims lows or holds a key moving average — down-on-the-day doesn't automatically mean bearish. (2020-11-27)
- Separates "front side" of a move (still finding support on rising MAs) from "backside" (MAs turning to resistance, lower highs forming) — only initiates parabolic shorts once backside is confirmed rather than guessing a top. (2020-12-09; 2020-12-15)
- ADR (average daily range) is the primary speed filter; avoid names under roughly 4-5% ADR since slow stocks won't move an account. Stop distance shouldn't exceed the stock's ADR, or risk/reward gets skewed — and the initial risk should be measured off the intraday range, not the overnight gap. (2020-11-25; 2020-12-11, "Lots of stocks still setting up!"; 2020-12-14)
- Weekly charts matter as much as daily — mega caps (Amazon, Microsoft, Nvidia) were flagged as "launching pads" off multi-month weekly bases. (2020-12-15; 2020-12-17)
- Down/shakeout days are useful information: watch which names hold up best on a red day — that relative strength points to the next leaders. (2020-12-02; 2020-12-10, "Leaders are still leading")

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Typical risk is 0.2-1% of equity per trade (rarely above 1%); typical overnight position size is 10-20% of equity, larger only intraday. Position size should be calculable mentally in seconds — "if you can't do that, work on your math." (2020-11-25)
- Never scale into a parabolic short as it runs against you; it can extend hundreds of percent further and this "guaranteed" blow-ups. Shorting rules require a stock be up several days and hundreds of percent first — "only play aces and kings." (2020-11-25)
- Avoid extreme leverage (e.g., 50x on futures/crypto) — a 2% adverse move wipes the account; not intuitively understanding why that's reckless disqualifies someone as a trader. (2020-12-17)
- Keep capital split across at least two brokers/platforms so an outage doesn't trap you in a losing position — a second account can even be used to hedge if the primary platform goes down (referencing an actual IB outage). (2020-12-10)
- Margin amplifies results well in a strongly trending market (mistakes get "forgiven" quickly) but should be pulled back the moment a run starts feeling "too easy" — described going into deliberate "red alert" defensive mode during the QuantumScape mania despite being up huge. (2020-12-09; 2020-12-14)
- Long-term edge comes more from avoiding catastrophic mistakes than from being smart — cites Buffett/Munger on "knowing your limitations" and Long-Term Capital Management as a cautionary tale of genius without risk control. (2020-12-07)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard sell rule, repeated across nearly every session: trim one-third to one-half of a winner on day 3-5, then trail the remainder with the 10-day moving average — applied mechanically to every position. (2020-12-04, "Starting to trade options!"; 2020-12-15)
- Give a working trade room to succeed — wait for the first actual close below the 10-day rather than selling into strength out of fear of extension. Cites repeatedly selling NVAX and others "too early" as his single biggest recurring leak this year. (2020-12-15)
- Raise stops intraday as a position extends (to breakeven, then the low of day) once a trade starts working. (2020-11-25 and throughout)
- On broker platforms that produce bad stop fills during fast drops, a "smart" routing algorithm (cited on Interactive Brokers) that pauses briefly for liquidity to return produces meaningfully better fills than a raw stop order. (2020-12-10)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Same trim/trail rule used as the core exit method; for shorts, the 10-day or 20-day moving average is the typical cover target. (2020-11-30, "Pullback attempt after historic November"; 2020-12-11)
- Accepts giving back some open profit on a strong winner rather than trying to time the exact top by selling purely because a stock looks "extended" — repeatedly notes this instinct has cost him large gains (AMD, Tesla, SPWR, Snapchat all cited as regretted early sells this batch). (2020-12-15; 2020-12-17)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- In a strong bull run, the job is simply to find good setups and "let the market do the rest" — align aggressiveness with how favorable conditions are. (2020-11-27)
- Market leadership rotates by cycle (commodities 2003-2007, software/EV in 2020); a trader following price/momentum rather than a fixed macro thesis can adapt across cycles, unlike managers who only ever nailed one era (Cathie Wood and the 2008-crash "market wizards" cited as cautionary examples). (2020-11-25; 2020-12-03)
- When stocks that have no business moving start ripping too (e.g., railroads), that signals a broad melt-up phase — profitable, but a reminder that "it's the market, not you." (2020-11-27; 2020-12-17)
- Being at "peak equity" is rare and short-lived — even elite, multi-year winners (Amazon, Netflix) spent large stretches, sometimes over half of any given year, in a drawdown; expecting to stay at highs is unrealistic. (2020-12-07)
- Sharp reversals historically follow long, straight-up runs without a pullback (references Aug 2020, the Feb 2020 COVID top, and early 2018) — not a top call, just a pattern to stay aware of. Conversely, the months right after a crash are typically the easiest stretch to make money in. (2020-12-17)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Prioritizes triple- or strong double-digit EPS/revenue growth (or a very hot, unproven story) — "earnings are fuel," a stock needs a real reason to keep advancing, not just a clean chart. Cross-checks trailing and forward growth estimates on MarketSmith/Koyfin before committing size. (2020-12-17)
- Identifying the market's 2-3 hottest sectors (by how many names in it have doubled/tripled recently) should be effortless — "if you can't name three sectors off the top of your head, you're doing something wrong." Owning the weakest stock in the hottest sector beats owning the best stock in a cold one. (2020-12-07, "Should I retire from options trading?"; 2020-12-09)
- Maintains dedicated theme watchlists (EV/battery, SPACs, cannabis, psychedelics, crypto) with notes on each name in advance, so he can react instantly once volume/momentum shows up — "you gotta put in the work, no one else is gonna do it for you." (2020-11-27; 2020-12-17)
- Avoids low-ADR mega caps/slow compounders for trading purposes (Starbucks, Workday, Microsoft cited) — fine to invest in, bad to trade short-term. Also avoids illiquid micro-caps/pink sheets regardless of setup quality, since commissions and lack of size capacity make them impractical. (2020-12-04; 2020-12-08, "Starting to trade options!")
- Credible institutional/name-brand backing (e.g., Bill Gates and Volkswagen behind QuantumScape) raises the odds a speculative story is real, though it's no guarantee — Nikola is cited as a story that "duped everyone" despite big-name involvement (GM). (2020-12-03; 2020-12-08)

## Psychology & Mindset
- Deliberately turns more conservative exactly when things feel easiest — describes going into a defensive, "shoot first" risk-reduction mode during the QuantumScape/battery mania precisely because the run was going too well. (2020-12-09)
- FOMO after missing a big winner is named as one of the main reasons traders fail, since it leads to forced, low-quality chases. (2020-12-03)
- Perfection isn't required to succeed with a real edge — obsessing over flawlessness is associated with people selling low-quality trading services. (2020-11-27)
- Took two full years of losses before turning consistently profitable, and estimates his own laziness (not studying specific setups sooner) cost an extra 6-12 months; struggling for a year or two is normal, but still losing money after ~2 years means the process needs to change. (2020-12-09)
- Says he has spent years unsuccessfully trying to "outsmart" his own 10/20-day rules with discretion, and following the mechanical rule beats overriding it. (2020-12-15)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Trading a random momentum stock with no growth story or hot sector behind it — no edge even if the chart looks fine. (2020-11-25; 2020-12-17)
- Overtrading a high-conviction idea pre-market and intraday to the point of exhaustion, which leads to bad late-day decisions (cites overtrading NIO/XPEV/EXPR shorts on a big down day). (2020-11-25)
- Copy-trading someone else's alerts instead of building personal rules — explicitly tells viewers never to follow his trades directly. (2020-11-25; 2020-12-09)
- Trading options without understanding strikes, expirations, or greeks — called needlessly complex, "so many moving parts... one mistake and it gets expensive fast"; his own on-stream options experiment repeatedly illustrates basic errors (buying puts by accident, not understanding premium/delta). (2020-12-04; 2020-12-07)
- Assuming any fast-rising stock is a "short squeeze" — shorts are a small share of total volume for most names, so most rallies are just momentum/long buying, not squeezes. (2020-12-17)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Realistic target for an active short-term trader with a smaller account: 100%+ annually; sub-100% while consistently profitable suggests the process needs work (cites Minervini's "if you're not making 100%, get a job"). (2020-12-03, "Mini earnings season today!")
- Recommends the Market Wizards series (earlier volumes praised, the newest called diluted with mediocre traders) plus material from/about Livermore, O'Neil, Minervini, and Dan Sanger. (2020-12-03; 2020-12-17)
- Warns that many paid alert services/"gurus" are themselves weak traders — judge any guru strictly by proven, real returns, not confidence or content quality. (2020-11-27; 2020-12-08)
- Recommends building a personal log of every big mover (news, gap, breakout) in real time as the main way to train pattern recognition — "no one else is gonna do it for you." (2020-11-25; 2020-12-07)
