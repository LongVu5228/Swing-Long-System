# Kristjan Kullamägi — Trading Lessons (Batch 19: 2021-01-28 to 2021-02-17)

## Entries & Setups
- His three "bread and butter" setups are episodic pivots (EPs), momentum breakouts/flags, and parabolic shorts. An EP only counts as a real edge when it happens early in a stock's trend (coming off a long sideways base); a gap-up on news for a stock already up thousands of percent is not a true EP, it's just a headline (2021-02-04, "Where is the line?"; 2021-02-10, "Buy the lows and sell the top on $TLRY?").
- The cleanest breakouts occur when price has just caught up to the 10- or 20-day moving average, not when it's already far extended above it (2021-02-10).
- "High tide flag" = relentless higher lows after a big move, tightening into a range before another leg; he treats extreme tightness on a high-ADR stock as a signal a big move (either direction) is imminent (2021-02-04, 2021-02-12, "Gamestop $GME ultra tight setup").
- He rarely chases extended breakouts or buys dips — he'd rather let a missed setup go than chase, though he'll occasionally make an exception for a technically clean re-entry near a prior breakout level (2021-01-29, "Meme stonks"; 2021-02-05).
- A false breakout that fails and falls back into its base can set up a stronger, cleaner breakout later — he calls this "embracing false breakouts" rather than treating a failed setup as invalid (2021-02-05, "Many people experiencing their first pump and dump").
- Post-earnings-announcement drift and momentum are, in his view, two of the only market anomalies that haven't been arbitraged away; scanning specifically for triple-digit EPS/revenue growth names is a durable edge (2021-02-02, "GameSTOP. Reality setting in."; 2021-02-09, "Good margin calls vs bad margin calls").
- He largely dismisses "resistance" as a meaningful concept in a strong uptrend — he trades ranges and breakouts, not lines drawn on a chart (2021-02-16, "Slow start to a 4-day week").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Uses linear charts by default; switches to logarithmic only for stocks that have fallen a lot, or for triple-leveraged decay ETFs, where a linear chart hides the real structure (2021-01-29; 2021-02-04).
- The specific moving average (10 vs 20, EMA vs SMA) barely matters — pick one, stay consistent, and focus on the big-picture pattern instead (2021-01-29; 2021-02-08, "Coins are back after some digestion").
- Watches dollar volume, not just share volume, to gauge real liquidity — GME's dollar volume fell roughly 97-99% off its peak even as share volume fell less, which materially changes how tradable a stock is (2021-02-09, "Good margin calls vs bad margin calls"; 2021-02-12).
- On very volatile names he uses the 60-minute chart with 10/20/65 EMAs instead of 5-minute charts, since 5-minute noise gets misleading (2021-02-12).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Risks roughly 0.5-1%, never more than ~2%, of account per trade; caps any single overnight position at roughly 20-25% of account (2021-02-05).
- Stops should be about a third to half of a stock's average daily range (ADR/ATR) — a stop near the full ADR wrecks the risk/reward (2021-02-08; 2021-02-10).
- Caps buy size near 1% of a stock's average daily dollar volume, since a stopped-out round trip effectively becomes 2% of volume — beyond that slippage gets expensive (2021-02-08; 2021-02-12, "Gamestop $GME ultra tight setup").
- Deliberately avoids sizing down just to trade a thin ticker: doing so "conditions your brain" to trade small, so when a genuinely liquid A+ setup appears you won't have the nerve to take proper size — better to sit out (2021-02-12).
- As an account or edge grows, size should scale up accordingly; refusing to increase size after success is called a "loser mentality" (2021-02-12).
- At the first sign of trouble while on margin, cut exposure immediately — protecting the account comes before protecting any individual trade (2021-02-08; 2021-02-10, "Buy the lows and sell the top").
- Tracks results in R-multiples (risk units), not raw percentage return, since percentage gain alone says nothing about how much risk was taken to earn it (2021-02-10).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- On shorts, scale out into weakness rather than waiting for a trailing stop — cover roughly 10-20% of the position for every additional 10-15% the stock falls, since a short's max gain is capped at 100% (2021-02-05).
- On longs, by contrast, upside is unknowable, so he trails with the 10- or 20-day moving average (or 10/20 EMA on the 60-minute for fast movers) instead of setting fixed targets (2021-02-05; 2021-02-09).
- A stop triggered in pre-market or after-hours is obeyed exactly like one during regular hours — time of day is irrelevant (2021-02-11, "Many extended stocks building high tight flags").
- Good setups often shake you out once before finally working — he re-enters without hesitation rather than treating the stop-out as proof the idea was wrong (2021-02-03, "bounce in memestocks soon?").
- Only holds through an earnings catalyst from "a position of strength" (meaning the position already has a solid profit cushion) — never holds into a catalyst from weakness (2021-02-16).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Rates his own entries near 5/5 stars but his exits only ~3.5/5 — repeatedly admits to selling winners far too early during this run, even while it kept working overall (2021-02-09, "Good margin calls vs bad margin calls").
- Sometimes deliberately overrides his own sell rules when a position is exceptionally strong, accepting he'll occasionally look wrong in hindsight either way (2021-02-10).
- Treats taking a loss as mechanically simple (hard stop, market order, no hesitation) — struggling to cut losses is framed as a sign of insufficient study/preparation, not a personality flaw (2021-02-09).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- The best time to aggressively buy breakouts is after a market pulls back 5-10% within an uptrend, or right as it breaks a multi-month range — not after months of uninterrupted melt-up (2021-01-28, "Cool $10M+ day today").
- An abundance of good setups (having to pass on many) signals a healthy, tradable market; a shortage of setups plus rising breakout-failure rates signals it's time to de-risk (2021-01-28; 2021-02-03).
- Uses Tesla's price action informally as a barometer for overall speculative risk appetite — TSLA losing its rising moving averages is treated as an early warning for momentum stocks broadly (2021-02-04; 2021-02-08; 2021-02-10).
- Having several parabolic winners simultaneously in his own portfolio is treated as a market-wide red flag, not just cause for celebration — it has often preceded a pullback (2021-02-09, "Good margin calls vs bad margin calls"; 2021-02-10, "Buy the lows and sell the top").
- Repeatedly compares Jan/Feb 2021 to 1998-2000 as the closest historical precedent for this many stocks moving explosively at once (2021-02-08; 2021-02-09).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Runs three saved scans (1-month, 3-month, 6-month price-growth gainers) filtered by minimum dollar volume (~$150M, a tenth of that for small accounts) and ADR (~3.5%+, 5%+ for small accounts), tightening filters to keep results under ~50 names (2021-02-09, "Good margin calls vs bad margin calls").
- Screens his nightly watchlist in a split second per name (does it resemble one of his three setups at all?), then does a slower 1-5 star rating pass only on names that pass the first filter (2021-01-28).
- Avoids low-ADR "grinder" stocks entirely — volatility/momentum is a required ingredient, not optional (2021-02-08; 2021-02-12).
- Avoids heavily-followed mega caps (e.g., Netflix) for EP-style trades because they're too "efficient" to produce genuine surprises; EPs work best on small/mid caps where news is a real surprise to the market (2021-02-09).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Frames trading success as almost entirely about discipline and focus, not intelligence — approaching markets "like a rocket scientist" only works if you actually are one; everyone else is better off with simple, repeatable setups (2021-02-10).
- Considers financial media, analyst price targets, and CNBC-style commentary to carry zero trading edge and tunes them out entirely; says analysts mostly follow price rather than lead it (2021-02-09).
- Says having a specific, well-defined setup with clear entry/exit/size rules largely eliminates "discipline problems" — no trading psychologist needed if the system is precise (2021-01-29, "Meme stonks").
- Emphasizes "listening" to price action rather than predicting it — "there's no money in prediction, there's a lot of money in listening" (2021-02-05).
- Treats 20%+ drawdowns as a normal, even necessary, cost of compounding aggressively — a trader with no real drawdowns probably isn't sizing up enough to capture outsized returns (2021-02-05).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Chasing a stock purely because it's already up thousands of percent and "everyone else" is excited is called out repeatedly as the core mistake behind retail losses in the GME/meme-stock mania (2021-01-28 through 2021-02-05, multiple streams).
- Warns that day trading doesn't scale the way swing/position trading does — even a highly skilled day trader is capped well below what a good swing trader can compound to (2021-02-10).
- Calls options and forex "loser's games" for most retail traders, saying he's never personally seen a forex trader or verified an options trader build lasting wealth that way, versus many profitable stock swing traders (2021-02-03; 2021-02-10).
- Avoids holding into an earnings catalyst without a real thesis/edge — treats that as pure gambling (2021-02-04, "Where is the line?").
- Flags that each successive "leg" of an already-extended move has progressively worse risk/reward — chasing a stock on its third leg higher is a much weaker trade than catching the first (2021-02-10, "Buy the lows and sell the top").

## Other Notable Lessons
- Sees short squeezes as inherently violent but short-lived, unlike a genuine multi-year winner — a stock up 25% in two weeks and a stock up 25% over five years are fundamentally different animals, and squeezes tend to mean-revert hard afterward (2021-01-28, "Cool $10M+ day today").
- Considers extended-hours trading access close to mandatory (to exit on overnight news) but says options or short-selling access isn't necessary to build wealth — a basic broker plus breakout/EP trading is enough (2021-02-03, "bounce in memestocks soon?").
- Notes that hiding order size on a large position produced visibly worse fills than showing size on the platforms/liquidity he trades (2021-01-28).
