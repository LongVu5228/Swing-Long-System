## Entries & Setups

- Primary long setup: buy earnings gap-ups (big EPS/revenue beats, raised guidance) coming out of a long base or multi-month sideways range, entered on the opening-range breakout — the 1-minute, 5-minute, or first-30-minute (60-min candle) high. (2019-10-02 "Least exposure this year," 2019-10-22 "Swing trades starting to work again," repeated across batch)
- The first flag/leg off a low tends to be the cleanest and most tradeable; the second breakout attempt in a short span is often a lower-quality "failure" setup. (2019-10-08 "Dead cat bounce or bottoming process?")
- Favorite mean-reversion pattern: short a stock after 3-8 straight up days into resistance, or buy one that's fallen hard for several days into support — waits for the actual reversal/weakness signal rather than anticipating it. (2019-10-21 "Finally getting some traction," 2019-10-25 "Gold and silver breaking out")
- Deeply beaten-down, near-bankrupt-looking stocks (fundamentals irrelevant) can produce the market's most explosive percentage moves once fear fades — he tracks a watchlist of these regardless of financials. (2019-10-18)
- Uses leveraged/triple ETFs (nugget, USLV, DRIP/DWT, PBR/EWC) to get outsized moves from small dollar positions since their ADR is several times the underlying's. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-08, 2019-10-24)

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- Trails positions with 10/20/50-day moving averages, scaling out roughly a third to a quarter of remaining shares on the first close below each. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-17, 2019-10-18)
- Watches VWAP as a key intraday pivot on extended momentum names — admits he doesn't fully understand why it works but trusts it. (2019-10-09, 2019-10-16)
- The more times a level (support or resistance) is retested, the higher the odds it eventually breaks. (2019-10-24)
- Checks market-breadth tools weekly (T2108, McClellan Oscillator, put/call ratio) to sense extremes and reduce/increase exposure accordingly. (2019-10-03)
- Distinguishes "trading stocks" from "investing stocks" — slow, steady compounders (Microsoft, O'Reilly) get skipped even with great fundamentals because they don't move fast enough. (2019-10-22, 2019-10-24)
- Judges liquidity by dollar volume, never share count — a low-priced high-share-volume stock can be far less liquid than a high-priced low-share-volume one. (2019-10-03)

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Typical position size is 5-20% of the account per name; overall exposure rarely exceeds ~150-200% net long even on margin. (2019-10-28, 2019-11-01)
- Enters with very tight "starter" stops specifically to generate huge risk multiples on winners — repeatedly cites being up 10-50x his initial dollar risk on trades like nugget, nut (VLD), and Roku. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-03, 2019-10-09)
- Is comfortable taking many small losses (five, ten, even fifty) because one outsized winner covers them all — "small losses, big wins" is a repeated mantra. (2019-10-04, 2019-10-10, 2019-10-28)
- Day trades need faster partial profit-taking (locking in at 1-3x risk) than swing trades because intraday reward potential is capped — this is why win rate needs to be higher for day trading than swing trading. (2019-10-03)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- Uses the day's low (or prior swing low) as the default stop about 95% of the time to avoid rationalizing a wider one. (2019-10-08, 2019-10-22)
- Keeps a "last-resort" stop that he honors no matter what — even if price would technically reclaim a moving average intraday, he still exits there because a stock can gap far through any level overnight. (2019-10-02)
- Prefers manual trailing stops over automated ones, since he trails using pivots/higher-lows/lower-highs rather than a fixed percentage. (2019-10-28)
- A drawdown that gives back recent gains feels psychologically very different (much easier) than a drawdown after already being flat/down for months — he treats these as distinct categories. (2019-10-02)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- Cuts exposure to almost nothing when the market is chopping sideways with no leadership, then re-levers aggressively (up to ~150-200%) once indices break out of a multi-month range — calls range-bound stretches "the easiest market ever, don't have to do anything." (2019-10-02, 2019-10-08, 2019-10-28, 2019-11-01)
- The biggest rallies historically start after a market decline, not during the first bounce off a low; he prefers buying the second or third leg down / an undercut of a prior low, since the very first bounce off a top is the hardest and most dangerous to buy. (2019-10-02)
- Leading growth stocks showing relative strength (holding up or basing) while the indices are still weak is an early tell that a market low may be forming. (2019-10-03, 2019-10-08)
- A one-to-two-year sideways base in the major indices, once resolved to the upside, has historically led to extended (year-plus) rallies — used as the basis for going aggressively long into Q4 2019. (2019-10-28, 2019-11-01)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- Screens primarily on revenue/EPS growth acceleration (via MarketSmith, fly-on-the-wall, briefing.com), not current profitability — many best winners are unprofitable but growing revenue triple digits. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-03, 2019-10-24)
- Maintains rotating "stronger stocks" watchlists plus scans requiring price above the prior day's high and up a set percentage of average daily range. (2019-10-03)
- Deliberately avoids biotech and random low-float "pump" names without an earnings or technical catalyst — needs an identifiable edge to take a trade. (2019-10-08, 2019-10-25)
- Maintains a personal multi-year "pattern database" (Evernote, tens of thousands of annotated charts) of past big movers to speed up recognition of similar setups. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-10)

## Psychology & Mindset

- Self-describes as a "chronic over-trader" who deliberately takes small, sub-optimal trades in small size just to scratch the itch — treats the resulting small losses as a "tax" that prevents larger, more damaging impulsive trades later. (2019-10-03, 2019-10-25)
- Argues emotions like FOMO should be accepted, not "managed away" — anyone claiming full emotional control is "full of it." (2019-10-25)
- Trading is ~90-95% boredom/waiting; getting excited is treated as a warning sign to size down, not up. (2019-10-04, 2019-10-08, 2019-10-25)
- Running a larger account reduces the pressure to force trades to pay bills, which itself improves decision quality — recommends new swing traders start with a meaningfully sized account for this reason. (2019-10-04)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- Covering a short position simply because "everyone else is covering" into a squeeze is called out as a classic way to lose money — being part of the herd late in a move is a losing habit even when in hindsight the setup still looked weak. (2019-10-16)
- Entering before a candle/opening range actually confirms ("shorting before the candle closed") repeatedly caused unnecessary stop-outs across multiple sessions. (2019-10-02, 2019-10-03, 2019-10-21)
- Forgetting to place stops during the chaotic first minutes after the open cost real money more than once — his own line was "I need four hands and two heads." (2019-10-24, 2019-10-28)
- Buying a large-cap stock on a 30-40%+ single-day gap is avoided — that much overnight move on a mega-cap has poor risk/reward and no historical edge. (2019-10-22)

## Other Notable Lessons

- Uses multiple brokers/clearing firms (Centerpoint Securities with Wedbush and Vision clearing) mainly to secure better short locates on runaway low-float names a few times a year. (2019-10-08)
- Avoids options entirely, viewing them as unnecessary complexity that erodes edge via time decay; standard margin leverage (about 4:1 intraday, 2:1 overnight) is "more than enough." (2019-10-04, 2019-10-09)
- States a personal benchmark that an active swing/day trader should aim for at least 100% account growth per year, and shares his own year-by-year returns (from triple digits early on down to double digits as account size grew into the millions). (2019-11-01)
