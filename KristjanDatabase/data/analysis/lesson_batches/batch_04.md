# Batch 04 — Kristjan Kullamägi Trading Lessons (2019-11 through 2020-01)

## Entries & Setups
- Core setup remains buying multi-week/multi-month range breakouts on volume; four out of five stocks just follow whatever the index/range is doing, so a range break in the market itself is a green light to add exposure broadly (2019-11-04, "Earnings, oil and China!").
- High-tight-flag stocks (already up huge %, consolidating tightly) are prime longs — several fuel-cell names (PLUG, FCEL, BLDP) and micro-cap "junk" stocks were played this way repeatedly through Nov–Dec (2019-11-25, "$250K in profits on $KRTX short!"; 2019-12-31, "$300K profit on $RAD short...").
- A classic day-trading reversal setup: a stock up several days in a row that goes green-to-red (or takes out opening-range lows) on the next session, especially into a 5th up day — repeated often as a reliable pattern (2019-11-06, "Earnings season"; 2019-11-15, "Last opportunities of the earnings season!").
- Never short a stock on day one of a big move — wait for it to become extended (up huge % over several sessions) before initiating shorts; that's "when amateurs get run over" (2019-11-29, "Markets are hot, sick opportunitues!").
- Beaten-down, near-bankruptcy stocks that announce restructuring, debt deals, or a partnership can bounce 100–300%+ off lows very fast (MNK, CHK examples) (2019-11-07, "Great earnings breakouts"; 2019-12-18, "Back at streaming!").
- For biotech, checks a catalyst calendar for upcoming FDA data before entering, since binary events can blow through any stop instantly (2019-11-08, "Slow earnings day!").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Always checks the weekly/monthly chart for the multi-year base context before trading a daily setup — a stock "surfing" its rising 10/20/50/200-day MA for a long stretch is a strong continuation signal (recurring, e.g., 2019-11-04, 2019-12-18 "Biotech MANIA?").
- A pullback to support that snaps back immediately (doesn't linger) is a sign of institutional buying; staying down at support is a warning sign (2019-11-12, "Almost end of earnings season").
- His "five-star setup" combo: big volume + good current earnings + good forward estimates + a clean chart pattern ("the perfect trifecta") (2019-11-06).
- Watches for stocks that "can't go down" — every dip of a couple percent finds buyers within the same session — as a sign of exceptional relative strength (2020-01-16, "$TSLA trades like a smallcap pump...").

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- "You should never have static risk... you always bet more on the stuff you like" — position size should scale with conviction, and conviction is built through years of experience and mistakes (2019-11-08).
- Target risk:reward of roughly 1:5 on swing entries when possible (2019-11-25).
- Rule of thumb for win rate: expect to pay for three or four losers with every big winner (2019-11-25).
- Avoids oversized shorts in low-priced stocks specifically because cheap stocks can double or triple even after already doubling — the math of percentage moves works against a short (2019-11-07).
- Treats ~10–15 open positions as comfortable; 25–30+ positions has repeatedly preceded a market pullback for him and adds real stress/confusion (2020-01-02, "New yeart starts..."; 2020-01-09, "Resilient markets").
- Shorting illiquid/expensive-to-borrow names carries real tail risk — described a trader stuck short a delisted stock for two years paying borrow fees the whole time; prefers liquid large caps (e.g., TSLA) for the short side precisely to avoid that (2020-01-14, "Almost $150K in profits out of the gate!").

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Adds to winners in tranches as they clear resistance/build higher lows, using the new higher low as the stop for each add (recurring throughout).
- Raises stops progressively toward breakeven as a position works, then keeps trailing higher — described as "training my stops" (2019-11-04, 2019-12-31).
- Standard trim rule: sell a chunk (often a third) when a stock closes below its rising 10-day or 20-day moving average (2019-11-07, 2019-11-12).
- Will occasionally hold a long and short of the same stock simultaneously across different accounts to play opposing intraday setups (2020-01-06, "$SAVA long up 50% in a few hours!").
- On extremely fast/violent candles, prefers scaling out in small clips (e.g., every 5–10 cents) rather than dumping the whole position at once, to avoid selling exactly at a shakeout low.

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Told a cautionary story of giving back roughly $200K of unrealized profit on FCEL in about 15 minutes by not watching the chart closely enough into an earnings reaction — lesson: in fast markets, actively defend big winners rather than letting them run unattended (2020-01-22, "I gave back $200K in profits on $FCEL in 15 minutes...").
- General profit-taking is "feel," not exact science — lock in more (a quarter to a third) after a losing streak or when nervous, hold longer when conviction and market strength are both high (2019-11-25).
- On squeeze/short trades (KRTX, CLVS, RAD) repeatedly took partial profits on the way down rather than trying to nail the exact bottom, explicitly to avoid repeating a past mistake of over-holding and giving back hundreds of thousands in profit (2019-11-25, 2019-12-31).
- Avoids holding expensive-borrow shorts over a weekend when possible to dodge weekend financing/borrow risk (2019-11-29).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Calls a clean, low-volatility, steadily-trending market "the best time to be a swing and position trader," citing 2017 and early 2018 as his best comparable stretches, with Nov 2019–Jan 2020 ranking as the second- or third-best environment he's traded since 2011 (2019-11-04, 2020-01-06, 2020-01-09).
- Uses his own position count/leverage as an informal sentiment gauge — loads up aggressively (margin, many positions) once convinced of a new bull leg via an index range break, and stays in only a handful of names when the market is range-bound (2019-11-04, "I was using some margin... right now I have 14 positions").
- Watches breadth across sectors (biotech, small caps/Russell, China, gold/silver, energy, fuel cells) turning up together as evidence that "speculation money" is broadening into risk assets (2019-11-11, "Earnings season almost over!"; 2020-01-02).
- When markets get extended and his long watchlist is empty while the short watchlist is full, treats that as a signal to stop initiating new longs and get more selective/defensive (2020-01-14).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Screens for accelerating revenue growth plus strong forward EPS/revenue estimates layered on a clean chart; explicitly avoids slow, low-growth "safe" names (used Kroger as the example of a stock with nothing to offer despite a decent-looking chart) (2019-11-06).
- Distinguishes "trading stocks" (fast movers with real volatility, e.g., SDN Baba/ecommerce China names) from "investing stocks" (mega caps like Netflix/Disney that move too slowly to swing) (2019-11-08, 2019-12-18).
- Treats emerging markets/China ETFs and weekly/monthly multi-year range breaks (EEM, FXI-style) as longer-term thematic buy signals distinct from his usual swing setups (2019-11-11).
- Passes on stocks that are technically excellent but too thin to build a meaningful position in, repeatedly, even when the setup looks "five-star" (recurring theme across nearly every session).

## Psychology & Mindset
- "It's 99% about waiting and letting things work for you... one of the biggest mistakes traders make is trying too hard" (2019-11-04).
- Deliberately plays video games during slow market stretches specifically to avoid the temptation to overtrade out of boredom (recurring throughout the batch).
- Describes blowing up his account three or four times in his first two years of trading (2011–2012) before stabilizing, and says it took about two years of full-time screen time to "do more of what worked and less of what didn't" (2019-11-11).
- Says he would "rather hang myself" than go back to day trading now that his account is large — day trading is a tool for growing a small account fast, not worth the time cost afterward (2019-11-11).
- Emphasizes exposing yourself to many traders/mentors early in your career, then discarding what doesn't fit your own style once you've absorbed enough (2019-11-11).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Being an early short on a stock that hasn't gone parabolic yet — wait for exhaustion (2019-11-29).
- Getting complacent/distracted during a huge favorable move, which directly caused the $200K FCEL giveback (2020-01-22).
- Sizing shorts too large in cheap stocks given how easily they can multiply against you (2019-11-07).
- Avoid options entirely with a small account, or as a trader who isn't already large and experienced — it adds more variables working against you on top of an already-hard game (2020-01-07, "$SAVA what a beast long and a beast short!").
- Chasing thin/illiquid recent IPOs beyond what position size the liquidity can actually support.

## Other Notable Lessons
- Uses a free site (referred to as "coif"/similar) for multi-year forward revenue estimate snapshots, and TC2000 for scanning and alerts despite its free-tier alerts running 10–20 minutes delayed (2020-01-08, "What is the price action telling us despite recent news?").
- Explains earnings-season calendar structure — the January season is the longest (~7–8 weeks) versus ~5 weeks for other quarters — and adjusts his daily prep/wake time accordingly during peak weeks (2019-11-11).
- Explains the mechanics and risk of hard-to-borrow shorts (locate costs, being stuck short a stock for months or years paying financing fees) as the reason he prefers liquid, easy-to-borrow large caps for shorting over speculative micro-caps (2020-01-14).
