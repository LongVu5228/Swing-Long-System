# Kristjan Kullamägi — Lessons Batch (2021-05-24 to 2021-06-16)

## Entries & Setups
- Core "high tide flag" pattern: an explosive prior move (up 50–300%+ in days/weeks), an orderly linear pullback finding support on the rising 10- or 20-day MA, a tightening range, then a volume breakout. Repeated constantly as the model setup (2021-06-03/09/15/16, multiple videos).
- Opening-range highs (1-min/5-min) are the trigger for breakouts and episodic pivots (EPs), but are only meaningful once a stock is actually breaking a range or is an EP — watching opening range highs on a range-bound stock is a waste of time (2021-06-10).
- Best EPs show massive volume in the first minutes (multiples of average daily volume), gap over and hold key moving averages rather than get rejected at them, and ideally break to new highs or out of a multi-month range — not ones stuck below declining MAs (2021-06-10, 06-11).
- A "pocket pivot" is a breakout inside a bigger range: a stock with prior momentum pulls to a major MA, builds a small range, then breaks that range on higher volume — considered a setup for slower-moving/larger stocks (2021-05-26).
- Never anticipate a breakout before price actually clears the prior range/candle high — compared to picking a marathon runner before the race starts rather than waiting to see who's actually running well (2021-06-16, 06-15 struggling-trader reviews).
- Never short a stock on "day one" of its initial pop; wait at least for day two (2021-06-02).
- Small/tiny accounts have a structural edge: high-ADR micro/small caps can double, triple, or quadruple in days — moves far bigger than what's available in mega-caps — so a small account is an edge, not a handicap (2021-06-02, 06-03, 06-15).
- Recent IPOs and IPO breakouts are favored setups — low float, exciting/new themes, and institutions not yet fully positioned can produce outsized moves (2021-06-09, 06-15).
- Nano/micro caps tend to find support on the rising 10-day; larger, slower stocks find support on the 20-day, and even slower ones on the 50-day (2021-05-25, 06-11).
- Getting stopped out doesn't invalidate a good setup — re-enter if the setup is still intact; this can happen 2-3 times before it works (2021-06-02).
- A valid base should be drawable as a simple triangle (VCP/tightening range); if you can't draw one, there's no real setup (2021-06-16).

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Focus on daily charts and 1/3/6-month momentum; weekly charts are irrelevant until an account reaches roughly $10M+ (2021-06-16).
- On extreme momentum stocks the 60-minute chart can reveal a cleaner setup than the daily (2021-06-15, 06-16).
- Leveraged/triple ETFs (TNA, SOXL, NRGU, BRZU, etc.) often show cleaner technical patterns and more effective liquidity than individual sector stocks, and let you express a sector view with size without single-name risk (2021-05-25, 06-01, 06-09).
- Compare ADR before choosing a vehicle — triple-leveraged sector ETFs can have 2-3x the ADR of the underlying, single-sector ETF, meaning bigger moves for the same directional bet (2021-06-01).
- Don't analyze moving averages directly on triple-leveraged ETFs (e.g. TNA, SOXL) — look at the underlying index/sector chart instead (2021-05-25).
- Level 2 is largely irrelevant except on micro-cap/OTC/pink-sheet stocks; on liquid stocks and ETFs it's misleading, and real size can far exceed what Level 2 depth implies (2021-06-01).
- Fundamentals/story matter progressively more the longer the intended holding period; for short-term trading, momentum and where money is flowing are what matter — "story" (theme/narrative), not balance-sheet detail, is what counts even when fundamentals matter (2021-06-01).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Position size can be derived two ways: let your risk tolerance (stop distance) determine share size, or let your desired size determine your dollar risk (2021-06-04).
- The standard 1% risk rule is calculated against total account size, not against the stock's price or a per-share basis (2021-06-10).
- Buy full intended size at entry rather than scaling in — averaging in "ruins your average" and undermines the ability to capture 10–50x reward-to-risk trades; let the position ride instead (2021-06-04).
- ETFs are framed as inherently "safer" than single stocks since they're a basket, reducing single-name blowup risk (2021-06-04).
- Excessive leverage is repeatedly cited (referencing 1929 and crypto blowups) as the recurring cause of trader wipeouts; leverage should be used sparingly even when available (2021-05-25).
- 20-30% drawdowns are described as an expected feature of any style chasing explosive returns, not a flaw — bigger gains inherently come with bigger swings, and 3-6 month drawdowns occur most years even for a profitable trader (2021-06-11, 05-24).
- The idea that day trading carries smaller drawdowns/less risk than swing trading is called a myth — his own historical day-trading drawdowns were larger than his swing-trading ones (2021-05-24).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard trim/trail process: sell a third to half of a winning position after 3-5 days, then trail the remainder using the 10-day (or 20-day, for larger/slower names) moving average as the stop (2021-06-15, 06-16).
- A close below the 10-day (or 20-day) MA is the standard signal to cut/reduce a trailing position (2021-06-11, 06-14).
- Hesitating to pay up a small amount (e.g., ~30 cents) to add to a strong breakout cost several million dollars in missed profit on one trade — a reminder that on high-ADR names, a small chase is immaterial relative to the potential move (2021-06-03, 06-09).
- Losing today's intraday lows is treated as a standard exit trigger for a long position, and the reverse (losing VWAP or a 5-minute low/high) is used as precise intraday entry/exit timing (2021-06-03, 06-04).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Selling aggressively into strength suits traders who prioritize consistent cash flow over maximizing total account growth; holding for the biggest possible move outperforms in strong trending markets but underperforms in choppy ones — it's a style trade-off, not a universal rule (2021-06-01).
- Don't try to pick tops or bottoms — wait for the stock/index to "prove itself" via confirmation signals (reclaiming/losing VWAP, breaking a prior 5-minute candle) rather than guessing (2021-06-04, 06-03).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- After a historic rally, expect weeks-to-months of sideways digestion so that 10/20/50-day (and weekly) moving averages can catch up before the next leg higher is likely (2021-05-24, 06-01, 06-04).
- Breakouts across the board work far better once major indices emerge from a multi-month/multi-quarter consolidation than while still range-bound; historically the best Nasdaq entries came right after such consolidations resolved (2021-06-10).
- Fed/macro headlines themselves don't matter for short-term trading — only the market's/price reaction (where money actually flows afterward) matters (2021-06-16).
- Late-stage speculative manias show random low-quality stocks suddenly doubling/tripling overnight and money diluting into many copycat/"tertiary" names instead of concentrating in the leader — treated as a tell the move is near exhaustion (2021-06-09, 06-10).
- Being willing to change your mind as facts change (cited via Druckenmiller) is framed as a defining trait of great macro/momentum traders (2021-06-01, 06-03).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Prioritize high-ADR names; low-ADR stocks are considered a poor use of time for compounding an account quickly, regardless of setup quality (2021-06-01, 06-02).
- Recent big, clean, linear momentum (up 50%+ in days/weeks) is the key predictor of which stocks can keep making outsized moves — not market cap (2021-06-11, 06-15/16 reviews).
- Liquidity (dollar volume), not market cap, should determine tradability — mid/small caps can be more liquid than mega-caps during a mania (2021-06-02).
- Avoid random, choppy, low-momentum micro caps with no clean trend or institutional support just because they're cheap or scanner-flagged (2021-06-16, 06-15).
- When a theme has several copycat names running, concentrate research/capital in the single strongest leader rather than spreading across many (2021-06-04, 06-14).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Most underperformance is attributed to lack of confidence, not lack of discipline — confidence is built only by studying hundreds/thousands of historical examples of a specific setup (2021-06-14).
- Trading is framed as more about "unlearning" false market assumptions than acquiring new information (2021-06-03).
- Emotions (fear, excitement, anger) are normal even for top traders; what matters is not letting emotion dictate deviation from the system (2021-06-01).
- It typically takes an account blowup or "rock bottom" moment before a trader gets serious enough to put in real study (2021-06-14).
- Deliberately doing things that feel uncomfortable but have a proven statistical edge — e.g., buying a stock already up hundreds of percent — is described as a skill that must be consciously trained (2021-06-03).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Anticipating/front-running breakouts before the range actually breaks is called out repeatedly as a top cause of losses in trade reviews (2021-06-15, 06-16).
- Chasing wide, loose, choppy ("barcode") charts with no clean trend or defined range (2021-06-15, 06-16).
- Buying broken, beaten-down micro caps below their 20-day MA with no institutional support, hoping for a bounce with no real edge (2021-06-16).
- Blaming "day traders"/algos for routine stop-outs instead of accepting that stocks sometimes just have downticks (2021-06-11, 06-15).
- Following commentators/newsletter writers who post trade ideas but trade tiny size themselves — better to study traders with demonstrable large size and long track records (2021-06-03).
- Needing a calculator to compute position size/risk in the moment is treated as a sign of not being ready to trade real size (2021-06-04).

## Other Notable Lessons
- Fundamental/valuation-based objections (e.g., to meme stocks) are dismissed for short-term trading — "the technicals don't lie" and price action should be trusted over a personal narrative about a company's true worth (2021-06-01, 06-02).
- Fundamental analysis is called "almost useless" for very short-term trading; the more important skill is reading order flow / where money is moving (2021-06-01, 06-03).
