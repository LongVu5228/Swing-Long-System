## Entries & Setups
- Core recurring pattern: a trending stock pulls back, builds a range with higher lows, tightens up, and breaks out once the 20-day (or relevant) moving average catches up to price — buy exactly as the breakout starts, not once it's obvious. (2020-10-02, "Leaders still leading?"; repeated 2020-10-06, 2020-10-09, 2020-10-16)
- "If it isn't tight, it's not right" — avoid loose, wide-ranging bases; the tightest setups (surfing a rising MA) offer the real edge. (2020-10-07; 2020-10-13, "So many strong sectors")
- Continuation setups (buying well after the primary breakout) have much worse odds than the primary breakout itself; treat them only as possible add spots, not new entries. (2020-10-12, "Now is the time to sit and profit")
- Opening-range breaks (1-min/5-min) are the standard precise entry mechanic for both day trades and swing entries, long or short. (2020-10-06 x2; 2020-10-08, "Possible short setups")
- Episodic pivots (huge gaps on earnings/news beats) are a distinct tradeable setup that can be day traded, swing traded, or even position traded off the opening-range high. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video"; 2020-10-22, "Leading stocks breaking down!")
- Don't fear a pullback right after entry — judge the stock by whether it builds higher lows afterward; that's normal, healthy digestion. (2020-10-06, "Strength into the elections?")
- For shorts: wait for confirmation (first red 1-min/5-min candle, a VWAP rejection after a big run, or an opening-range-low break) and always use a hard stop — never anticipate a breakdown. (2020-10-08, 2020-10-09)
- Extended stocks that "can't dip" are often the best future longs once they finally set up. (2020-10-09, "Now is the time"; 2020-10-19, "Solar stocks still the strongest")
- Buying in anticipation of a breakout wastes capital as "dead money" and risks a reversal before it triggers; wait for the stock to prove itself. (2020-10-09, "A lot of wisdom today")

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Core toolkit is the 10/20/50/100/200-day moving averages; fast/high-ADR stocks tend to find support on the 10-day, slower names on the 20th or 50th day. (2020-10-02; 2020-10-16, "Solar stocks leading")
- The 60-minute chart with matching short EMAs is used heavily for precise intraday entries, exits, and reading structure. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-09)
- "Resistance" from old volume shelves is largely imaginary for genuine leaders — cites Netflix and Apple both blowing through supposed resistance zones repeatedly; only key moving-average support matters for real leaders. (2020-10-12)
- Simple way to measure momentum: scan for the biggest percentage gainers over 1/3/6 months with a liquidity/ADR filter — no fancier indicator needed. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video")
- Many leading stocks breaking major moving averages at the same time is a market-wide warning sign, distinct from one stock weakening in isolation. (2020-10-22)
- A rising market gapping down into support is generally a bad time to initiate new shorts — odds favor a bounce. (2020-10-16)
- A failed breakdown through a key moving average followed by an immediate strong reclaim is a high-quality bullish tell. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video")

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Never run more than roughly 100–130% margin in one net direction; 200%+ is called reckless and something he'd never do. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video")
- Margin is "deserved, not entitled" — only lever up meaningfully after being up big for the year; increase size as results improve and cut size in drawdowns, never the reverse. (2020-10-06)
- Cap any single stock at roughly 25% of account equity. (2020-10-09)
- Minimum acceptable swing-trade risk/reward is about 1:10. (2020-10-06, "Strength into the elections?")
- Micro-caps let you risk ~10% for a 100%+ move; large caps risk ~2% for a ~20% move — the risk/reward ratio ends up similar, just wider stops on smaller names. (2020-10-06)
- Spread margin across many names (10–15+) instead of concentrating leverage in a few stocks — no need to go max size in only two or three names when a bull market lifts so many. (2020-10-06)
- Limit how much total exposure changes in a single day (e.g., no more than ~50–80 percentage points of invested capital) even in a hot breakout environment. (2020-10-06)
- Edge + risk management beats intelligence; you don't need to be a genius, just "not stupid" — overconfident "stupid smart" traders blow up because they think they can't lose. (2020-10-06)
- A 25–30% win rate is fine as long as losers are cut small and winners run 10–50x the initial risk. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-09)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard sell rule: after 3–5 days, sell a third to half into strength, move the stop to breakeven, then trail the rest with the 10-day MA (or 20-day for slower/lower-ADR names). (2020-10-06 multiple videos)
- Sell in small pieces (roughly 3–5% at a time) into strength rather than dumping a big chunk at once, scaling frequency to the stock's ADR. (2020-10-07)
- Only add to a position when the add setup itself is strong and you aren't already at full size; otherwise a continuation move is watch-only. (2020-10-12, 2020-10-13)
- A close below the key trailing moving average is an exit trigger regardless of story or recent news. (2020-10-07, 2020-10-14)
- When many leading stocks break key averages simultaneously, it's fine to override normal sell rules and play defense immediately rather than waiting for the exact trigger. (2020-10-22, "Leading stocks breaking down!")
- If a position is still red 3–5 days after entry, consider selling and rotating into a stronger setup rather than waiting it out. (2020-10-22)
- Never rebuy a stopped-out stock just because you liked it — wait for a fresh, valid setup. (2020-10-09)
- Avoid holding extended, high-conviction winners into earnings without a profit cushion; one weak guidance line can erase 20–30% overnight. (2020-10-12)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Selling is far harder than entering — entries are "the easy part," which is why mechanical, simple sell rules matter more than clever ones. (2020-10-07, 2020-10-21)
- On very high-ADR names, take profits in smaller, more frequent slices since single moves are larger; slower names get fewer, larger trims. (2020-10-07)
- When a stock gets extremely extended above its 10-day MA (50–100%+), there's a real tension between fear and greed, and eventually you must sell some without waiting for a formal signal. (2020-10-07)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- A healthy bull market makes higher lows on every dip; when rallies instead get sold into lower highs, that's the signature of a weakening market. (2020-10-27, "Mild bull market pullback")
- The clearest tell of genuine strength is your portfolio green while the index is red/flat — that's how you know you're in real leaders. (2020-10-02; 2020-10-06, "Strength into the elections?")
- Conversely, leading stocks red while the index is flat/green is a real warning sign, worse than the index reading itself. (2020-10-22)
- Down/red days are when relative strength is easiest to spot — "down days are where your profits are made." (2020-10-08, "Possible short setups"; 2020-10-12)
- Ignore news, politics, and macro headlines entirely — none of it has predictive value; only price action and setups matter. (recurring theme across nearly every session — 2020-10-02, 2020-10-07, 2020-10-16, 2020-10-19, 2020-10-27)
- After a market runs hard for 2–3 weeks, expect a pause while extended stocks let their moving averages catch up; this is normal digestion, not a top. (2020-10-14, 2020-10-19)
- Patience during choppy or "good setups but no follow-through" markets is a core skill — "those who win have the best ability to wait." When there are no good setups, do less or nothing. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-19, 2020-10-23)
- A market with many decent-looking setups that simply fail to follow through is worse/harder than an obvious downtrend, which is easy to just sit out. (2020-10-21, "Snapchat great report!")

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Hard filters before anything goes on the watchlist: roughly $10–15M+ daily dollar volume and ADR above ~5%; thin or slow stocks aren't worth the time regardless of pattern. (2020-10-02, 2020-10-06, 2020-10-14, 2020-10-16)
- Focus exclusively on momentum leaders (biggest % gainers over 1/3/6 months); sector or story is irrelevant if the stock is among the strongest. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video"; 2020-10-22)
- Fundamentals used are simple: fast (often triple-digit) revenue/earnings growth plus strong price momentum; deep fundamental digging (SEC filings, etc.) isn't required to make large returns. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-14)
- Instrument/sector doesn't matter — the same setups are traded across stocks, crypto proxies, gold/silver, and commodities. (2020-10-02)
- Small accounts have a structural edge over large ones because they can concentrate in the fastest, highest-ADR micro/small caps a bigger account can't size into. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-08)

## Psychology & Mindset
- "You are your own worst enemy" — most losses trace back to emotional reactions to news or tweets, not bad setups. (2020-10-02, 2020-10-07, 2020-10-13)
- Sitting out and doing nothing is a trading "superpower" (echoing Minervini/Sanger) — emotionally hard but essential when conditions aren't favorable. (2020-10-02)
- Don't punish yourself for a missed trade — there's always another opportunity in a bull market; dwelling on one miss leads to revenge trading. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-08, 2020-10-09)
- Trading is one of the hardest endeavors (roughly 99% failure rate); keeping the process simple is what lets a trader survive long enough to get good. (2020-10-06)
- The loudest traders on social media tend to make the least money — a general, not absolute, rule for filtering signal from noise. (2020-10-06)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Chasing a stock already up big intraday instead of waiting for a proper low-risk entry, which skews risk/reward and forces a wider stop. (2020-10-19)
- Trading illiquid or low-ADR names that don't meet the liquidity/speed filter, no matter how good the pattern looks. (2020-10-02, 2020-10-13, 2020-10-14, 2020-10-16)
- Blaming short sellers or "manipulation" for a losing trade instead of taking responsibility. (2020-10-14, "Potential High Tight Flags")
- Hesitating on short entries — most short setups trigger and reverse within minutes, so slowness turns a good thesis into a loss. (2020-10-08, 2020-10-21, 2020-10-22)
- Adding complexity (more indicators, more tracked data points) tends to reduce profitability rather than improve it; keep the trade tracker and toolkit simple. (2020-10-06)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Recommends the "Market Wizards" book series and studying William O'Neil (whose setups are described as the same patterns, adapted to weekly charts) as near-required reading. (2020-10-06, "This is yesterdays video")
- Micro-cap setups are identical to large-cap setups; the only real adjustment is using faster moving averages (leaning on the 10-day) since micro-caps move faster and build shorter bases. (2020-10-06)
- Building a personal "pattern database" of thousands of historical winning and short setups, studied over hundreds/thousands of hours, is presented as the real path to recognizing quality setups instinctively. (2020-10-06, 2020-10-09)
