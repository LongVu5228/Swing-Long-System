## Entries & Setups

- Favorite long setup: a big prior move, then sideways consolidation ("flag") into a volume-expansion breakout, ideally after the 20-day (or rising 50/100-day) moving average has "caught up" to price rather than chasing immediately. (2017-01-22, "Watchlist 23 january 2017"; 2017-01-24, "Trading Recap and Watches for Tuesday Jan 24")
- Recent IPOs are a favorite category because technical analysis "works very well" on them — no multi-year overhead resistance or baggage. (2017-01-24; 2017-02-19, "Friday Recap and My Worst Box Attempt")
- On strongly-trending stocks, the first test of a rising 50-day (and later the 150-day) moving average tends to produce a clean, tradeable bounce; he found the 200-day works less reliably than the 150-day for this. (2017-02-28, "Biotechs! and Watches for Tuesday February 28")
- Mid-day higher lows are often a better entry window than the first minutes of trading, which are prone to noise/chop. (2017-02-08, "Recap and watches for Feb 8")
- "Sell the news" pattern: stocks that ran up on anticipation of a hyped catalyst (AMD's processor launch, a pre-announced earnings beat) often reverse right when the news actually lands. (2017-02-09; 2017-02-23, "$CNBX death and Watches for Thursday February 23")

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis

- Tight, narrowing range candles right before a breakout are a strong "tell" for a high-quality setup; loose, wide candles going into a breakout are a lower-quality signal. (2017-01-22; 2017-02-11, "Friday recap and watches for Monday Feb 13" — Trice example)
- Higher-lows on the 60-minute chart signal quiet accumulation ahead of a move. (recurring throughout)
- Being technically "extended" isn't itself a reason to fade a stock — names like NVDA, SKX, and ASIA kept running well past prior earnings gaps in strong markets; the key is finding a tight, low-risk entry within the extension, not avoiding the stock. (2017-02-16, "Wednesday Recap and Watches for Thursday February 16")
- Separates "real" institutionally-owned stocks from purely promotional micro-caps — the former hold up and bounce differently, and shouldn't be treated with the same short-thesis patience as garbage names. (2017-02-12, "$71K in profits and 41% win rate for January - Review of mistakes made")
- When headline indices make new highs but small caps/biotechs lag underneath, treat that divergence as a caution flag for new long breakouts. (2017-02-24; 2017-03-01, "Tuesday Recap and Watches for Wednesday March 1")

## Position Sizing & Risk Management

- Core philosophy, repeated constantly: risk small to make many multiples of that risk (targets of 5–13x initial risk cited on multiple trades). (2017-02-08; 2017-02-17, "Thursday Recap and Watches for Friday February 17")
- Cut size sharply on illiquid/thin names since fills are unreliable regardless of how good the chart looks; after a 20,000-share mistake in one thin name, capped himself at 10,000 shares going forward. (2017-01-24; 2017-02-03, "Recap and watches for Feb 3")
- "Boxing" (holding offsetting long and short positions in the same name across accounts overnight) is dangerous — partial boxes force bad-price covers when the stock keeps moving; better to either fully box/unbox or avoid it. (2017-02-22, "Unboxed part of $CNBX squeezing me..."; 2017-02-23)
- Keeps both longs and shorts open simultaneously as a hedge, especially when many positions in the market look stretched — avoids getting maximally biased in one direction. (2017-02-26, "Friday Recap and Watches for Monday February 27")

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)

- Standard stops: prior day's close, opening-range low/high, or the most recent swing low, tightened and trailed up as new higher lows print.
- Self-identified mistake: tightening a stop emotionally when a stock pulled back into its base, even though the actual technical stop (a recent higher low) hadn't been violated — this shook him out right before a huge gap up the next session. (2017-02-12)
- On momentum-stock shorts, cut size by half or more once price takes out the range to the upside, and always carry a hard stop instead of "riding it out." Broke this rule once on a low-float runner by chasing into strength on day one — a mistake he says he "usually never" makes — and it turned a should-have-been-small loss into an unnecessarily large one. (2017-02-12)
- Adds to winners on confirmed strength (breakout above range) rather than only entering once. (recurring, e.g., 2017-02-01, "Recap and watches for Feb 2")

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy

- Scales out gradually into strength on multi-day swings, continually raising the stop as shares are trimmed, rather than selling all at once. (recurring across nearly every video, e.g. NAK, weeks positions)
- Sets price targets off prior resistance, round numbers, or moving-average confluence and takes partial profits at each level.
- Reduces or exits size ahead of binary catalysts (earnings, trial data) unless conviction is unusually high, since gap risk cuts both directions. (2017-02-08)

## Market Timing & Regime Reading

- When the broader market itself is basing sideways, individual stocks tend to build flags too — expect a cluster of breakouts once the index resolves upward. (2017-02-03)
- Long stretches of low volatility raise the odds of an eventual violent reversion; treats instruments like UVXY as a standing watch item after prolonged calm. (2017-01-31/2017-02-01)
- Deliberately keeps "one foot out the door" when many names are simultaneously extended, rather than getting fully committed long or short — follows price action over a fixed thesis. (2017-02-16; 2017-02-22)
- Sector/group sympathy trades: when a sector leader (gold miners, cannabis OTCs, shippers) makes a big move, laggards in the same group tend to follow — worth tracking as a group. (recurring throughout)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria

- Favorite category: heavily beaten-down, heavily-shorted stocks (high "days to cover") that get a positive catalyst, since a lack of natural sellers plus forced short-covering can produce outsized squeezes. (2017-02-22 through 2017-02-28)
- Avoids trading OTC "pump" names with real size due to unreliable, wide fills — even though percentage moves can be enormous. (2017-02-19)
- Within a hot group of similar small-caps, prioritizes the one showing relative strength over the first name noticed. (2017-02-07, "Recap and watches for Feb 7")

## Psychology & Mindset

- Explicitly warns viewers never to buy/short a stock purely because he said it "has potential" — entry price and risk matter as much as the thesis; someone who bought 20% above his pass-level got burned. (2017-02-17)
- Names "fear of missing out" as a real bias he manages by sizing down rather than avoiding a trade entirely. (2017-01-24)
- Identifies stubbornness — refusing to honor a stop once emotionally invested — as a recurring, costly flaw. (2017-02-12; 2017-03-02, "Wednesday Recap and Watches for Thursday March 2")
- Notes that being overly patient/greedy on shorts of fundamentally solid ("real") companies, expecting outsized follow-through like on garbage micro-caps, repeatedly turned winners into breakeven trades — a distinct failure mode from being early. (2017-02-12)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do

- Repeatedly tried to anticipate breakdowns in strong market leaders (Goldman Sachs, AMD, Chipotle) using a trendline thesis before price actually broke it, taking several small losses before finally being right — net result barely recovered the prior losses. (2017-02-12)
- Missed clean, pre-announced-earnings setups due to hesitation, calling himself "asleep at the wheel." (2017-02-24)
- Refused to pay up even a penny to get filled and missed a 300%+ move as a result. (2017-02-23)
- Got "boxed" long and short the same stock and only partially unboxed it, locking in poor economics regardless of direction — estimates this and the missed-fill mistake above cost roughly $50,000 combined that month. (2017-02-22; 2017-02-23)

## Other Notable Lessons

- A fundamental catalyst (loosened regulatory comments, M&A rumors, short-squeeze dynamics) can elevate a purely technical setup into a much higher-conviction trade. (2017-02-01, "Recap and watches for Feb 2")
- Flags that mid-video account P&L screenshots can be misleading since they sometimes reflect the prior day's closing price rather than true realized average cost — a recurring caveat about his own broker screenshots. (2017-01-22 through 2017-02-04)
