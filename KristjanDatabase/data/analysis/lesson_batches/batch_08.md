# Batch 08 — Kristjan Kullamägi Trading Lessons (2020-05-11 to 2020-06-08)

## Entries & Setups
- His "best swing trading setup": stock beats EPS/revenue estimates and raises guidance, gaps up 10%+ (ideally 15-20%+) on volume, and trades its entire average daily volume within the first 5-15 minutes, breaking out of a multi-month (3-6mo+) basing range. Cited repeatedly as the single highest-probability setup in the market (Shake Shack, T-Doc, DXCM, Fastly, ZS, Livongo). (2020-05-27 "My setups, methodology...", 2020-06-03 "Market is getting too easy")
- Second core setup: flag/range breakout on a momentum leader — a stock shows relative strength in a market selloff, pulls back to "surf" its rising 10- or 20-day moving average for days/weeks in a tightening flag, then breaks out on above-average volume (SC, PDD, Tesla, Livongo). (2020-05-27)
- Parabolic short (mean-reversion): stock up 200-1000%+ in a short span; never short day 1-2, wait for day 3+ when weakness first appears (failed opening range, failed VWAP reclaim), then enter. Parabolic long is the mirror: a washed-out stock reclaiming VWAP and opening-range highs after a violent decline. (2020-05-27)
- IPO breakouts out of multi-month sideways bases can be extremely powerful and are a distinct setup he actively hunts for (Livongo, Fastly). (2020-05-27)
- Best day-trading setup is the intraday version of opening-range breakout: buy the 1-min, 5-min, or 60-min opening-range high/low depending on which timeframe shows the clearest volume confirmation. (2020-06-02 "Best market ever", 2020-06-03)
- Calls the "third/fourth-day reversal on an already-extended stock" one of the most profitable, repeatable patterns in the market, used for both shorts and (inverted) longs. (2020-05-21 "Will my value investments...")

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- VWAP is used mainly on the short side (or on bounce plays): failing to reclaim VWAP is a short trigger; reclaiming and holding above it is a long/bounce trigger. (2020-05-27)
- Relative strength/weakness only means something across many days or against a moving market (e.g. a stock gapping down while the market gaps up); in the middle of a range it's noise. (2020-05-20 "If Twitter says MARK is worth $16...")
- Trails stops with a moving average matched to the stock's speed — 10-day for fast/extended movers, 20-day for normal swings, 50-day for slower names; very fast micro/small caps need something faster than even the 10-day, sometimes just the day's low. (2020-06-08 "Never in 9 years...", 2020-05-20)
- Says roughly 90-95% of technical analysis/indicators are "total garbage" — doesn't think in cup-and-handle or candlestick-pattern terms, just range tightness, volume, and a couple of moving averages. (2020-06-02)
- Rejects the idea of round-number "resistance" on a trending breakout stock — a strong stock goes wherever it goes; he just sells into strength and trails the rest instead of pre-guessing a ceiling. (2020-06-03, 2020-06-08)
- Uses weekly charts for context on longer earnings/range breakouts and thinks most traders would do better relying mainly on weekly + daily charts instead of over-trading intraday noise. (2020-05-21)
- Level 2/time-and-sales has no edge on the liquid momentum names he trades; only useful on illiquid micro-caps with one dominant participant. (2020-05-13 "Finally about to get a base building period")

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Rarely holds more than 20-25% of the account overnight in one name; can flex to ~30-50% intraday. (2020-06-02, 2020-06-04 "THE best daytrading setup")
- Newer traders should risk a consistent dollar amount per trade; experienced traders should size up meaningfully on rarer, higher-conviction setups ("once-a-week" vs. "once-a-year" opportunities) and size down on lower-conviction ones — this judgment only comes with years of screen time. (2020-06-02)
- Consistency in stop placement matters as much as the level itself: always low-of-day for longs bought on opening-range highs, high-of-day for shorts on opening-range lows. Changing the rule trade-to-trade breeds self-doubt and worse decisions. (2020-06-02)
- Mostly uses mental stops; only places hard stop orders when price is near entry/exit. Got burned by this once on GBTC (no hard stop during a violent gap cost an extra ~$15K he could have avoided). (2020-06-02, 2020-06-08)
- Keeps trading and short-selling on physically separate brokers/computers so a single platform outage never leaves both sides of the book unmanageable — happened literally during this batch (2020-05-29 "ZS new earnings trade").
- Avoids halt-risk, hard-to-borrow, or fraud-flagged names for size even when the chart looks tempting — the tail risk isn't worth it. (2020-06-08)

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Scales into winners in stages across multiple opening-range timeframes (1-min add, 5-min add, 60-min add) rather than one entry. (2020-06-02)
- Raises stops mechanically as a trailing moving average "catches up" to price — only moves the stop to the 20-day once the 20-day itself crosses above the current stop, not before. (2020-05-20)
- Repeatedly cuts size on a position that's produced a large unrealized gain rather than holding full size through a possible reversal — explicitly ties this discipline to avoiding the recurring mistake of giving back big profits in past cycles. (2020-05-27 "Millions in profits and how to quickly switch from net long to net short")
- Distinguishes a breakout "retest" from a "failure": most breakouts don't go straight up, so a pullback to the breakout level is normal and not a reason to bail unless a specific level (moving average, base low) actually breaks. (2020-06-04)
- When shorting extended momentum names, deliberately waits for the squeeze to exhaust (day 3+), accepting a few early stop-outs, rather than guessing the top — wants the setup where "you can almost not lose money." (2020-06-04)

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Core rule: sell into strength on the way up instead of trying to sell the exact top, then trail the remainder with a moving average. (2020-05-20, 2020-05-27)
- For trades targeting 8-20% moves, discipline means actually selling most of the position once that target is hit, not holding for more. (2020-05-27)
- No fixed price target on pure momentum/pump names or fresh IPOs with no trading history — any target is "a random number," so he trails a stop instead of pre-setting an exit price. (2020-05-29, 2020-06-02)
- Selling winners too early is a recurring, explicitly named regret (VTIQ, Codex, Fastly), used repeatedly as the lesson to trail rather than fully exit into strength during a strongly trending market. (2020-05-13, 2020-05-14 "The most speculative portfolio ever")

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Treats a rotation into previously-lagging, low-quality names (airlines, cruise lines, pump stocks) outperforming prior leaders as a late-cycle/euphoria warning sign that triggers hedging and raising short exposure. (2020-05-27, 2020-06-04)
- Uses his own emotional state as a signal: feeling euphoric about the market himself is treated as a personal sell/hedge signal ("one foot out the door"). (2020-05-27)
- A day with an unusually large number of simultaneous 5-star long and short setups across many tickers at once is read as a sign of overheated, unusually strong momentum conditions rather than a normal market — title theme of the final video in this batch. (2020-06-08)
- Believes low retail bullish sentiment plus large amounts of sidelined cash fueled the 2020 rally even against terrible macro headlines, and deliberately trades price action rather than news or macro narrative. (2020-05-11, 2020-06-03)

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Nightly/weekly scans built around $60M+ average dollar volume, 2.4%+ average daily range over 20 days, and ranking among the top ~7% strongest stocks over lookbacks from 1 month to 1.5 years — the same scan, inverted, surfaces short candidates among the weakest names. (2020-05-27)
- Trades only stocks with both range and volume; a stock sitting mid-range with no volume is skipped regardless of story, because there's no catalyst or edge. (2020-06-08)
- Prefers real growth companies (30-100%+ revenue growth) over "bloated" slow-growers, citing Domino's dramatically outperforming Papa John's over a decade due to superior revenue growth and gross margin — used as the reason growth-plus-margin beats a cheap multiple. (2020-05-20)
- Keeps a separate, small "pump stock" account for speculative low-quality names so losses there don't touch the main portfolio. (2020-05-14)
- A stock that keeps stopping him out repeatedly (5+ times) on the same side is read as a signal its "personality" doesn't fit his style, not a reason to keep forcing the trade. (2020-05-21, 2020-05-26)

## Psychology & Mindset
- Calls the ability to adapt — not raw hard work or intelligence — the single biggest edge in trading and in business generally. (2020-05-14)
- Roughly 60-70% of his trades in the prior year were losers or breakeven, yet he was highly profitable, because losers are kept small and winners are allowed to run — used to normalize a high loss rate as compatible with strong results. (2020-05-27)
- Pushes back on "be emotionless" trading psychology advice — believes emotion is fine and useful as long as it's channeled into discipline rather than suppressed. (2020-05-27)
- Frames the constant cycle of near-misses and FOMO (chasing weaker names after missing a leader) as universal, and the fix is doing more of what's working and less of what isn't, not forcing new trades. (2020-06-03)
- A gut-check sell rule: once a position becomes so talked-about that people outside trading circles mention it, that's read as a sign the move is getting late. (2020-06-03)

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Chasing "late-cycle" names — buying a hot theme's stragglers well after the early leaders already moved — is called out repeatedly as a costly, recurring mistake even though the early entries in the same theme worked. (2020-05-15 "All the pumps lost mojo")
- Getting overexposed on both sides at once by chasing every setup in a hyperactive market leads to avoidable losses; better to size down and only take the best handful. (2020-05-15)
- Avoids options entirely, calling them too complex to fully understand the risk on. (2020-06-02)
- Explicitly warns smaller/newer traders against shorting extended momentum stocks without experience — this is exactly the type of market where undercapitalized short sellers blow up. (2020-06-04)
- Notes day trading doesn't scale well past roughly $300-500K in account size; below that, day trading compounds faster, and the shift toward swing trading should be gradual (took him over a year), not rushed. (2020-06-02)

## Other Notable Lessons
- Maintains a personal database (in Evernote) of thousands of historical breakout/breakdown examples going back decades, used to train pattern recognition — considers this kind of self-driven study, not following other traders' tips, the only durable way to build an edge. (2020-05-27)
- The volume "tell" for earnings plays: if a stock trades its entire average daily volume within the first several minutes after the open, that confirms the move is real and tradeable. (2020-05-29, 2020-06-02)
- Uses sector ETFs instead of single names when he wants directional sector exposure without single-company risk (e.g., a triple-leveraged financial ETF long, an oil-services ETF short). (2020-05-26, 2020-06-08)
- Distinguishes "trading stocks" from "investing stocks" — large, slow, low-beta names (big ADRs, slow blue chips) are avoided for trading regardless of quality because they lack the range/volume his strategy needs. (2020-05-29, 2020-06-02)
