## Entries & Setups
- EPs (episodic pivots) are his dominant setup; classic breakouts have been mostly unreliable since late 2021 and only work well when the market is genuinely trending out of a correction with broad participation (2023-05-19, "Episodic Pivots, Kenny boy and old times"; 2023-06-01, "AI and the shittier the stock..."; 2023-06-09, "Market mechanics, setups and more"; 2023-12-01, "EP party!"; 2023-12-15, "Speculation money is back!").
- Most good EPs must be entered within the first 3-10 minutes of the move; entering an hour later rarely works (occasional exceptions exist) (2023-06-08, "EPs, AI and quantum computing stocks").
- Textbook EP profile: huge relative volume gapping out of a multi-month base of higher lows, ideally paired with an earnings beat and/or raised guidance; volume matters more than how good the numbers actually are (2023-05-23, "EPs good follow through..."; 2023-06-09).
- A high-volume EP in one name within a theme (e.g., quantum computing, AI) signals the whole group is waking up — worth having a pre-built watchlist ready before that happens (2023-05-23; 2023-06-01; 2023-12-01).
- Never buys a biotech ahead of a scheduled binary data/catalyst readout ("plain Russian roulette") but has no problem buying immediately after good data drops, since the event risk is gone (2023-06-09).
- Avoids buyout-rumor and index-addition stocks entirely — no edge there (2023-06-08).
- Avoids "one-day wonder" micro-cap pumps that superficially look like flags but aren't real setups (2023-06-01, "Zzzlow").

## Chart Reading & Technical Analysis
- Relative strength is a key signal: when the broad market pulls back but a stock holds up much better, funds are effectively "drawing the chart" for you — this is how he found NVDA/AMD in Oct 2021 and flagged PDD's strength in 2023 (2023-05-19; 2023-12-01).
- Prefers stocks with ADR (average daily range) of 4-5%+ and ideally double digits; below ADR 4 the failure rate rises sharply (2023-05-19; 2023-06-08).
- Distinguishes "clean movers" that respect moving averages smoothly (e.g., CVNA) from choppy, mean-reverting stocks that should never be bought on a breakout; also notes NYSE-listed names tend to have worse momentum characteristics than NASDAQ names (2023-06-08; 2023-12-01).
- Trades mainly off the daily chart; only drops to 60-minute or 5-minute charts around the open for EP entries/volume confirmation (2023-05-23; 2023-06-09).
- Simple trend filter: 10-day vs 20-day (E)MA, both sloping up = tradeable, one/both rolling over = caution, 10 crossing below 20 = stand aside; uses a shared TradingView script called "Chillax Moving Averages" for this (2023-06-01; 2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-15).
- Forward-looking estimates matter far more than trailing numbers — stocks can rally hard on weak current numbers if forward guidance improves, and vice versa; price action itself is often predictive of fundamentals still to come (NVDA trended up 6 months before its blowout report) (2023-05-19; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-09).

## Position Sizing & Risk Management
- Vowed never again to hold ~30 positions at once — too unmanageable (2023-05-23).
- Keeps illiquid microcap/quantum-computing type names small and confined to tax-advantaged accounts for their multi-bagger potential ("small account edge"); large, liquid, high-conviction names get much bigger size, since a 10% move in a big position can outearn a 100% move in a tiny one (2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-15).
- Says his single biggest recurring performance drag is overtrading — taking marginal half-size trades in bad conditions "because I can't help myself," which compounds into a real cost over a year (2023-12-15).
- In a confirmed uptrend, scales into margin gradually: start near 0% invested, build to ~20-30%, then add further as new setups confirm and existing positions show a profit cushion (2023-12-01).
- Treats long-side parabolic stocks as far scarier than shorting them (halt risk), so mostly takes parabolic setups only on the short side (2023-05-19).

## Trade Management (adds, trims, stops)
- Standard trailing tools are the 10-day (or 10 EMA) for smaller/more volatile names and the 20-day for larger-cap/institutional names; which works better varies by stock and can't be known in advance (2023-05-19; 2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-15).
- On extreme-momentum movers (up 100-300%+ in a day or two), a daily MA stop is useless since price gives back the whole move before touching it — instead sell into strength or trail with an intraday (60-min) moving average (2023-12-15).
- If an EP runs hard for 3-5 days after entry, it's fine and often correct to start trimming, especially on smaller/more explosive names; larger caps can be held longer before trimming (2023-05-19).
- Berated his girlfriend for panic-selling a position that had not actually violated its stop (10-day/EMA) — stick to the rule rather than react emotionally to a normal dip (2023-06-08).
- A post-EP secondary offering isn't a red flag when the underlying news is genuinely game-changing; funds still want in and drive the stock higher despite dilution (2023-05-19).

## Profit Taking & Exit Strategy
- Standard cadence: trim part of a winning EP after 3-5 days of a sharp move, then trail the remainder with the 10- or 20-day MA (2023-05-19).
- On outsized "silly-season" runners (up 100-500%+ in days), a moving-average trail alone gives back too much — must actively sell into strength; but cautions this reflex can also cost you if applied to a name that just keeps running for months (2023-05-19; 2023-12-01; 2023-12-15).
- A common small-account approach (used by his girlfriend): sell a partial slice into the initial spike, hold/trail the rest (2023-05-19; 2023-05-23).

## Market Timing & Regime Reading
- Considers the best time to go long either right after a bear market ends or in the late stage of a strong bull market; viewed mid-2023 as early-cycle in a new bull run (2023-05-23).
- Draws a hard line between a genuine trending/"runaway" market (breakouts and EPs both work, broad participation) and a range-bound market (only EPs work, breakouts fail) (2023-05-23; 2023-06-09).
- Watches for the Russell/small caps to start leading after a rally driven only by 5-7 mega-caps — a sign of broadening participation and more opportunity ahead; conversely, an index gain concentrated in a handful of mega-caps while breadth lags is a warning sign (2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-01; 2023-12-15).
- Broad up-volume thrust days and many stocks simultaneously "surfing" their rising moving averages without giving back gains mark a genuinely strong bull leg, distinct from a bear-market rally (2023-06-09; 2023-12-15).
- Treats macro headlines (Fed meetings, debt ceiling, war, recession calls) and analyst forecasts as largely irrelevant — almost no one consistently calls turns, so price/volume and a simple market filter are the only "analyst" worth following (2023-05-19; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-05; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-01).
- After a strong multi-week run, expects and accepts a test of the 20-day or even 50-day as healthy rather than alarming (2023-12-01).

## Watchlist & Stock Selection Criteria
- Builds thematic watchlists proactively, before a theme becomes obvious — e.g. made an AI/quantum-computing list in January 2023, months ahead of the run, crediting "it's all about preparation" (2023-05-23; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-05; 2023-12-01).
- When a new theme emerges (e.g. GLP-1 weight-loss drugs), lists every loosely related stock, since a handful of junk names become the 5-10 baggers (2023-12-01).
- Story/theme power beats fundamentals for driving the biggest moves — "the shittier the stock, the bigger the move" is a repeated refrain, since weak, low-quality names are most sensitive to speculative flow in a hot theme (2023-05-23; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-01; 2023-12-15).
- Avoids stocks with a history of choppy, non-trending action regardless of how good the current setup looks — "some stocks are just not meant to be traded" (2023-06-01; 2023-12-01).

## Psychology & Mindset
- Warns against absorbing persistent bearish media narratives — there is always something to worry about; tuning it out and following price/volume is the better use of energy (2023-05-19; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-09).
- Conviction has to be earned by personally studying hundreds of past setups (e.g. "go through 500 setups") rather than copying someone else's rules, or you'll never trust your own exits (2023-06-08).
- Don't chase into a short-term-extended market out of FOMO after missing a rally — wait for the next setup (2023-06-09; 2023-12-01).
- Blanket anti-scam rule: never send money to anyone who approaches you unsolicited, online or in person, no matter how convincing (2023-06-09).

## Common Mistakes / What Not To Do
- Overtrading — taking marginal, half-size trades even in poor conditions — is his single biggest recurring drag on returns (2023-12-15).
- Repeatedly missed large winners by hesitating on clear five-star EPs (semis basket, Ionic Digital, CXAI); reinforces that most EPs must be bought within minutes (2023-06-01; 2023-06-08; 2023-06-09; 2023-12-01).
- Missed a cluster of oil-sector long/short EP opportunities while traveling and not watching screens — being away during a live theme has real opportunity cost (2023-05-19; 2023-12-01).
- Passing on a name purely because it's fundamentally "a piece of junk" is a mistake in a hot theme — some of the biggest winners are the lowest-quality companies (2023-05-23; 2023-06-01; 2023-06-08).
- Buying ahead of a scheduled binary biotech data event is treated as pure gambling, not a trade with edge (2023-06-09).
- Passed on SMCI purely on decelerating fundamentals and then watched it double — a reminder that price/volume/theme strength can override a weak fundamental picture (2023-06-09).
